# Business finance – sources — Summary

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Specification points | 3.1.1–3.1.3 |
| Paper notes | both papers (small and large business contexts) |

## Need for finance

- **Short-term** (within a year): day-to-day costs, cash-flow gaps.
- **Long-term** (over a year): non-current assets, start-up, expansion.
- Match the length of finance to the need; then weigh cost, control, availability, risk.

## Sources

| Source | Type | Key plus | Key minus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal savings | internal | no interest, full control | limited; owner's money at risk |
| Retained profit | internal | no interest, no loss of control | needs past profit; lower dividends |
| Selling assets | internal | no debt | asset lost; only if spare assets |
| Overdraft | external, short | flexible, quick | high interest; can be withdrawn |
| Trade payables | external, short | interest-free credit | late payment damages supplier relationship |
| Loan capital | external, long | large sum, fixed repayments, keep control | interest; security needed |
| Share capital / flotation | external, long | large sums, never repaid | lose control; dividends; plc only for flotation |
| Venture capital | external, long | for risky start-ups; expertise | give up ownership and some control |
| Crowdfunding | external, long | when banks refuse; publicity | fees; may fail; idea copied |

## Traps

- Share capital is not a loan. Overdraft is not for buying machinery. Only a plc floats on a stock market.
