# Gases in the atmosphere — Questions

Specification points 2.9–2.14 · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Name the four most abundant gases in dry air and give their approximate percentages. (4)
2. Describe what you see when magnesium burns in oxygen, and write the equation. (3)
3. Write the equation for the burning of sulfur in oxygen, and state whether the product is acidic
   or basic. (2)
4. Write the equation for the thermal decomposition of copper(II) carbonate and state the colour
   change. (2)
5. Explain why increasing amounts of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere may contribute to climate
   change. (2)

## 2. Practical

6. A student pushes damp iron wool into a measuring cylinder, inverts it in water and records
   95 cm³ of air. A week later the volume of gas is 76 cm³.
   (a) Calculate the percentage of oxygen in the air. (2)
   (b) Explain why the water level rises. (2)
   (c) Explain why the iron wool must be damp. (1)
7. Air is passed back and forth between two gas syringes over heated copper.
   (a) State how the student knows all the oxygen has reacted. (1)
   (b) Explain why the apparatus must cool before the final volume is read. (1)
   (c) State the colour change of the copper. (1)
8. Explain why the delivery tube must be removed from the limewater before heating is stopped. (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Nitrogen 78% (1); oxygen 21% (1); argon 0.9% / about 1% (1); carbon dioxide 0.04% (1).
2. Bright white flame / light (1); white solid / powder (1); 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (1).
3. S + O₂ → SO₂ (1); acidic (1).
4. CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂ (1); green → black (1).
5. Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas / absorbs heat radiated from the Earth (1); more heat trapped
   / Earth's average temperature rises (1).
6. (a) 95 − 76 = 19 cm³ (1); 19 ÷ 95 × 100 = 20% (1).
   (b) Oxygen reacts with / is used up by the iron (1); gas volume decreases so water replaces it
   (1). (c) Iron needs water to rust (1).
7. (a) The volume stops changing (1). (b) Hot gas has a larger volume / gas expands when hot (1).
   (c) Pink-brown / orange → black (1).
8. Otherwise limewater is sucked back into the hot tube (and it could crack) (1).
