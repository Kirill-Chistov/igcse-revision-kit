## 1. The units (7.1)

| Unit | Symbol | Used for in this topic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| becquerel | Bq | **activity** of a source: **1 Bq = 1 nucleus decaying per second** |
| centimetre | cm | range of radiation in air; thickness of an absorber; source-to-detector distance |
| hour, minute, second | h, min, s | half-lives; the time over which counts are taken |

A Geiger–Müller counter gives a **count rate** — counts per second or **counts per minute** — not
the activity in Bq. Only some of the radiation from a source enters the detector, so the count rate
is smaller than the activity, but it halves in the same time and is used in the same half-life
calculations.

## 2. Time conversions

```
1 h = 60 min = 3600 s          1 min = 60 s
```

::: note | Method
**Put every time in the same unit before you count half-lives.**

- A half-life of 15 min and a time of 2 h: 2 h = 120 min, so 120 ÷ 15 = **8 half-lives**.
- 90 counts in 30 s = 3 counts per second = **180 counts per minute**.
:::

::: traps
| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| Writing "bq", "BQ" or "becquerels per second". | **Bq** — it already means "decays per second". |
| Calling a count rate an activity. | A detector measures a **count rate**; the activity (Bq) is the total decay rate of the source. |
:::
