## Answers and marking guidance

1. Industry that provides a service to people or businesses (1).
2. Coal miner primary; software developer quaternary; bricklayer secondary; nurse tertiary; fisher
   primary; car-factory worker secondary (½ each, round down).
3. The percentage of the workforce employed in each sector of industry (1).
4. Tertiary provides a service to people/businesses (1); quaternary is knowledge-based — research,
   development, ICT (1).
5. Primary fell sharply from 60% to 23% (1); secondary rose from 21% to 31% (1); tertiary more than
   doubled from 19% to 46% and is now the largest sector (1).
6. MIC (1): secondary is rising and primary still 23%, too high for an HIC and too low for an LIC (1).
7. Any two, developed: mechanisation of farming replaces labour (1), so fewer workers are needed per
   hectare (1); rural–urban migration (1) to better-paid jobs in city factories and services (1);
   workforce growth in other sectors makes primary a smaller share (1).
8. TNCs/the state invest in factories (1) attracted by cheap, plentiful labour (1); goods are made for
   export / incentives such as SEZs (1).
9. Automation replaces factory workers (1); manufacturing moves to MICs where labour is cheaper (1)
   — global shift, helped by containerisation (1); older industries close as resources run out or
   demand falls (1).
10. Few factory jobs exist in LIC cities (1); tertiary work — street trading, transport, domestic
    work — needs no capital or qualifications (1), so much of it is informal (1).
11. An industry not tied to a raw material or energy source (1), so it can locate almost anywhere
    (1).
12. Standard containers move between ship, train and lorry without unloading (1), so transport is
    fast and cheap (1); factories no longer need to be near their market (1), so they locate at
    container ports in low-wage countries (1).
13. The raw material is heavy/bulky or loses weight in processing (1), so processing at the source
    cuts transport costs (1).
14. Two of: tax breaks lower costs (1+1); grants/subsidies cut set-up costs (1+1); SEZs/EPZs offer
    low taxes and relaxed rules for exporters (1+1); tariffs make firms build inside the market to
    avoid them (1+1).
15. Needs skilled, educated workers (1), found near universities/cities (1); products are knowledge,
    not bulky goods (1), so fast communications, not raw materials, link it to customers (1).
16. Level-marked. Three factors, each explained and applied to the plant, e.g. land — large flat
    site for assembly lines (2); labour — plentiful semi-skilled workers at low cost (2);
    transportation — motorway/port access for components and finished cars (2); markets, political
    policies, technology equally creditable.
