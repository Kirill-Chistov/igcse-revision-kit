# Gas exchange — Questions

**Specification points 2.40B–2.50** · questions marked (P2) are Paper 2 only

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Plants (P2)

1. Explain why a leaf takes in carbon dioxide during the day but gives it out at night.
2. Describe how stomata open and close, and why they close at night.
3. Tubes of hydrogencarbonate indicator with a leaf are left in the dark, in dim light and in bright
   light for an hour. Give the final colour of each and explain.
4. Why is a tube with no leaf set up in this experiment?

## Humans

5. Name the structures air passes through from the trachea to the alveoli.
6. What is the function of the pleural membranes?
7. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how the intercostal muscles and diaphragm cause air to enter the lungs.
8. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how the alveoli are adapted for efficient gas exchange.
9. Explain why a smoker may become breathless during mild exercise. Give two reasons.
10. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how smoking increases the risk of coronary heart disease.
11. Describe how you would show that exhaled air contains more carbon dioxide than inhaled air.
12. Explain why breathing rate stays high for some minutes after exercise.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. In the day photosynthesis is faster than respiration, so there is net uptake of CO₂ (1); at
   night there is no photosynthesis, only respiration, which releases CO₂ (1).
2. In light guard cells take in water / become turgid and curve apart, opening the pore (1); in
   the dark they become flaccid and the pore closes (1); closing reduces water loss when no CO₂ is
   needed for photosynthesis (1).
3. Dark: yellow — respiration only, CO₂ increases (2). Dim: stays red/orange — photosynthesis
   equals respiration, no net change (2). Bright: purple — photosynthesis faster than respiration,
   CO₂ removed (2).
4. Control: shows the colour change is caused by the leaf and not by light/something else.
5. Bronchus, bronchioles, alveoli.
6. They line the lungs and chest wall; the fluid between them reduces friction as the lungs move.
7. External intercostal muscles contract, ribs move up and out (1); diaphragm contracts and flattens
   (1); volume of thorax increases (1); pressure inside falls below atmospheric so air is drawn in
   (1).
8. Large surface area — millions of alveoli (1); walls one cell thick — short diffusion distance
   (1); moist lining — gases dissolve (1); dense capillary network / good blood supply maintains
   the concentration gradient (1); ventilation maintains the gradient (1). Max 4.
9. Any two: carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin, so blood carries less oxygen; emphysema
   reduces the surface area of the alveoli; mucus blocks airways / bronchitis; narrowed coronary
   arteries.
10. Nicotine raises blood pressure / heart rate (1); nicotine and carbon monoxide damage the lining
    of arteries (1); fatty deposits build up and narrow the coronary arteries / clots form, so heart
    muscle gets less oxygen (1).
11. Breathe in through a tube that draws air through limewater and out through a tube that bubbles
    breath through limewater (1); limewater in the exhaled-air tube turns cloudy first / sooner (1).
12. Lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration must be broken down (1), which needs extra oxygen (1).
