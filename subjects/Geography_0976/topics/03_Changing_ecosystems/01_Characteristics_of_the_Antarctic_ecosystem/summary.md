# Characteristics of the Antarctic ecosystem — Summary

Specification points 3.1.1–3.1.4 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1.

## Location (3.1.1)

- Continent centred on the **South Pole**, almost all south of the **Antarctic Circle (66.5° S)**; the
  **Antarctic Peninsula** points north towards South America. Surrounded by the **Southern Ocean**.
- The ecosystem reaches north to the **Antarctic Convergence** (about 50°–60° S), where cold Antarctic
  water meets warmer water.

## Climate (3.1.2)

- **Latitude** 63°–90° S: 24-hour darkness in winter, 24-hour daylight in summer; no solar energy in
  the winter.
- **Angle of the sun** low even in summer → energy spread over a larger area, through more atmosphere;
  ice reflects most of it (**high albedo**).
- **High pressure**: cold, dense air sinks → no clouds; air drains outwards as **katabatic winds**.
- **Temperature**: interior winters below −60 °C; record **−89.2 °C**, Vostok, 1983; coasts about
  0 °C in summer; the high ice sheet adds to the cold.
- **Precipitation**: under **50 mm** a year inland, over 200 mm on coasts, as snow → a **cold desert**.

## Features (3.1.3)

- Ice sheet covers ~**98%** (about 14 million km²), up to 4.8 km thick; ice shelves float at its edge.
- Sea ice grows from ~3 million km² (Feb) to ~18 million km² (Sept).
- Under 1% ice-free; thin or no soil. On land only mosses, lichens and **two native flowering plants**
  (hair grass, pearlwort); no land mammals.
- The sea is rich in summer: few species but huge numbers, short food chains built on **krill**.
- Fragile — cold slows growth and decay. No permanent population, only research stations and tourists.

## Interrelationships and adaptations (3.1.4)

- Sunlight + cold, nutrient- and oxygen-rich water + sea ice → phytoplankton and ice algae → **krill**
  → fish, penguins, seals, whales → leopard seals, orcas.
- More sea ice → more ice algae → more krill. Summer daylight → plankton bloom → whales arrive.
- Cold → almost no soil → only mosses and lichens; penguin droppings fertilise the ground.
- **Emperor penguin**: fat, dense feathers, huddling, heat exchange in feet. **Weddell seal**: blubber,
  keeps breathing holes open. **Krill**: eat under-ice algae, shrink when starved. **Fish**: antifreeze
  proteins. **Hair grass / pearlwort**: low cushions, photosynthesise near 0 °C. **Mosses and lichens**:
  go dormant when frozen or dry.

## Common traps

- Antarctica is a **desert** — the ice built up because snow never melts, not because much falls.
- Ice sheet (on land) ≠ ice shelf (its floating edge) ≠ sea ice (frozen sea water).
- No polar bears in Antarctica.
