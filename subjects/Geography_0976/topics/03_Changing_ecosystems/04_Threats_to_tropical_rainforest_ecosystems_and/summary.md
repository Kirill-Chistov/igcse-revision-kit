# Threats to tropical rainforest ecosystems and how they can be managed — Summary

Specification points 3.4.1–3.4.4 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1.

## Threats (3.4.1)

- **Deforestation** = cutting or burning forest without replanting. Record **6.7 million ha** of tropical
  primary forest lost in 2024, nearly half to fire.
- **Cattle ranching** (largest cause in the Amazon) · **commercial farming** (soya, oil palm) ·
  **subsistence farming and settlements** · **logging** (selective and clear-felling, often illegal) ·
  **resource exploitation** (mining, oil, gas; mercury pollution) · **roads and railways** (open up the
  forest — "fishbone" clearance) · **hydroelectric dams** (flood forest, methane, displacement).
- Driven by national need for income and global demand for beef, soya, palm oil, timber.

## Impacts (3.4.2)

- Environmental: soil erosion and silted rivers · infertile soil within years → more clearance ·
  flooding downstream · less transpiration → less rain, drought, fire · CO₂ released, carbon sink lost
  → global warming · species lost.
- Social: indigenous people lose land and culture; conflict; disease. + jobs, roads, services.
- Economic: long-term resources and future income lost. + short-term income, exports, taxes.
- Benefits short-term and local/national; costs long-term and global.

## Management (3.4.3)

| Strategy / technique | Judgement |
| --- | --- |
| **Protected areas, indigenous territories** | Sustainable if enforced; "paper parks" otherwise |
| **Laws + satellite monitoring + fines** | Brazil cut deforestation ~**80%** 2004–12; rose when enforcement weakened |
| **REDD+, CITES, debt-for-nature swaps** | Make standing forest valuable; payments small |
| **Certification** (FSC, palm oil) | Consumer pressure; small share of trade |
| **Selective logging** | Forest regenerates; still damages trees; roads open access |
| **Replanting** | Slow; repairs rather than prevents |
| **Agroforestry, sustainable extraction, ecotourism** | Most sustainable: forest worth more standing |

**Judgement:** make the standing forest worth more than cleared land to local people, and enforce the
law; demand is global, so consuming countries share responsibility.

## Common traps

- Deforestation is not mainly logging: in the Amazon, cattle ranching is the largest single cause.
- Impacts are not all negative — weigh the short-term economic and social benefits against the costs.
