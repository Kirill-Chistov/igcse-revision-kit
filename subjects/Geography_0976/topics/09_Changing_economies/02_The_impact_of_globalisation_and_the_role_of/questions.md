## Answers and marking guidance

1. The process by which the world's economies, cultures and people become increasingly connected
   (1) and interdependent through flows of goods, money, people and information (1).
2. A company that operates in more than one country (1), with its headquarters usually in its
   country of origin (1).
3. Any three, 1 each: growth in world trade; global supply chains; FDI/flows of money; growth and
   power of TNCs; flows of people; instant flows of information; global culture; time–space
   compression; trade organisations/blocs; uneven connection.
4. The world effectively shrinks (1) because moving goods, people and information takes less time
   and costs less (1).
5. Containerisation/air freight made moving goods cheap and fast (1), so production can be spread
   across countries in supply chains (1); internet/telecommunications allow instant contact (1), so
   a TNC can manage factories and offices worldwide from its HQ (1).
6. Positive, developed: exchange of food, music, ideas (1) widens choice and understanding (1).
   Negative, developed: global brands/media replace local traditions and businesses (1) —
   homogenisation, mostly from HIC culture (1).
7. Access to world markets raises export earnings and jobs (1+1); but many LICs export a few primary
   products (1) whose prices are volatile, so income is unstable/dependent (1).
8. Two of: shipping and aviation emit growing amounts of greenhouse gases (1+1); air and water
   pollution around ports/airports (1+1); wider development of ports/airports damages habitats
   (1+1).
9. The gap between people with and without access to ICT (1); those without are excluded from the
   jobs, trade and information globalisation brings (1), widening inequality (1).
10. HQ and R&D in the home country, usually an HIC (1); regional HQs (1); factories/assembly in MICs
    and LICs (1); outsourced suppliers (1); sells worldwide, profits returned to HQ (1). Max 4.
11. HQ/R&D need highly skilled graduates, universities, finance and access to government (1+1);
    factories need large amounts of cheap labour and land (1) plus tax incentives/SEZs and port
    access (1).
12. Contracting another company to make parts or provide services (1); cheaper / flexible — can
    switch supplier (1).
13. Factory creates jobs (1) and buys from local suppliers, which then employ more (1); workers
    spend wages locally, creating service jobs (1); more tax funds infrastructure, attracting more
    investment (1).
14. Profits are sent to the home country (1); tax breaks reduce government revenue (1); many parts
    are imported, so few local linkages (1); senior jobs go to foreign staff / the TNC may leave (1).
15. Level-marked. Top level: benefits (jobs, FDI, tax, multiplier, skills, infrastructure) and
    problems (profit leakage, low wages, conditions, pollution, footloose) both developed; a clear
    judgement that depends on government regulation, linkages, type of jobs and permanence,
    ideally distinguishing LIC from HIC hosts.
16. Mongolia / Rio Tinto. Level-marked; top level needs both parts with place facts.
    Globalisation (max 4): opened up after the Soviet collapse — democracy 1990, free market 1992,
    WTO 1997 (1); World Bank/ADB loans and Japanese aid (1); FDI almost all into mining, now ≈21% of
    GDP, exports earn foreign exchange (1); LIC → lower-middle-income 2020 (1); benefits unequal,
    weakest in rural areas / traditional values eroded (1).
    TNC (max 4): Rio Tinto runs Oyu Tolgoi copper–gold mine, 66/34 joint venture (1); profit share,
    up to 33% of GDP (1); local supply chain — southern Gobi spending $0.5 m → $888 m (1); heritage
    protection / water-efficient, safe mining (1); dependence on one mine, environmental impact,
    feeling Mongolians gain too little (1).
