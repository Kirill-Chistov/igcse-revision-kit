# The management of urban growth — Summary

Specification points 7.3.1–7.3.2 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Strategy** = plan, policy or agreement. **Technique** = the practical method.
- **Strategies:** city master plans and zoning · national policies that spread growth (rural areas,
  regional towns, new towns) · slum-upgrading programmes (city + residents + NGOs + World Bank) ·
  green belts / urban growth boundaries · SDG 11 and the UN New Urban Agenda (2016).

| Technique | One-line judgement |
| --- | --- |
| Rural development | only one that treats the **cause**; slow |
| New towns / growth poles | work only if jobs move too; very expensive |
| Clearance and relocation | moves the problem; **least sustainable** |
| Site-and-service | cheap; tenure + services; plots may be far from jobs |
| Self-help | low cost, builds skills; slow, quality varies |
| In-situ upgrading | keeps community in place; **most sustainable** housing option |
| Mass transit / BRT | cuts congestion and emissions; BRT far cheaper than metro |
| Traffic restraint | works only alongside good public transport |
| Waste collection + recycling | protects health; cooperatives add income |
| Green belt | protects countryside; raises house prices, development leaps beyond |
| Brownfield + densification | saves countryside; costly clean-up |

- **Four tests for evaluation:** cause or symptom? helps the poorest? affordable to keep going?
  environmental cost?
- **Judgement:** bottom-up, low-cost techniques that keep communities in place are the most sustainable;
  top-down clearance the least. Only rural development and falling birth rates slow growth itself. The
  real limit in LICs is money and governance.

## Definitions

**Sustainable urban management** — improving life for today's residents in ways that are affordable to
keep going, include the poorest, and do not damage the environment for future generations.

**Bus rapid transit (BRT)** — buses running in dedicated lanes with raised stations and payment before
boarding.
