# Economies and diseconomies of scale — Summary

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Average total cost (ATC) | cost of producing each unit: **total costs ÷ output** (learn — not on the formula list) |
| Economies of scale | fall in ATC as a business increases its scale of production |
| Internal economies | ATC falls as the firm's **own output** increases |
| External economies | ATC falls due to **factors outside the firm** (industry growing in the area) |
| Diseconomies of scale | rise in ATC when a business grows beyond a certain size |

## Essential facts

- Internal: **purchasing** (bulk discounts), **technical** (specialist machinery), **financial** (cheaper loans), **managerial** (specialist managers), **marketing** (advertising spread over more units).
- External: skilled labour pool, specialist suppliers nearby, better infrastructure.
- Diseconomies: poor **communication**, **coordination/control** problems, low **motivation**.
- **Limit of growth** = optimum size (lowest ATC); growing past it raises ATC.
- Growth: lower ATC → lower prices or higher margin; but finance needed, risk of diseconomies, less flexible.

## Common traps

- Lower **average** cost, not lower total cost.
- Diseconomies = ATC rising, not a loss.
