## Answers and marking guidance

**AI-written for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.** One mark per creditable
point unless stated.

1. Housing built by residents on land they do not legally own or rent (1), without planning permission
   / usually without basic services such as piped water or sanitation (1).
2. Work that is not officially registered or taxed / no contract, sick pay or pension (1); e.g. street
   selling, waste picking, shoe shining (1).
3. The unplanned, often low-density (1), outward spread of a built-up area into the surrounding
   countryside (1).
4. The zone at the edge of a city where urban and rural land uses meet and mix (1).
5. Any four: new housing estates; retail parks; business / science parks; warehouses; landfill; sewage
   works; airports; country parks / golf courses; commuter villages; farmland. (1 each)
6. Many people live close together (1), so there are enough customers to make services such as
   hospitals and universities worth running (1).
7. Any three described: jobs and higher wages; hospitals, schools, universities; wider choice of housing
   with water, sanitation, electricity; culture — museums, theatres, festivals; leisure — stadiums,
   cinemas, parks; consumption — shopping centres, more choice, lower prices. (1 each)
8. Opportunity: new jobs in factories, construction and services / the informal sector gives newcomers
   an income (1) + development (1). Challenge: too few formal jobs (1), so many are unemployed or in
   insecure, low-paid informal work with no rights (1).
9. People arrive faster than the city can plan and pay for new infrastructure (1); water mains, sewers
   and power grids take years and large sums to extend (1); residents of unplanned settlements are not
   officially recognised / cannot pay, so they are not connected (1).
10. Waste is dumped in rivers or open ground / burnt (1); it blocks drains, so water collects and floods
    (1); contaminated water spreads diseases such as cholera; burning causes air pollution (1).
11. It is the only land left that nobody else wants (1) — steep slopes, floodplains or land beside dumps
    — and it is free or cheap and often close to work (1).
12. Any three from the photo: houses built by residents, of unrendered brick; very dense, packed
    together; no planned street pattern / regular layout; built on a steep hillside; water tanks on
    roofs, showing an unreliable supply. (1 each)
13. Wealthy residents can afford good housing, private services and security (1), while newcomers end
    up in unplanned settlements with poor services (1); the gap in income and access to services widens
    and becomes visible, e.g. gated communities beside slums (1).
14. Land at the edge is cheaper (1); firms need large sites (1); roads and car ownership make
    commuting possible (1); in LICs, migrants build on unclaimed land at the edge (1). (Max 2)
15. Any two, each explained: loss of farmland and habitats (1) → loss of biodiversity (1); concrete and
    tarmac increase surface runoff (1) → higher flood risk (1); more car journeys (1) → air and noise
    pollution (1).
16. Commuters buy homes in the village but work in the city (1); house prices rise beyond local young
    people (1); commuters shop and use services in the city (1), so village shops, buses and schools
    close / the village loses its community identity (1).
17. Levels (6 marks). Challenges: inequality, overloaded services, housing shortage, insufficient formal
    jobs, congestion, waste, unplanned settlements on hazardous land. Opportunities: jobs and income,
    services cheaper per person, informal sector, community networks, recycling income, construction.
    **L3** needs a supported judgement — e.g. the challenges dominate in the short term when growth
    outpaces the city's budget, but the opportunities are why people keep coming.
18. Levels (6 marks). Negative: farmland and habitat loss, flood risk, pollution, farmers' losses,
    city-centre shops lose trade, commuter villages lose services and become unaffordable. Positive:
    jobs in retail and business parks, landowners' income, tax revenue, housing choice, new services,
    country parks. **L3** needs a judgement, e.g. economic gains go to the city and landowners, while
    environmental costs are permanent.
