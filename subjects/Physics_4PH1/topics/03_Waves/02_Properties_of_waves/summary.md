## Essential facts

- Waves transfer **energy and information, not matter**; particles oscillate about fixed positions.
- **Transverse:** oscillations **perpendicular** to energy transfer — EM waves, water ripples, string.
- **Longitudinal:** oscillations **parallel** to energy transfer; compressions and rarefactions — sound.
- **Doppler effect:** a moving source emits each wavefront from a new position → wavefronts
  bunched in front (shorter λ, higher *f*), spread behind (longer λ, lower *f*). Wave speed unchanged.
- **All waves reflect and refract.** Refraction = speed change in a new medium; *f* constant, so λ
  changes; at an angle the wavefront turns — slower → towards normal, faster → away. Along the
  normal → no bend.

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Amplitude | maximum displacement from the rest position |
| Wavelength λ | distance from a point on one wave to the same point on the next (crest to crest) |
| Frequency *f* | number of waves passing a point per second |
| Period *T* | time for one complete wave to pass a point |
| Wavefront | line joining points at the same stage of oscillation (e.g. a crest) |

## Equations

- *v* = *f* λ — **recall**. *f* = 1/*T* — given.
- Sound in air ≈ 330–340 m/s; EM waves in a vacuum 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

## Common traps

- Amplitude is rest → crest, not trough → crest.
- Doppler effect: the **speed** does not change; the observed λ and *f* do.
- Refraction: the **frequency** does not change; the speed and λ do.
