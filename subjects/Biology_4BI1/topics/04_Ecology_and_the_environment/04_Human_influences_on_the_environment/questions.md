# Human influences on the environment — Questions

Specification points 4.12–4.17, 4.18B; questions marked **[P2]** are Paper 2 only. Marking guidance
is this repository's own, not an official Pearson mark scheme. No past papers are stored for Biology
yet.

## 1. Recall

1. Name three greenhouse gases and a human activity that releases each. (3)
2. State how carbon monoxide is produced. (1)
3. Define *eutrophication*. (2)

## 2. Understanding

4. Explain how burning coal can lead to the death of fish in a lake. (3)
5. Explain why carbon monoxide can be fatal. (3)
6. Explain how an increase in greenhouse gases leads to global warming. (3)
7. Describe two possible biological consequences of global warming. (2)

## 3. Application

8. After heavy rain, fertiliser is washed from a field into a lake. A few weeks later many fish die.
   Explain why. (5)
9. Untreated sewage enters a river. Give three ways it harms the river's organisms or people. (3)
10. **[P2]** Explain how clearing a rainforest can reduce rainfall in the area and increase
    carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. (4)
11. **[P2]** Explain why soil becomes infertile after deforestation. (3)

## Marking guidance

1. Any three with a correct source (1 each): CO₂ — burning fossil fuels / deforestation; methane —
   cattle / rice fields / landfill; nitrous oxide — fertilisers / vehicle exhausts; CFCs — aerosols /
   refrigerators.
2. Incomplete combustion of fuels, e.g. car engines / faulty boilers / cigarette smoke (1).
3. Enrichment of water with mineral ions / nitrates and phosphates (1) causing rapid growth of
   algae (and then a fall in oxygen) (1).
4. Coal contains sulfur; burning releases sulfur dioxide (1); SO₂ dissolves in rain water to form
   acid rain (1); the lake becomes acidic and fish / their eggs die (1).
5. CO combines with haemoglobin (1) more readily than oxygen / irreversibly (1); less oxygen is
   carried to cells for respiration (1).
6. Greenhouse gases absorb heat (infrared) radiated from the Earth (1) and re-radiate it back to
   Earth (1); more gases → more heat retained → enhanced greenhouse effect → mean temperature rises (1).
7. Any two: sea levels rise → flooding / habitat loss; climate change / droughts / extreme weather;
   species migrate or become extinct; loss of biodiversity; changes in crop yields; spread of pests
   or disease vectors (2).
8. Nitrates / phosphates leached into the lake (1); algae grow rapidly — algal bloom (1); algae block
   light so plants below cannot photosynthesise and die (1); bacteria / decomposers feed on dead
   plants and respire aerobically (1); dissolved oxygen falls so fish die (1).
9. Mineral ions cause eutrophication (1); organic matter is decomposed by bacteria that use up
   dissolved oxygen, killing fish (1); pathogens spread diseases such as cholera (1).
10. Fewer trees so less transpiration (1) → less water vapour in the air → less rainfall (1); less
    photosynthesis so less CO₂ removed (1); burning / decay of trees releases CO₂ (1).
11. Fewer roots to absorb mineral ions (1); rain leaches the minerals out of the soil (1); no roots
    to hold the soil / no canopy, so topsoil is eroded (1).
