# Changing employment structures — Summary

Specification points 9.1.1–9.1.3 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Primary** extracts (farming, fishing, forestry, mining). **Secondary** makes (manufacturing,
  construction). **Tertiary** serves (retail, health, transport, tourism). **Quaternary** knows
  (research, ICT, software).
- **LIC:** mostly primary, informal tertiary growing. **MIC:** primary falling fast, secondary at its
  peak. **HIC:** tertiary dominant, secondary falling (deindustrialisation), quaternary growing.
- **Why primary falls:** mechanisation of farming + rural–urban migration.
- **Why secondary rises then falls:** TNC and state investment in factories (MICs) → automation and
  the shift of factories to lower-wage countries (HICs).
- **Why tertiary rises:** higher incomes and ageing populations buy more services.
- **Footloose** = not tied to raw materials or energy; chooses by labour, communications, incentives.
- **Containerisation** made long-distance transport cheap → factories moved to low-wage countries
  at container ports.

## Definitions

**Employment structure** — the percentage of a country's workforce employed in each sector.

**Deindustrialisation** — the decline of manufacturing in a country or region, shown by falling
secondary-sector employment and output.

**Footloose industry** — an industry not tied to a raw material or energy source, so it can locate
almost anywhere.

## Location factors — the ten named

**Tied (weight/bulk/power):** land · raw materials · energy.
**People:** labour (cheap *or* skilled) · markets.
**Movement:** transportation · containerisation · communications.
**Choice-shapers:** political policies (tax breaks, SEZs, EPZs, tariffs, planning) · technology
(automation, science parks).

## Common traps

- Percentages are shares — a falling % is not always falling numbers.
- Mining = primary; construction = secondary; research = quaternary.
