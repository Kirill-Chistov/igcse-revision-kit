## Energy transfers (4.18P)

- All except solar cells: something turns a **turbine** → turns a **generator** → electrically to
  the grid.
- Thermal stations (fossil, nuclear, geothermal, solar heating): heat boils water → steam → turbine;
  condenser wastes energy heating the surroundings.
- Wind: kinetic store of air. Hydroelectric: gravitational store of high water. Tides/waves:
  moving sea water. Geothermal: thermal store of hot rocks. Fossil: chemical store (burned).
  Nuclear: nuclear store (fission). Solar heating: mirrors focus sunlight to make steam.
- Solar cells: light → electrically, directly; no turbine.

## Advantages and disadvantages (4.19P)

- **Renewable** (won't run out): wind, water, geothermal, solar. **Non-renewable**: fossil fuels,
  nuclear fuel.
- Fossil: reliable, on demand / CO₂ (climate change), SO₂ (acid rain), will run out.
- Nuclear: no CO₂, reliable, huge output / radioactive waste, accident risk, costly, slow start.
- Wind, waves, solar: free, no CO₂ running / unreliable (weather, night), large areas, low output.
- Hydroelectric: reliable, fast response / floods valleys, costly.
- Tidal: predictable / few sites, habitats and shipping affected.
- Geothermal: reliable / volcanic areas only, drilling costs.

## Common traps

- Nuclear gives no CO₂ while running — its problem is radioactive waste.
- Solar cells use no turbine.
- Renewable ≠ no environmental impact.
