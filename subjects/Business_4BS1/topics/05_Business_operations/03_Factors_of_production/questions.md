## 1. Recall

1. **(1)** Define the term *enterprise*.
2. **(1)** State **one** example of capital used by a restaurant.
3. **(1)** State **one** difference between a labour-intensive and a capital-intensive activity.

## 2. Explain (3 marks each)

4. Explain **one** reason why many businesses have replaced labour with capital.
5. Explain **one** advantage for a hairdresser of being labour-intensive.

## 3. Exam-style

6. **(6)** A bottled-water company is moving from a labour-intensive to a capital-intensive production process. Analyse the impact on the company.
7. **(9)** A furniture maker sells hand-made tables. It is considering two options for growth. Option 1: stay labour-intensive and hire more craftspeople. Option 2: become capital-intensive with automated cutting machines. Justify which **one** of these two options it should choose.

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. The skill and risk-taking of the entrepreneur who organises land, labour and capital to produce goods or services.
2. Any one: ovens, kitchen equipment, tables and chairs, the building, a delivery van.
3. Any one: labour-intensive uses mainly people, capital-intensive mainly machinery; main cost wages vs machinery; varied/customised output vs large volumes of standardised products.
4. Identify (1) e.g. rising wages / cheaper, better technology; develop twice (2): machines become cheaper than workers → lower cost per unit → lower prices or higher profit.
5. Identify (1) e.g. flexibility / personal service; develop twice (2): each customer wants a different style → stylist adapts to each request → higher satisfaction, repeat custom.
6. Two developed impacts in context (3 + 3), e.g. high productivity → lower cost per bottle → competitive prices; high initial cost of the bottling line → finance/interest; redundancies → morale and image.
7. For / against / judgement in context (`EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` § Justify). Option 1 keeps the hand-made quality that justifies a premium price and flexibility for custom orders but unit costs stay high; Option 2 raises productivity and cuts unit cost but needs finance and may lose the hand-made appeal.
