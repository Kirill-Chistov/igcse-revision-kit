# Change of state — Summary

Specification points 5.8P–5.14P · Topic 5 · **all Paper 2 only**.

## Particles

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrangement, spacing | regular, touching | irregular, close | random, far apart |
| Motion | vibrate about fixed positions | slide past each other | fast, random, all directions |

## Heating

- Heating → energy stored by the particles increases.
- No change of state → particles' **kinetic energy** rises → **temperature rises**.
- Change of state → energy **breaks/weakens bonds** between particles → **temperature constant**.
- Temperature–time graph (heat crushed ice, read every 30 s, stir): flat at **0 °C** (melting) and
  **100 °C** (boiling).

## Changes of state

- **Melting:** particles overcome some forces, leave fixed positions, stay close.
- **Boiling:** at boiling point, throughout the liquid, bubbles; particles overcome forces completely.
- **Evaporation:** any temperature, surface only, fastest particles escape → liquid cools.

## Specific heat capacity

- Energy to change the temperature of an object by 1 °C per kg of mass; J/kg °C.
- **Δ*Q* = *m* × *c* × Δ*T*** (given in the exam). Water 4200 J/kg °C.
- Practical: insulated block + heater + joulemeter (or *E* = *I* × *V* × *t*) + thermometer;
  *c* = *E* ÷ (*m* × Δ*T*); use the highest temperature. Energy lost → *c* comes out **too high**.

## Common traps

- Temperature does not rise during melting or boiling.
- Δ*T* is a change; mass in kg.
