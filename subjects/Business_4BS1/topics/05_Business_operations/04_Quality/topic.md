## 1. What quality means (5.4.1)

**Definition — Quality:** how well a good or service meets customers' needs and expectations — fit for purpose, reliable and free from faults, every time.

Fit for purpose, reliable, free from defects, safe.
:::
::: card | Quality in services
On time, helpful staff, clean surroundings.
:::

## 2. Quality control and total quality management (5.4.1)

**Definition — Quality control:** checking (inspecting) products at the end of production, or at set stages, to find and remove those that do not meet the required standard. In services: e.g. checking hotel rooms, customer feedback surveys.

**Definition — Total quality management (TQM):** an approach in which every employee is responsible for quality at every stage of production, aiming to get things right first time with zero defects.

![Top lane, quality control: products pass through stages 1 to 3, then inspectors check the finished products; those that pass go to customers, rejects are scrapped or reworked. Bottom lane, TQM: every worker checks their own work at each of stages 1 to 3, so products are right first time with zero defects](assets/fig_qc_vs_tqm.svg)

*Figure 1. Quality control finds faults at the end; TQM prevents them at every stage.*

:::: grid
::: advantages | Quality control — advantages
- Faulty products are caught before they reach customers → protects reputation, fewer returns.
- Simple to organise; production workers can concentrate on output.
:::
::: disadvantages | Quality control — disadvantages
- Faults are found only after they are made → wasted materials and time (scrap or rework).
- Cost of employing inspectors; only a sample may be checked, so some faults slip through.
- Workers may leave quality to the inspectors.
:::
::::

:::: grid
::: advantages | TQM — advantages
- Faults are prevented, not just found → less waste, lower costs.
- Quality is built in → consistently high quality and a strong reputation.
- Staff have responsibility → higher motivation.
:::
::: disadvantages | TQM — disadvantages
- Expensive and slow to introduce — every employee must be trained.
- Needs commitment from every worker; hard to change attitudes.
- Benefits take time to appear.
:::
::::

## 3. Quality and competitive advantage (5.4.1)

**Definition — Competitive advantage:** something that makes a business's product more attractive to customers than its rivals' products, e.g. better quality or a lower price.

![High quality leads to three benefits: a good reputation and customer loyalty bringing repeat purchases and recommendations; customers paying more so the business can charge a premium price for a higher profit margin; and fewer faults, returns and complaints so costs are lower. All three give a competitive advantage over rivals. Poor quality leads to returns, refunds, recalls and bad reviews, losing sales and reputation](assets/fig_quality_advantage.svg)

*Figure 2. Why quality matters: three routes to a competitive advantage — and the cost of poor quality.*

## Common traps

- High quality ≠ expensive: a cheap product is good quality if it meets customers' expectations for its price.
- Quality control **inspects** faults out at the end; TQM **builds** quality in at every stage.
