## 1. Short answer

1. **(2)** Define *food security*.
2. **(2)** Identify **one** natural and **one** human factor that reduces food supply.
3. **(3)** State the **three** types of food aid.
4. **(2)** Define *desertification*.

## 2. Explain

5. **(4)** Explain how food insecurity can trap people in a cycle of poverty.
6. **(4)** Compare the problems caused by food insecurity in an LIC and an HIC.
7. **(4)** Explain **two** techniques used to increase food supply on small farms.
8. **(4)** Using Figure 6 (photographs), explain how technique **A** and technique **C** reduce soil erosion.

## 3. Extended

9. **(6)** Evaluate the role of food aid in improving food security.
10. **(9)** Evaluate the strategies and techniques used to manage desertification and soil erosion. Include how sustainable they are.
11. **(9)** For a named country or area you have studied, describe the causes of food insecurity and the problems it causes, and explain the strategies used to increase food supply.

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. When all people at all times have access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food (1) to maintain a healthy and active life (1).
2. Natural: drought / floods / pests / poor soil (1). Human: conflict / poverty / poor storage / overgrazing (1).
3. Relief (emergency), programme, project.
4. Degradation of land in arid/semi-arid areas until it is desert-like (1), caused by drought/climate and human activity such as overgrazing (1).
5. Lack of food → malnutrition (1) → illness, stunting, weakness (1) → less able to work or learn, lower productivity (1) → lower income, cannot buy food or inputs, so food insecurity continues (1).
6. LIC: undernutrition, stunting, famine, dependence on aid (1–2). HIC: food poverty, food banks, obesity from cheap processed food, actual shortage rare because food can be imported (1–2). Credit a comparative statement.
7. Two techniques each with how it raises supply, e.g. drip irrigation — water to roots, crops in dry season; airtight storage — fewer losses to pests; improved seed — higher yields, drought-resistant.
8. A terraces: flat steps hold water and soil so run-off cannot wash soil downslope (2). C stone half-moons/lines: slow run-off, trap soil, water soaks in (2).
9. Level marking. *L1 (1–2):* simple benefits or problems. *L2 (3–4):* strengths and weaknesses explained, types distinguished. *L3 (5–6):* balanced, with a judgement — vital short-term relief but long-term problems (dependency, undercutting farmers), works best bought locally and linked to development.
10. Level marking. *L1 (1–3):* describes techniques. *L2 (4–6):* explains how several techniques work, with strengths and limitations. *L3 (7–9):* evaluates across techniques, judges sustainability (cost, local materials, community ownership, tackles cause vs symptom) and reaches a supported conclusion.
11. Level marking (Nigeria): *L1 (1–3):* general points, no place detail. *L2 (4–6):* causes, problems and strategies with some Nigerian detail. *L3 (7–9):* all three parts with accurate figures — conflict in the NE (>2 m displaced), inflation (food ≈50% of spending), 2022 floods (>675,000 ha), desertification (15 of 36 states); malnutrition and stunting, stabilisation centres; storage/processing, microfinance, training, WFP programmes.
