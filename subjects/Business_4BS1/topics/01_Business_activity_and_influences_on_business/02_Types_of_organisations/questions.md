## Practice

1. **[1]** Define the term *limited liability*.
2. **[1]** Define the term *stakeholder*.
3. **[3]** Explain one disadvantage of being a sole trader.
4. **[3]** Explain one advantage to a franchisor of expanding through franchises.
5. **[3]** Explain one reason for keeping a country's water supply under public ownership.
6. **[6]** Two friends run a successful bicycle shop as a partnership and want to open two more shops. Analyse the benefits to them of becoming a private limited company.
7. **[9]** Maria wants to open a coffee shop. Option 1: buy a franchise of a national coffee chain. Option 2: open her own independent coffee shop as a sole trader. Justify which one of these two options Maria should choose.

## Marking guidance (not an official mark scheme)

1. The owners can lose only the money they invested in the business; their personal assets are safe.
2. Any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, the activities of a business.
3. Unlimited liability (1) → if the business fails, the owner must pay its debts (1) → personal assets such as a house could be taken (1). Or: limited finance → hard to expand.
4. Franchisees pay to open outlets (1) → the franchisor expands without raising much finance itself (1) → lower risk, plus income from fees and royalties (1).
5. Water is essential (1) → the government can supply it even where it is unprofitable (1) → and stop a private monopoly charging high prices (1).
6. In context, e.g. limited liability → personal assets protected while borrowing to expand → less risk; can sell shares to family/friends → more finance for the new shops while keeping control.
7. For (e.g. franchise): trusted brand → customers from day one → lower risk of failure, training and support. Against: fee and royalties cut profit; must follow rules → less independence. Judgement specific to Maria, e.g. her experience, capital and the competition locally.
