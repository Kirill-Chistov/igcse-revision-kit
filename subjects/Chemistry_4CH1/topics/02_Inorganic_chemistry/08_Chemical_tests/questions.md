# Chemical tests — Questions

Specification points 2.44–2.50 · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Describe the tests for (a) oxygen; (b) ammonia; (c) chlorine. (6)
2. Give the flame colour of (a) K⁺; (b) Ca²⁺; (c) Li⁺. (3)
3. Describe how to carry out a flame test. (3)
4. Describe a test to show that a liquid contains water, and a test to show that it is pure water.
   (4)

## 2. Identifying ions

5. Sodium hydroxide solution is added to solutions of three compounds. Name the cation in each:
   (a) green precipitate; (b) blue precipitate; (c) orange-brown precipitate. (3)
6. Describe how to show a solid contains ammonium ions. (3)
7. Describe a test for bromide ions, giving the result. (3)
8. Explain why dilute hydrochloric acid is not used to acidify the solution in the halide test. (1)
9. A white solid gives a yellow flame, fizzes with dilute hydrochloric acid and the gas turns
   limewater milky. Identify the solid. (2)
10. Write the ionic equation for the test for sulfate ions. (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. (a) Glowing splint (1) relights (1). (b) Damp red litmus paper (1) turns blue (1). (c) Damp blue
   litmus paper (1) turns red then bleached / white (1).
2. (a) lilac (1); (b) orange-red (1); (c) red (1).
3. Clean nichrome / platinum wire with concentrated HCl in a flame (1); dip in acid then the solid
   (1); hold in a (blue) Bunsen flame and observe colour (1).
4. Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (1) turns white to blue (1); boils at 100 °C (1) / melts at 0 °C
   exactly (1).
5. (a) Fe²⁺ (1); (b) Cu²⁺ (1); (c) Fe³⁺ (1).
6. Add sodium hydroxide solution and warm (1); test gas with damp red litmus paper (1); turns blue /
   ammonia (1).
7. Add dilute nitric acid (1) then silver nitrate solution (1); cream precipitate (1).
8. It contains chloride ions, which would give a white precipitate (1).
9. Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃) — sodium (1), carbonate (1).
10. Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) (1).
