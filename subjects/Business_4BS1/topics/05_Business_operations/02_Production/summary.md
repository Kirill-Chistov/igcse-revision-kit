## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Job production | one unique product made to an individual customer's order |
| Batch production | a group of identical products made together, then equipment changed for the next batch |
| Flow production | continuous production of large quantities of identical products on a production line |
| Productivity | output per worker (per unit of input) in a given period |
| Lean production | methods that use as few resources as possible by cutting waste |
| JIT | stock control: materials arrive exactly when needed, so little or no stock is held |
| Kaizen | continuous improvement — all staff make small, regular improvements |
| Robotics | programmable machines doing production tasks people used to do |

## Formula (learn — not on the formula list)

- **Labour productivity = total output ÷ number of workers** (per period). % change = change ÷ original × 100.

## Essential facts

- Job: exact needs, premium price, motivated staff / highest unit cost, slow.
- Batch: some variety, lower unit cost / down time between batches, storage costs.
- Flow: lowest unit cost, high consistent output / high set-up cost, inflexible, boring, breakdown stops line.
- Productivity ↑ → labour cost per unit ↓ → lower prices **or** higher margin; fewer workers needed.
- JIT: low storage cost, less waste / relies on suppliers, no buffer, no bulk discounts.
- Kaizen: small gains add up, motivation / slow, can't fix big problems, needs commitment.
- Using resources effectively → lower costs, higher productivity, better environmental image.
- Robotics: productivity and consistent quality ↑ / high cost, less flexible, job losses.
- Balance: more technology suits high volume, high wages, available finance; less suits customised products, low wages, limited finance.

## Common traps

- Productivity is per worker, not total output.
- JIT = timing of stock; Kaizen = small continuous change.
