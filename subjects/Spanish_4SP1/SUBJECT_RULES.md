# Spanish 4SP1 — Subject Rules

**Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (9–1) Spanish (4SP1)**, specification Issue 2 (July 2019;
first teaching 2017, first exams June 2019). Not UK GCSE Spanish (1SP1 / 1SP0), not the old International GCSE (4SP0),
not Cambridge IGCSE (0530). **No tiers** — one set of papers, grades 9–1.

## Three papers — the order differs from Portuguese 1PG0

| Paper | Code | Skill | Time | Marks | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4SP1/01 | Listening | 30 min + 5 min reading | 40 | 25% |
| 2 | 4SP1/02 | Reading (Section A, 40) and Writing (Section B, 40) | 1 h 45 min | 80 | 50% |
| 3 | 4SP1/03 | Speaking (teacher-conducted, marked by Pearson) | 8–10 min | 40 | 25% |

Name papers by skill when talking to the student (in Portuguese 1PG0 there are four papers).

## Facts that change what is written

- **Rubrics, questions and answers are in Spanish** on Papers 1 and 2 (answers are
  non-verbal or short Spanish words/phrases). Spelling and grammar are not marked in listening or
  reading as long as the answer is comprehensible — but a Portuguese spelling can make an answer
  incomprehensible or a different word.
- **Listening:** 7 tasks, each recording heard **twice**; short statements building to longer
  conversations; multiple choice, matching, note-taking, table completion, gap-fill.
- **Reading:** 5 tasks, one a literary extract (adapted story/novel/play); multiple choice,
  matching, note-taking, table completion, gap-fill, short answers in Spanish.
- **Writing (Section B):**
  - Q6: 60–75 words using **four prescribed words/short phrases** — 5 communication + 5 language.
  - Q7: choice of 3 tasks (different topics), 130–150 words, **four bullet points**, all must be
    covered — 10 communication + 10 language. Top band needs "secure use of at least three
    tenses" and "some complex lexical items".
  - Q8: grammar task, 10 marks — change the word in brackets so it fits the sentence (verbs,
    adjectives; sometimes no change is needed).
- **Speaking:** three tasks in one continuous recording; three **different** topic areas.
  - Task A, picture-based discussion, 2–3 min (8 communication + 4 language). The student
    chooses their own picture beforehand: at least two people, objects in the background,
    interactions, no text, from any sub-topic except A3, C3, C5, D2, E4. Five questions in fixed
    order: description → specific factual detail → past or future hypothesis → opinion →
    evaluation (wider topic). Only prompts allowed: *¿Por qué (no)? ¿Algo más? ¿Es todo?*
  - Tasks B and C, conversations on two further topic areas chosen at random by Pearson's
    randomisation grid, 3–3.5 min each (12 communication + 8 interaction/spontaneity + 8 language).
    Examiners must elicit past, present and future, opinions and justifications; rehearsed
    speeches are discouraged.
  - No notes in the exam. Pronunciation and intonation are marked in every task.
- **Topics:** five topic areas, five sub-topics each (Pearson codes A1–E5). A3 Services,
  C3 Role models, C5 Childhood, D2 Weather and climate, E4 Accidents/ailments/health are
  **not assessed in Speaking** but are assessed in Papers 1 and 2.
- **Grammar list** (spec pp. 34–35): structures marked **(R)** — passive voice, imperfect
  subjunctive — are for recognition only.
- **Vocabulary:** Appendix 1 is a *minimum core* list organised by sub-topic, plus
  non-topic-specific vocabulary. Tasks aimed at grades 1–5 use it; higher-grade tasks may include
  unfamiliar words. It is not a closed list.

## Past papers

Past papers are not shipped with this repository. The student downloads them from Pearson and keeps
them in `me/papers/Spanish_4SP1/` (not in Git). A layout that works:

- `1 Listening/` and `2 Reading and Writing/`: one folder per test — live 4SP1 papers June 2019 –
  June 2025 (most years two different papers: `2025 June` and `2025 June (version 2)`, Pearson's
  01/01R and 02/02R; "2020 November" = the June 2020 papers sat in November) and `Sample paper
  2017` / `Sample paper 2018`. Each holds `<test> - Questions.pdf`, `<test> - Mark scheme.pdf`,
  and for listening `<test> - Audio.mp3` (Sample 2018 has a transcript instead of audio).
  `2 Reading and Writing/` also has two marked writing-example PDFs.
- `3 Speaking/`: the 2017 sample speaking test (pictures, examiner questions, mark scheme) and
  8 recordings of marked speaking tests with examiner commentary.
- The most recent series is usually teacher-only on Pearson's site for a while.

## If the student speaks Portuguese

Applies only when `me/profile.md` lists Portuguese as a home or studied language (say which variety).
It is then the single biggest factor in how Spanish material is written:

- **Use Portuguese as the bridge, briefly.** Where Spanish works like Portuguese, say so in one
  line and move on — do not teach from zero what the student already has (gender, most tenses, ser/estar,
  reflexives, subjunctive concept). This is category B bridging: short, and only when it saves
  time.
- **Spend the space on the differences**, because that is where the marks are lost
  ("portuñol"). Main interference areas, to be taught in the relevant topic:
  - *Sounds (marked in Speaking):* final -e/-o not reduced to [i]/[u]; no nasal vowels;
    *ti/di* not [tʃi]/[dʒi]; initial *r*/*rr* trilled, not [h]; final *-l* clear, not [w];
    *j/ge/gi* = [x], not [ʒ]; *ll* ≠ *lh*, *ñ* ≠ *nh*; *v* = [b]; *s* never [z] or [ʃ];
    *z/ce/ci* never [z].
  - *Spelling (marked in Writing and Q8):* -ción/-ciones (not -ção/-ções), -dad (not -dade),
    -mente, no *ç*, no *ã/õ/â/ê/ô*, only acute accents, ¿ ¡; *ll/ñ* not *lh/nh*; *j* where PT
    has *j/g* in different words (*viaje*, *hijo*).
  - *Grammar:* *hay* not *tiene* for "there is"; *me gusta el…* (no *de*, thing is the subject);
    only two contractions (*al, del* — never *no/na/pelo/num*); personal *a*; *muy* vs *mucho*;
    *ir **a** + infinitive*; perfect = *haber* (PT *tenho comido* means something different);
    pluperfect *había*; object pronoun *lo/la* (never *vi él*); no personal infinitive; no future
    subjunctive (*cuando vengas*); apocope (*buen, gran, primer, algún*); plurals *-iones*,
    *-les*; gender switches (*el viaje, la leche, la sal, el color, el árbol, la nariz, el
    dolor, el puente*); diphthonging verbs (*puedo, quiero, duermo*) where PT has none.
  - *Vocabulary:* false friends (e.g. *exquisito, embarazada, largo, oficina, polvo, rato,
    apellido, cena, pronto, todavía, luego, sitio, propina, taller*) — check each against
    Appendix 1 before teaching it as examinable.
- **Never present a Portuguese form as Spanish**, and never let a Portuguese gloss replace the
  English meaning given in Appendix 1.
- Compare with the student's own variety (Brazilian or European) only.
- Spanish pronunciation: both Peninsular [θ] and Latin-American seseo [s] for *z/ce/ci* are
  acceptable; recommend one and be consistent.

## Identifiers used here

`A1`–`E5` are Pearson's own sub-topic codes (safe to use with teachers). `G1`–`G12` grammar,
`P1`/`P2R`/`P2W`/`P3` papers and skills, `V0` non-topic vocabulary are repository-local.

## Watch out for

Material for UK GCSE Spanish (1SP1/1SP0 — different papers: read aloud, role play, dictation,
translation), Cambridge 0530 or AQA presented as 4SP1. Latin-American vs Peninsular vocabulary
is fine; Portuguese is not.
