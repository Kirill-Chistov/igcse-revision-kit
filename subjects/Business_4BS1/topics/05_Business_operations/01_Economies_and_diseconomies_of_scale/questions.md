## 1. Recall and calculation

1. **(1)** State **one** external economy of scale.
2. **(1)** Define the term *internal economies of scale*.
3. **(2)** A factory's total costs are $180 000 when it makes 60 000 units and $250 000 when it makes 100 000 units. Calculate the change in average total cost.

## 2. Explain (3 marks each)

4. Explain **one** internal economy of scale a large supermarket chain could benefit from.
5. Explain **one** reason why a business may choose not to grow beyond a certain size.

## 3. Exam-style

6. **(6)** A car manufacturer plans to double its output by opening a second factory. Analyse the impact of economies of scale on the manufacturer.
7. **(9)** A furniture maker in a region with many furniture businesses is considering two options to cut its average costs. Option 1: expand its own factory. Option 2: stay the same size and use the specialist suppliers and trained workers in the region. Justify which **one** of these two options it should choose.

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. Any one: pool of skilled labour; specialist suppliers nearby; better infrastructure/transport links; local training courses.
2. A fall in average total cost as the business's own output increases (from the firm's own growth).
3. $180 000 ÷ 60 000 = $3.00; $250 000 ÷ 100 000 = $2.50 (1) → ATC **falls by $0.50 per unit** (1).
4. Identify (1) e.g. purchasing/bulk buying; develop twice (2): buys very large quantities → suppliers give discounts → lower cost per unit, so it can charge lower prices / earn a higher margin.
5. Identify (1) diseconomies of scale beyond the optimum size; develop twice (2): e.g. workers feel remote from managers → motivation and productivity fall → ATC rises, so profit per unit falls.
6. Two developed impacts in context (3 + 3), e.g. bulk buying of components → lower ATC → lower prices than rivals → higher market share; *or* technical economies from a larger automated line → more output per worker → higher profit margin. Credit a diseconomy (coordinating two sites) as a limiting impact.
7. Paragraph for the chosen option, paragraph against it, judgement in context (`EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` § Justify). Option 1 → internal economies but needs finance and risks diseconomies; Option 2 → external economies with no investment, but rivals get the same benefit so no cost advantage.
