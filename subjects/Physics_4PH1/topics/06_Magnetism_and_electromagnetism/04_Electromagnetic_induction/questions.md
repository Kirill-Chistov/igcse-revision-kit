## 1. Induction and generators (6.15, 6.16)

1. A magnet is pushed into a coil connected to a centre-zero meter, and the needle deflects to the right. State what the needle does when the magnet is: (a) held still inside the coil; (b) pulled out; (c) pushed in faster. (3)
2. Describe **two** ways to induce a voltage in a wire, and state **three** factors that affect the size of the induced voltage. (5)
3. A wire is moved between the poles of a magnet but no voltage is induced. Suggest why. (1)
4. Describe how a coil rotating in a magnetic field generates an alternating voltage. (4)
5. The coil of a generator is turned twice as fast. Describe how the graph of output voltage against time changes. (2)
6. A bicycle dynamo has a magnet that turns inside a fixed coil. Explain why an alternating voltage is induced in the coil. (2)

## 2. Transformers (6.17P–6.20P) — Paper 2 only

7. Describe the structure of a transformer. (3)
8. Explain how a transformer produces an output voltage, and why it does not work with a d.c. supply. (4)
9. A transformer has 500 turns on the primary coil and 25 turns on the secondary. The input voltage is 230 V. Calculate the output voltage and state whether it is a step-up or step-down transformer. (3)
10. A 100% efficient transformer has an input of 230 V and 0.5 A. The output voltage is 11.5 V. Calculate the output current. (3)
11. Explain why electricity is transmitted through the cables at a very high voltage, and why a step-down transformer is used before homes. (4)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. (a) Returns to / stays at zero (1); (b) deflects to the left / opposite way (1); (c) deflects further to the right (1).
2. Move the wire through / across a magnetic field, cutting field lines (1); change the magnetic field through a coil, e.g. move a magnet in or out (1). Factors: speed of movement / rate of change (1); strength of the magnetic field (1); number of turns on the coil (1).
3. It was moved parallel to the field lines / did not cut any field lines (1).
4. The sides of the coil cut the magnetic field lines as it turns (1); a voltage is induced (1); each half turn each side moves in the opposite direction through the field (1); so the voltage reverses direction — alternating; slip rings and brushes connect the coil to the circuit (1).
5. Larger peak voltage (double) (1); higher frequency / twice as many cycles in the same time (1).
6. The magnetic field through the coil keeps changing (1); and reverses direction every half turn, so the induced voltage reverses (1).
7. Two coils of (insulated) wire — primary and secondary (1); wound on the same core (1); made of soft iron (1).
8. Alternating current in the primary produces a changing magnetic field (1); the soft iron core carries the changing field through the secondary coil (1); this induces an alternating voltage in the secondary (1). d.c. gives a constant field, so there is no change and no voltage is induced (1).
9. *V*s = *V*p × *N*s ÷ *N*p = 230 × 25 ÷ 500 (1) = 11.5 V (1); step-down (1).
10. *V*p*I*p = *V*s*I*s (1); 230 × 0.5 = 11.5 × *I*s (1); *I*s = 10 A (1).
11. A step-up transformer raises the voltage so the current is smaller for the same power (1); a smaller current heats the cables less (1); so less energy is wasted (1). The step-down transformer reduces the voltage to a safe level for homes (230 V) (1).
