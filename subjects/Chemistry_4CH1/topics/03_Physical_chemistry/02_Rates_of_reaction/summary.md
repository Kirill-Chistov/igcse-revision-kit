# Rates of reaction — Summary

Specification points 3.9–3.16 · Topic 3 Physical chemistry. 3.14C is **Paper 2 only**.

## Essential facts

- Follow a gas-producing reaction by **gas volume** (gas syringe) or **mass lost** (balance,
  cotton wool). Rate graph: steepest at start; levels off when a reactant is used up; final amount
  set by the limiting reactant.
- Increasing **surface area, concentration, pressure, temperature** or adding a **catalyst**
  increases the rate.
- **Activation energy** — minimum energy colliding particles need to react.

## Collision theory

- Concentration / pressure: more particles per volume → more **frequent** collisions.
- Surface area: more particles exposed → more **frequent** collisions.
- Temperature: more kinetic energy → greater **proportion** of collisions with energy ≥ Ea
  (and more frequent).
- **Catalyst**: speeds up a reaction, chemically unchanged at the end; alternative pathway with
  **lower Ea**.

## Reaction profiles (P2)

- Ea: reactants → peak. ΔH: reactants → products. Catalysed curve: lower peak, same ΔH.

## Practicals

- CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂: vary chip size **or** acid concentration; keep the rest
  the same; record volume every 30 s.
- 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂: same mass of different powdered solids; most O₂ in a fixed time = best
  catalyst; filter, dry, reweigh → mass unchanged.

## Common traps

- Faster ≠ more product. Temperature: say "proportion of collisions with enough energy".
