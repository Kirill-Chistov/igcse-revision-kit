## 3. Explain and analyse

7. A small bakery forecasts a negative closing balance in July because it plans to buy a new oven.
   Analyse the importance of this forecast to the bakery. (6)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. A prediction of a business's cash inflows and outflows over a future period.
2. The cash a business has at the end of a period: opening balance + net cash flow.
3. 9,800 − 14,300 (1) = (€4,500), i.e. −€4,500 (1).
4. April closing = 1,500 + (14,200 − 11,600) = 4,100 = May opening; May closing =
   4,100 + (−4,500) (1) = (€400), i.e. −€400 — the business would be overdrawn (1).
5. Customers buy on credit (1) → revenue and profit are recorded now but the cash arrives later (1)
   → the business may not have cash to pay suppliers or wages when they are due (1).
6. It is based on estimates (1) → actual sales may be lower or costs higher than predicted, e.g. a
   supplier raises prices (1) → so the business may still face an unexpected shortage (1).
7. Look for linked, in-context points, e.g. the forecast shows the shortfall in advance → the
   bakery can arrange an overdraft before July or delay the oven purchase → it avoids being unable
   to pay suppliers of flour or staff wages → protects production and reputation; and/or it can
   show the forecast to the bank to support a loan for the oven. Two points × three links, or one
   point × five links.
