# Introduction to organic chemistry — Questions

Specification points 4.1–4.6 · Topic 4 Organic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. What is a hydrocarbon? (1)
2. State what is meant by a homologous series. (3)
3. What is a functional group? (1)
4. What are isomers? (2)

## 2. Formulae and names

5. Hexane has the molecular formula C₆H₁₄. Give its empirical formula and its structural formula. (2)
6. Give the general formula of the alkanes and use it to find the molecular formula of the alkane
   with 8 carbon atoms. (2)
7. Name: (a) CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (b) CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃ (c) CH₃CH₂CH₂OH (d) CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH. (4)
8. Draw the displayed formulae of the two isomers of C₄H₁₀. (2)
9. A student draws CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ with the chain bent into an L shape and says it is an isomer of
   butane. Explain why the student is wrong. (1)

## 3. Reaction types

10. Classify each reaction as substitution, addition or combustion:
    (a) C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (b) C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl (c) C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O. (3)
11. Explain how you can tell from the number of products that a reaction is an addition reaction. (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. A compound of hydrogen and carbon only (1).
2. Any three: same general formula (1); same functional group (1); similar chemical properties (1);
   trend in physical properties / each member differs by CH₂ (1).
3. An atom or group of atoms that determines the characteristic reactions of a compound (1).
4. Same molecular formula (1); different structural / displayed formulae (1).
5. C₃H₇ (1); CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (1).
6. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (1); C₈H₁₈ (1).
7. (a) hexane (1) (b) pent-2-ene (1) (c) propan-1-ol / propanol (1) (d) butanoic acid (1).
8. Butane: four C in a chain with ten H (1); methylpropane: three C chain with a CH₃ on the middle
   carbon, ten H (1). Every C has four bonds.
9. It has the same arrangement of atoms (still a four-carbon unbranched chain); bending a chain does
   not change the structure (1).
10. (a) addition (1) (b) substitution (1) (c) combustion (1).
11. Addition gives a single product (two reactants → one product) (1).
