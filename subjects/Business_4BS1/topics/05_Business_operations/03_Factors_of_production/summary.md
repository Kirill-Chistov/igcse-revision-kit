## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Land | natural resources (sites, raw materials) |
| Labour | physical and mental effort of people at work |
| Capital | man-made resources used in production (machinery, tools, buildings) |
| Enterprise | the entrepreneur's skill and risk-taking in organising the other three |
| Labour-intensive | high proportion of labour compared with capital; labour the main cost |
| Capital-intensive | high proportion of capital compared with labour; capital the main cost |

## Essential facts

- Enterprise chooses the mix; **capital replaces labour** as technology improves, wages rise and customers want volume, low prices and consistency → fewer, more skilled workers; more finance needed.
- Labour-intensive: hairdressing, restaurants, crafts — flexible, low set-up cost, personal quality / high unit cost if wages high, less consistent, staff to manage.
- Capital-intensive: car assembly, oil refining — high productivity, low unit cost, consistent, 24/7 / high initial cost, less flexible, breakdowns, job losses.

## Common traps

- Capital here = machinery, not money.
- Managers are labour; enterprise is the owner's risk-taking.
