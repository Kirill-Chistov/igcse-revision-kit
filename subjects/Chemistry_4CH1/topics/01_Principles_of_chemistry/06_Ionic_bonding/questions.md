# Ionic bonding — Questions

Specification points 1.37–1.43 · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Give the charge on each ion: potassium, sulfide, aluminium, nitrate, ammonium. (5)
2. Define *ionic bonding*. (2)
3. State the conditions under which sodium chloride conducts electricity. (2)

## 2. Formulae

4. Write the formulae of: calcium chloride, lithium oxide, aluminium sulfate, zinc nitrate,
   copper(II) hydroxide, magnesium nitride. (6)

## 3. Understanding and explanation

5. Describe, in terms of electrons, how a magnesium atom becomes a magnesium ion. (2)
6. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram to show the formation of lithium fluoride from its atoms. Show outer
   electrons only. (3)
7. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for the ions in sodium oxide, Na₂O. (3)
8. Explain why magnesium oxide has a high melting point. (3)
9. Explain why solid potassium bromide does not conduct electricity but its solution does. (2)
10. Suggest why calcium oxide has a higher melting point than potassium chloride. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. K⁺ (1); S²⁻ (1); Al³⁺ (1); NO₃⁻ (1); NH₄⁺ (1).
2. (Strong) electrostatic attraction (1) between oppositely charged ions (1).
3. When molten (1); when dissolved in water / aqueous (1).
4. CaCl₂ (1); Li₂O (1); Al₂(SO₄)₃ (1); Zn(NO₃)₂ (1); Cu(OH)₂ (1); Mg₃N₂ (1).
5. Loses (1) two (outer) electrons (1) (to form Mg²⁺).
6. Li with one cross in its outer shell and F with seven dots (1); arrow showing transfer of the
   electron (1); [Li]⁺ and [F]⁻ with 8 electrons (7 dots + 1 cross) in brackets with charges (1).
7. Two [Na]⁺ ions (or [Na]⁺ × 2) (1); [O]²⁻ with 6 dots and 2 crosses (1); brackets and charges
   (1).
8. Giant (ionic) lattice (1); strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions (1);
   lots of energy needed to overcome them (1).
9. In the solid the ions are in fixed positions / cannot move (1); in solution the ions are free to
   move (and carry charge) (1).
10. Ca²⁺ and O²⁻ have higher charges than K⁺ and Cl⁻ (1); so stronger electrostatic attractions,
    needing more energy to overcome (1).
