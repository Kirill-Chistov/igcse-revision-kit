# Movement of substances into and out of cells — Questions

**Specification points 2.15–2.17** · Papers 1 and 2

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Recall and understanding

1. Define *osmosis*.
2. State two differences between diffusion and active transport.
3. Explain why a red blood cell placed in pure water bursts, but a plant cell does not.
4. Explain why increasing temperature increases the rate of diffusion.
5. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube of side 5 cm.

## Practical

6. In the agar-cube practical, which cube becomes colourless first? Explain why.
7. Potato cylinder results: initial mass 2.50 g, final mass 2.15 g. Calculate the percentage
   change in mass.
8. Why is percentage change in mass used rather than change in mass?
9. Why are the cylinders blotted dry before reweighing?
10. A student's graph crosses the x-axis at 0.32 mol dm⁻³. What does this tell you?
11. In the Visking tubing experiment, explain why the liquid rises up the capillary tube.

## Exam-style

12. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how root hair cells take up mineral ions from soil where the
    concentration of ions is lower than inside the cell.
13. **[3 marks]** Cells in the centre of a large organism receive oxygen more slowly by diffusion
    than cells in a small organism. *Explain* why.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Net movement of water molecules from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution (1)
   through a partially permeable membrane (1).
2. Any two: diffusion is down, active transport against, the concentration gradient; diffusion
   needs no energy, active transport needs energy from respiration; active transport needs carrier
   proteins in a membrane.
3. Both gain water by osmosis (1); the red blood cell has no cell wall so swells until it bursts
   (1); the plant cell wall resists expansion, so the cell becomes turgid (1).
4. Particles gain kinetic energy / move faster (1), so net movement down the gradient is faster (1).
5. SA = 6 × 25 = 150 cm²; V = 125 cm³ (1); ratio 150 : 125 = 1.2 : 1 (1).
6. The smallest (1); it has the largest SA:V / shortest distance to its centre (1), so acid reaches
   the centre soonest (1).
7. (2.15 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = −14% (a 14% decrease).
8. Cylinders may start with different masses, so % change allows fair comparison.
9. To remove surface solution, which would add to the mass (1) and is not water taken in by the
   cells (1).
10. The potato cell contents have a concentration equivalent to about 0.32 mol dm⁻³ sucrose / no
    net movement of water at this concentration.
11. Water moves into the tubing by osmosis (1) from the dilute water outside to the concentrated
    sucrose solution inside, through the partially permeable tubing (1); sucrose cannot move out,
    so volume inside increases and liquid is pushed up (1).
12. By active transport (1); against the concentration gradient / from low to high concentration
    (1); using carrier proteins in the cell membrane (1); energy from respiration in mitochondria
    (1).
13. A large organism has a smaller surface area to volume ratio (1); less surface area to supply
    each unit of volume (1); longer diffusion distance to the centre (1).
