# Rates of reaction — Questions

Specification points 3.9–3.16 · Topic 3 Physical chemistry. Question 9 is **Paper 2 only**.
Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall and explanation

1. State what is meant by the term *catalyst*. (2)
2. State what is meant by *activation energy*. (1)
3. Explain, in terms of particles, why increasing the concentration of hydrochloric acid increases
   the rate of its reaction with marble chips. (2)
4. Explain why powdered calcium carbonate reacts faster than the same mass of marble lumps. (2)
5. Explain, in terms of particles, why increasing the temperature increases the rate of a
   reaction. (3)
6. Explain why increasing the pressure increases the rate of a reaction between two gases. (2)
7. Explain how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction. (2)

## 2. Graphs

8. A student reacts excess marble chips with 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid and
   collects the carbon dioxide.
   (a) Explain why the graph of volume against time levels off. (1)
   (b) The experiment is repeated with 50 cm³ of 2.0 mol/dm³ acid. Describe how the curve
   differs. (2)
   (c) It is repeated again with 25 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ acid. State the effect on the final volume
   of gas. (1)

## 3. Reaction profiles — Paper 2 only

9. The reaction of hydrogen with oxygen is exothermic. Draw a reaction profile for it, labelling
   the activation energy, ΔH, and the pathway with a catalyst. (4)

## 4. Practical

10. Describe an experiment to investigate the effect of the surface area of marble chips on the
    rate of reaction with dilute hydrochloric acid. (5)
11. A student tests three solids as catalysts for the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.
    (a) Write the equation for the reaction. (1)
    (b) Give two variables the student must keep the same. (2)
    (c) Describe how the student can show that the best solid is a catalyst. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Substance that increases the rate of a reaction (1); chemically unchanged at the end (1).
2. Minimum energy (colliding) particles need to react (1).
3. More (acid) particles in the same volume (1); more frequent collisions (1).
4. Larger surface area / more particles exposed (1); more frequent collisions (1).
5. Particles have more (kinetic) energy / move faster (1); greater proportion of collisions have
   energy ≥ activation energy / more successful collisions (1); collisions more frequent (1).
6. More (gas) particles in the same volume (1); more frequent collisions (1).
7. Provides an alternative pathway / route (1) with lower activation energy (1).
8. (a) The acid (limiting reactant) is used up (1).
   (b) Steeper at the start / levels off sooner (1); levels off at a higher (double) volume —
   the acid is limiting and there is twice as much (1).
   (c) Half the final volume (1).
9. Products lower than reactants, both labelled (1); curve with a peak (1); Ea from reactants to
   peak (1); ΔH from reactants to products **and** catalysed curve with a lower peak (1).
10. Measured volume of acid in a flask (1); add a weighed mass of chips, bung + gas syringe /
    flask on a balance (1); record volume (or mass) at regular intervals (1); repeat with the same
    mass of smaller chips / powder (1); same volume, concentration and temperature of acid (1).
11. (a) 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (1).
    (b) Any two: volume of H₂O₂; concentration of H₂O₂; mass of solid; temperature; particle size
    of solid (2).
    (c) Weigh the solid before (1); filter off, wash and dry after the reaction (1); mass is the
    same (1).
