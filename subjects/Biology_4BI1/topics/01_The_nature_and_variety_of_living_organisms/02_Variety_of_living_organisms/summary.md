# Variety of living organisms — Summary

**Specification points 1.2–1.4** · Papers 1 and 2 (no B content)

## Groups

- **Eukaryotic** (cells with a nucleus): plants, animals, fungi, protoctists.
- **Prokaryotic** (no nucleus): bacteria.
- **Viruses**: not living organisms.

## Features and examples

| Group | Key features | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plants | multicellular; chloroplasts, photosynthesis; cellulose walls; store starch or sucrose | maize; peas, beans |
| Animals | multicellular; no chloroplasts; no walls; nervous co-ordination, move about; store glycogen | humans; housefly, mosquito |
| Fungi | no photosynthesis; mycelium of hyphae with many nuclei (or single cells); chitin walls; saprotrophic; may store glycogen | *Mucor*; yeast (single-celled) |
| Protoctists | microscopic, single-celled; animal-like or plant-like | *Amoeba*; *Chlorella*; *Plasmodium* (malaria) |
| Bacteria | single-celled; wall, membrane, cytoplasm, plasmids; no nucleus — circular chromosome; most feed off living or dead organisms | *Lactobacillus bulgaricus* (rod, yoghurt); *Pneumococcus* (sphere, pneumonia) |
| Viruses | smaller than bacteria; protein coat + DNA **or** RNA; no cells; reproduce only inside living cells; infect all organisms | TMV (no chloroplasts → discoloured leaves); influenza (flu); HIV (AIDS) |

## Definitions

- **Saprotrophic nutrition:** enzymes secreted onto food, digested outside, soluble products absorbed.
- **Pathogen:** a microorganism that causes disease — may be a fungus, bacterium, protoctist or virus.

## Traps

- Fungi are not plants. Yeast is a fungus, not a bacterium.
- Bacteria do have DNA — just no nucleus.
- Viruses are not cells and not alive.
