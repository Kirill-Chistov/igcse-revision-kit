# Electric charge — Questions

Specification points 2.22P–2.28P · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1 · Paper 2 only.

> These questions and the marking guidance are **written for this repository by AI**. They are not
> official Pearson questions and the guidance is not an official mark scheme.

## 1. Conductors, insulators and charging (2.22P, 2.24P, 2.25P)

1. From this list, identify two electrical conductors and two insulators: copper, polythene, aluminium, rubber, graphite, glass. (2)
2. A polythene rod becomes negatively charged when rubbed with a cloth. Explain how, and state the charge on the cloth. (3)
3. Explain why a metal rod held in the hand cannot be charged by rubbing, but a plastic rod can. (2)
4. State what happens when two negatively charged rods are brought close together, and when a negative and a positive rod are brought close together. (2)

## 2. Practical (2.23P)

5. Describe how you would show that a rubbed acetate rod and a rubbed polythene rod carry opposite charges. (4)
6. A student finds the experiment works poorly on a humid day. Suggest why. (2)
7. A rubbed rod attracts a hanging rod. Explain why this does not prove that the two rods carry opposite charges. (2)

## 3. Electrostatic effects (2.26P)

8. A negatively charged balloon sticks to an uncharged wall. Explain why, in terms of electrons. (3)
9. A charged metal sphere on an insulating stand is connected to earth by a wire. Describe what happens if the sphere is (a) negative, (b) positive. (2)
10. A person who has walked across a nylon carpet gets a small shock from a metal door handle. Explain this in terms of electrons. (3)

## 4. Dangers and uses (2.27P, 2.28P)

11. Explain why refuelling an aircraft can be dangerous, and how the danger is reduced. (5)
12. Describe how a photocopier uses electrostatic charge to copy a page. (5)
13. Explain how an inkjet printer makes each drop of ink land in the correct place on the paper. (4)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. Conductors: any two of copper, aluminium, graphite (1); insulators: any two of polythene, rubber, glass (1).
2. Friction transfers electrons (1) from the cloth to the rod (1); the rod gains electrons so is negative, and the cloth, having lost electrons, is positive (1).
3. In a metal electrons are free to move, so charge flows away through the hand to earth (1); in plastic electrons cannot move, so the charge stays on the rod (1).
4. Two negative rods repel (1); negative and positive attract (1).
5. Rub a polythene rod and hang it so it can turn freely, e.g. in a stirrup on a nylon thread (1); bring a second rubbed polythene rod near: it repels, so the hanging rod is charged (1); bring the rubbed acetate rod near without touching (1); it attracts, while polythene repelled, so the acetate carries the opposite charge (1).
6. Moisture on the rods makes the surface conduct (1), so the charge leaks away (1).
7. A charged object also attracts an uncharged object (1), so the hanging rod might be uncharged; only repulsion proves charge (1).
8. The balloon's negative charge repels electrons in the wall (1) away from the surface nearest the balloon (1); that surface is left positive, and unlike charges attract (1).
9. (a) Electrons flow from the sphere to earth until it is neutral (1); (b) electrons flow from earth to the sphere until it is neutral (1).
10. Friction with the carpet transfers electrons, so the person becomes charged (1); near the earthed handle, electrons jump across the gap / flow through the person (1) suddenly, as a spark, which is felt as a shock (1).
11. Fuel flowing through the hose causes friction, transferring electrons (1); charge builds up on the aircraft / tanker, giving a large voltage (1); a spark may jump (1) and ignite the fuel vapour, causing a fire or explosion (1); the aircraft and tanker are connected to earth by a conducting cable so the charge flows away before it can build up (1).
12. The drum is given a positive charge (1); light reflected from the white parts of the page makes the drum conduct there, so the charge leaks away and charge is left only where the page is dark (1); negatively charged toner is attracted to the charged areas (1); positively charged paper attracts the toner off the drum (1); heat melts the toner onto the paper (1).
13. Each drop is given a charge (1); the drops pass between deflecting plates with a voltage across them (1); each drop is attracted to the oppositely charged plate and deflected (1); a computer varies the charge on each drop to set how far it is deflected (1).
