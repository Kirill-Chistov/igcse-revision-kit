# Motion in the universe — Questions

Specification points 8.2–8.6 · Topic 8 Astrophysics. Marks in brackets.

> **All questions, answers and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository and
> are not official Pearson questions or mark schemes.**

## 1. Recall

1. Complete: a galaxy is a large collection of billions of ______; the universe is a large
   collection of billions of ______; our solar system is in the ______ galaxy. (3)
2. Name the force that keeps an artificial satellite in orbit around the Earth. (1)
3. State two differences between the orbit of a comet and the orbit of a planet. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

4. The Moon has a gravitational field strength of 1.6 N/kg. Explain why this is smaller than on
   the Earth. (2)
5. An astronaut has a mass of 70 kg. State her mass on the Moon and calculate her weight there
   (*g* = 1.6 N/kg). (3)
6. Explain how the gravitational force of the Sun keeps a planet moving in a circular orbit. (3)
7. Describe how the speed of a comet changes during one orbit of the Sun. (2)

## 3. Application

8. The Moon orbits the Earth at a radius of 3.84 × 10⁸ m with a time period of 27.3 days.
   Calculate its orbital speed. (3)
9. A satellite orbits at 7.7 km/s at a radius of 6.8 × 10⁶ m from the centre of the Earth.
   Calculate its time period in seconds. (3)

## 4. Higher demand

10. A satellite takes 24 hours to orbit the Earth at 3.07 × 10³ m/s. Calculate its orbital radius. (3)
11. **[4 marks]** *Explain* why a satellite orbiting far above the Earth has a smaller gravitational
    force on it than it would have on the ground, but still stays in orbit.

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. stars (1); galaxies (1); Milky Way (1).
2. Gravitational force / gravity of the Earth (1).
3. Any two (1 each): comet orbit is highly elliptical / planet's nearly circular; comet's speed
   changes a lot / planet's almost constant; comet orbit can be at any angle to the plane of the
   planets' orbits.
4. The Moon has a much smaller mass than the Earth (1), so its gravitational pull on each kg of mass
   is weaker (1).
5. Mass is still 70 kg (1); W = m × g = 70 × 1.6 (1) = 112 N (1).
6. The Sun's gravitational force acts towards the centre of the orbit / towards the Sun (1); at right
   angles to the planet's motion (1); so it changes the planet's direction continuously without
   changing its speed (1).
7. Speeds up as it approaches the Sun / fastest when closest to the Sun (1); slows down as it moves
   away / slowest when furthest away (1).
8. T = 27.3 × 86 400 = 2.36 × 10⁶ s (1); v = 2πr/T = 2π × 3.84 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.36 × 10⁶ (1)
   = 1.0 × 10³ m/s (1).
9. v = 7.7 × 10³ m/s (1); T = 2πr/v = 2π × 6.8 × 10⁶ ÷ 7.7 × 10³ (1) = 5.5 × 10³ s (1).
10. T = 24 × 3600 = 86 400 s (1); r = vT/2π = 3.07 × 10³ × 86 400 ÷ 2π (1) = 4.2 × 10⁷ m (1).
11. Gravitational field strength decreases with distance from the Earth's centre (1), so the force is
    smaller (1); but it is not zero and still acts towards the centre of the Earth (1), continually
    changing the satellite's direction so it follows a curved path / orbit (1).
