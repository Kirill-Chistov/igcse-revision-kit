## Questions

1. **[2 marks]** *Define* sustainable development.
2. **[2 marks]** *Explain* the difference between a strategy and a technique.
3. **[3 marks]** *Describe* one social, one economic and one environmental technique used to try to
   achieve sustainable development.
4. **[4 marks]** *Explain* why appropriate technology is more sustainable than high-tech solutions
   in many LICs.
5. **[4 marks]** *Compare* top-down and bottom-up development projects.
6. **[6 marks]** *Evaluate* the use of international aid to reduce uneven development.
7. **[6 marks]** *Evaluate* fair trade and debt relief as strategies to reduce the development gap.
8. **[7 marks]** For a named MIC or LIC you have studied, *explain* the reasons for its current level
   of development.
9. **[7 marks]** For a named MIC or LIC you have studied, *explain* how strategies and techniques
   have been used to raise its quality of life and standard of living.

## Answers and marking guidance (not official)

1. Development that meets the needs of the present (1) without compromising the ability of future
   generations to meet their own needs (1). Accept the balance of economic, social and
   environmental aims.
2. Strategy = a plan, policy or agreement (1); technique = the practical method used to carry it
   out (1).
3. One mark each, e.g. social: community health workers / girls' education; economic: microfinance
   / skills training; environmental: solar mini-grids / drip irrigation / agroforestry.
4. Cheap to build and run (1); uses local materials and skills (1), so locals can repair it (1);
   no imported fuel or parts / no debt (1); keeps working after the donor leaves (1). Max 4.
5. Who decides: governments/large organisations vs communities/NGOs (1); scale: large and costly
   vs small and cheap (1); technology: high-tech vs appropriate (1); outcome: national gains but
   may miss the poorest vs local benefit that lasts but reaches few (1).
6. Strengths: emergency aid saves lives; long-term aid funds schools, clinics, infrastructure;
   bottom-up NGO aid targets the poorest and is sustainable (up to 3). Weaknesses: dependency;
   tied aid benefits donor firms; loans add to debt; corruption; big projects miss the poorest
   (up to 3). Judgement needed for full marks: most effective when long-term, untied, bottom-up.
7. Fair trade: stable, higher price, premium for community projects vs small share of trade, crops
   only, certification costs (up to 3). Debt relief: money freed for services, poverty-reduction
   conditions vs spending cuts, corruption, new borrowing, no rise in exports (up to 3). Judgement
   linking to causes of the gap.
8. Vietnam: Đổi Mới 1986 opened economy to trade, private business, foreign investment; export
   manufacturing (trade ≈ 170% of GDP) created wage jobs; productive rice farming raised rural
   incomes; better health, education, electricity. Result: GDP per capita < $700 → ~$4,500;
   extreme poverty 14% → < 4%. Not yet HIC: productivity, technology, skills, climate risk.
   Level marking — top level needs named place, several explained reasons and figures.
9. Health insurance 93%, infant mortality 32.6 → 12.1; electricity 14% → nearly all; education
   enrolment 95% / 80%; Can Tho 420,000 protected. Each: what, figure, how it helps, link to QoL
   or SoL. Top level needs several strategies, each with a figure and a clear link.
