## 1. Recall

1. Define (a) amplitude (b) wavelength (c) frequency (d) period (e) wavefront.
2. Give one example of a transverse wave and one of a longitudinal wave.
3. State the equation linking wave speed, frequency and wavelength. Which wave equation is given
   in the exam?

## 2. Understanding

4. A cork floats on a pond as ripples pass. Describe its motion and explain what this shows about
   waves.
5. What are compressions and rarefactions?

## 3. Application

6. A water wave has frequency 2.0 Hz and wavelength 0.75 m. Calculate its speed.
7. A microwave oven uses microwaves of frequency 2.45 GHz. Speed = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Calculate the
   wavelength.
8. A sound wave has wavelength 1.7 m in air (speed 340 m/s). Calculate its frequency and period.

## 4. Higher demand

9. A 500 Hz sound passes from air (340 m/s) into water (1500 m/s). Calculate the wavelength in
   each and explain why the frequency does not change.

## 5. Exam-style prompts

10. **[2 marks]** *Explain* the difference between transverse and longitudinal waves.
11. **[3 marks]** An ambulance with its siren on drives towards a pedestrian and then past her.
    *Explain* why she hears the pitch fall as it passes.
12. **[3 marks]** Straight water waves travel from deep into shallow water, meeting the boundary at
    an angle. *Explain* why their direction changes.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Not an official mark scheme.*

1. (a) maximum displacement from the rest position (b) distance from a point on one wave to the
   same point on the next wave (c) number of waves passing a point per second (d) time for one
   complete wave to pass a point (e) line joining points at the same stage of oscillation, e.g. a
   crest.
2. Transverse: any EM wave / light / water ripples / wave on a string. Longitudinal: sound.
3. *v* = *f* λ (recall). *f* = 1/*T* is given.
4. Bobs up and down (1); does not move along with the wave (1); so waves transfer energy, not
   matter (1).
5. Compression: region where particles are closer together; rarefaction: further apart.
6. *v* = 2.0 × 0.75 = 1.5 m/s
7. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.45 × 10⁹ = 0.12 m
8. *f* = 340 ÷ 1.7 = 200 Hz; *T* = 1/200 = 0.005 s
9. Air: 340 ÷ 500 = 0.68 m; water: 1500 ÷ 500 = 3.0 m. The frequency is set by the source, which
   does not change; only the speed (and so the wavelength) changes.
10. Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer (1); longitudinal:
    oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer (1).
11. Any three: source emits wavefronts from a moving position (1); approaching — wavefronts
    squashed together / shorter wavelength, higher frequency (1); moving away — wavefronts spread
    out / longer wavelength, lower frequency (1); wave speed unchanged, and higher frequency = higher
    pitch (1). Just naming the Doppler effect does not explain it.
12. Waves slow down in shallow water (1); one end of each wavefront reaches the shallow water and
    slows first (1); so the wavefront turns / bends towards the normal (1).
