# Market research — Summary

Specification points 4.1.1–4.1.3 · both papers (apply to a small or a large business)

## Purpose (4.1.1)

- **Market research** — gathering, analysing and interpreting information about customers,
  competitors and the market.
- Why: understand customer needs · find gaps in the market · reduce risk (not remove it) ·
  inform decisions on the 4Ps.

## Methods (4.1.2)

| | Primary (field) | Secondary (desk) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | New data, collected for this purpose | Existing data, collected by others |
| Methods | survey, questionnaire, focus group, observation, test marketing | internet, market reports, government reports |
| + | specific, up to date, not seen by rivals | cheap, quick, large samples |
| − | costly, slow, risk of bias | out of date, not specific, rivals have it |

- Focus group → detailed reasons, small sample. Observation → what people do, not why.
  Test marketing → real buying behaviour, but rivals see the product.
- Market reports: expert but expensive. Government reports: free but general and dated.

## Data (4.1.3)

- **Qualitative** = opinions and reasons in words (focus groups, open questions) → explains *why*,
  hard to analyse.
- **Quantitative** = numbers (closed questions, sales data) → easy to analyse and compare,
  does not explain *why*.
- **Social media**: polls, comments/reviews, analytics → cheap, fast, reaches target market;
  but biased sample, fake reviews, lots to analyse.
- **Reliable data** = accurate and represents the market. Weakened by small/unrepresentative
  samples, biased questions, dishonest answers, out-of-date data.
- Unreliable data → wrong decisions → wasted money; research no longer reduces risk.

## Common traps

- Online questionnaire = still primary. Primary/secondary depends on who collected the data.
- Qualitative/quantitative ≠ primary/secondary.
