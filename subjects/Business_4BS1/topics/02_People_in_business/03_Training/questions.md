## Recall

1. Define *induction training*. (1)
2. State two things a new employee may learn during induction. (2)
3. State one example of off-the-job training. (1)

## Explain and apply

4. Explain one disadvantage to a business of off-the-job training. (3)
5. Explain one benefit to employees of being trained. (3)
6. A small bakery has four staff. Outline one reason why it uses on-the-job training. (2)
7. Explain one reason why a factory complies with health and safety laws. (3)

## Marking guidance

*Written for this repository — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. Training given to new employees when they start, to introduce them to the business, its
   people, rules and procedures.
2. Any two: colleagues/managers, layout, rules and procedures, health and safety, aims of the
   business.
3. College course, training centre, conference, online course.
4. Identify (costly / no output / may leave once qualified) + two links, e.g. fees and wages
   paid with no output → higher costs → lower profit.
5. Identify (new skills/qualifications) → promotion more likely → higher pay / job security.
6. Only four staff and cannot spare workers or pay fees → learns from the baker while still
   producing → keeps costs low.
7. Identify (avoid accidents / fines / claims) → e.g. fewer injuries → less absence → output
   maintained.
