## Answers and marking guidance (not official)

1. Hardware that captures data from outside the computer and converts it into a form the computer
   can process.
2. An output device that converts a signal from a computer into physical movement (e.g. motor, valve).
3. Humidity sensor.
4. Infra-red.
5. Conductive layer under glass has an electrostatic field (1); finger is a conductor and changes the
   field where it touches (1); the change is measured to calculate the coordinates of the touch (1).
6. Light (laser/LED) is shone on the barcode (1); dark bars reflect less light than white spaces (1);
   a sensor detects the reflected pattern and it is converted to a (digital) number/product code (1).
7. LCD screen: liquid crystals block or let through light from a backlight (1); LED screen: each
   pixel is made of LEDs that give out their own light / no backlight (1).
8. Any two: continuous readings (24/7); more accurate/reliable; can work in dangerous places; no
   human needed to be present; faster response.
9. (a) light (b) accelerometer (c) moisture (d) pressure (e) level.
10. Flow sensor (1) — rate of flow of air through the tube (1); gas (oxygen) sensor (1) — oxygen
    level (1).
11. Laser printer (1); fast / prints many copies quickly (1); large toner cartridge and paper tray,
    so fewer refills (1).
12. One mark each, max 5: drum given a charge; laser beam scans the drum removing charge to leave an
    (electrostatic) image of the page; charged toner sticks only to the image; oppositely charged
    paper rolls over the drum and attracts the toner; fuser/heated rollers melt the toner onto the
    paper; drum discharged ready for next page.
13. One mark each, max 4: digital design/CAD file created; design sliced into thin layers; material
    (e.g. melted plastic/powder) laid down one layer at a time by the nozzle/print head; each layer
    hardens/bonds to the one below; object built up layer by layer (additive manufacturing).
14. Infra-red: a touch breaks the IR beams across the screen (1), and any object can break them,
    including a glove (1). Capacitive: relies on the finger conducting electricity to change the
    electrostatic field (1); a thick glove does not conduct, so no touch is detected (1).
