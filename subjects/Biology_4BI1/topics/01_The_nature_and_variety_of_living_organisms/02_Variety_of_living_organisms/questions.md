# Variety of living organisms — Questions

**Specification points 1.2–1.4** · Papers 1 and 2 (no B content)

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## 1. Recall

1. Name the four groups of eukaryotic organisms.
2. What substance makes up the cell walls of (a) plants, (b) fungi?
3. Name the carbohydrate stored by (a) plants, (b) animals.
4. Define *pathogen*, and name the four types of organism or particle that can be pathogens.
5. Name the disease caused by (a) *Plasmodium*, (b) *Pneumococcus*, (c) HIV.

## 2. Understanding

6. Describe how *Mucor* obtains its food.
7. State two structural differences between a bacterial cell and a plant cell.
8. Give three reasons why viruses are not considered living organisms.
9. Explain why tobacco plants infected with tobacco mosaic virus have discoloured leaves.

## 3. Application

10. An organism is single-celled, has a nucleus and chloroplasts, and lives in pond water. Which
    group does it belong to? Name an example.
11. An organism is made of hyphae with walls of chitin. Give two other features you would expect it
    to have.
12. A microorganism has a cell wall, a circular chromosome and plasmids. Identify its group and
    give one piece of evidence that it is **not** eukaryotic.

## 4. Exam-style

13. **[4 marks]** *Compare* the features of plants and fungi.
14. **[3 marks]** *Describe* the structure of a virus.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Plants, animals, fungi, protoctists.
2. (a) cellulose; (b) chitin.
3. (a) starch (or sucrose); (b) glycogen.
4. A microorganism that causes disease (1). Fungi, bacteria, protoctists, viruses (1).
5. (a) malaria; (b) pneumonia; (c) AIDS.
6. Its hyphae secrete digestive enzymes onto the food (1); the food is digested outside the hyphae
   (1); the soluble products are absorbed (1). (Saprotrophic nutrition.)
7. Any two: bacterium has no nucleus / has a circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm / has
   plasmids / has no chloroplasts (most) / wall not made of cellulose.
8. Any three: no cellular structure / no cytoplasm or cell membrane; can reproduce only inside
   living cells; do not carry out nutrition, respiration, excretion or growth on their own.
9. The virus prevents chloroplasts from forming (1), so affected parts of the leaf lack the green
   colour / cannot photosynthesise (1).
10. Protoctist (1); *Chlorella* (1).
11. Any two: many nuclei in the hyphae; cannot photosynthesise / no chloroplasts; saprotrophic
    nutrition (extracellular digestion); may store glycogen; forms a mycelium.
12. Bacterium / prokaryote (1); it has no nucleus — DNA is a circular chromosome free in the
    cytoplasm (1).
13. One mark per paired point, max 4: plants photosynthesise, fungi cannot; plant walls cellulose,
    fungal walls chitin; plants store starch/sucrose, fungi may store glycogen; plants are
    multicellular with cells, fungi usually hyphae with many nuclei (some single-celled); plants make
    their own food, fungi feed saprotrophically. Similarity: both eukaryotic / both have cell walls.
14. Protein coat (1); containing nucleic acid (1); only one type — DNA or RNA (1). (Also credit: no
    cellular structure / no cytoplasm or membrane.)
