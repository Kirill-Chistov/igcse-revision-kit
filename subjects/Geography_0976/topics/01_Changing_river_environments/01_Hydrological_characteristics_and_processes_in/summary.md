# Rivers and drainage basins: characteristics and processes — Summary

Specification points 1.1.1–1.1.5 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Basin terms:** drainage basin (area drained by a river and its tributaries) · watershed (its
  boundary, on high ground) · source · mouth · tributary · confluence · channel (bed + banks).
- **Channel terms:** width · depth · wetted perimeter (bed and banks in contact with water) ·
  velocity (m/s) · **discharge = cross-sectional area × velocity** (m³/s, cumecs).
- **Long profile:** steep near the source, gentle near the mouth — concave.
- **Bradshaw model, downstream:** discharge, width, depth, velocity and load quantity **increase**;
  load particle size, bed roughness and gradient **decrease**.
- **Why velocity rises:** smoother, deeper channel → less of the water touches the wetted perimeter
  → less friction.
- **Water cycle:** closed global system; the drainage basin is an **open system** — input
  (precipitation) → stores → flows → outputs (evapotranspiration, river discharge to the sea).
- **Fast route → flood risk:** overland flow fastest, throughflow slower, groundwater flow slowest.
- **Erosion:** hydraulic action · abrasion · attrition · solution. Vertical near the source,
  lateral downstream.
- **Transport:** traction (boulders rolled) · saltation (pebbles bounced) · suspension (fine silt and
  clay) · solution (dissolved). Traction + saltation = bedload.
- **Deposition** when velocity falls: after a flood, on the floodplain, inside meander bends, at the
  mouth, when load increases. Heaviest dropped first.

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Interception | precipitation caught by vegetation before reaching the ground |
| Infiltration | water soaking into the soil from the surface |
| Percolation | water moving down from the soil into the rock |
| Overland flow | water flowing over the surface (rain too heavy, or soil saturated) |
| Throughflow | water moving downslope through the soil |
| Groundwater flow | slow movement of water through saturated rock |
| Evapotranspiration | evaporation + transpiration |
| Hydraulic action | force of water, air forced into cracks, rock breaks away |
| Abrasion | load scraping the bed and banks |
| Attrition | load knocking together — smaller, rounder |

## Common traps

- Velocity *increases* downstream. Attrition wears the load, not the banks.
- Infiltration (into soil) ≠ percolation (soil → rock). Discharge is m³/s, not m/s.
