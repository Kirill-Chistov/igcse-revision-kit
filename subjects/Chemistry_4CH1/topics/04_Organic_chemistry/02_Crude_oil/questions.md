# Crude oil — Questions

Specification points 4.7–4.18 · Topic 4 Organic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. What is crude oil? (1)
2. Name the fraction used (a) for aircraft fuel (b) for road surfaces (c) for heating and cooking. (3)
3. What is a fuel? (1)
4. Give the conditions used for catalytic cracking. (2)

## 2. Fractional distillation

5. Describe how fractional distillation separates crude oil into fractions. (4)
6. State and explain how boiling point changes going down the column, and give the trend in colour
   and viscosity. (4)

## 3. Combustion and pollution

7. Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane, C₃H₈. (2)
8. Write a balanced equation for the incomplete combustion of methane to form carbon monoxide. (2)
9. Explain why carbon monoxide is poisonous. (2)
10. Explain how oxides of nitrogen form in car engines. (2)
11. Explain how burning some fuels produces sulfur dioxide. (2)
12. Explain how sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen cause acid rain. (2)

## 4. Cracking

13. Complete the equation: C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + ____ (1)
14. Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, is cracked to give hexane, C₆H₁₄, and one alkene only. Write the equation. (2)
15. Explain why oil companies crack some of the fractions from crude oil. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. A mixture of hydrocarbons (1).
2. (a) kerosene (1) (b) bitumen (1) (c) refinery gases (1).
3. A substance that releases heat energy when burned (1).
4. Silica or alumina catalyst (1); 600–700 °C (1).
5. Crude oil heated / vaporised (1); vapour enters the bottom of a column that is hot at the bottom and
   cool at the top (1); vapours rise and cool (1); each fraction condenses where the temperature is
   below its boiling point and is collected at that level (1).
6. Boiling point increases (1); molecules are longer / larger (1) so intermolecular forces are stronger
   (1); fractions become darker and more viscous (1).
7. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O — formulae (1), balancing (1).
8. 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O — formulae (1), balancing (1).
9. It reduces the capacity of the blood (1) to carry oxygen (1).
10. The temperature in the engine is high (1); nitrogen and oxygen from the air react (1).
11. Fuel contains sulfur (compounds) as impurities (1); sulfur is oxidised / burns to form SO₂ (1).
12. SO₂ and NOₓ dissolve in rain water (1); forming sulfuric acid and nitric acid (1).
13. C₄H₈ (accept 2C₂H₄) (1).
14. C₁₂H₂₆ → C₆H₁₄ + C₆H₁₂ — correct alkene formula (1), balanced (1).
15. Supply of long-chain fractions is greater than demand (1); demand for short-chain fractions such as
    gasoline is greater than supply (1); cracking converts long chains into shorter, more useful
    molecules / alkenes for polymers (1).
