# Electrolysis — Questions

Specification points 1.55C–1.60C · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. **All Paper 2 only.** Marks in
brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. What is meant by the terms *anion* and *cation*? (2)
2. Explain why ethanol (a covalent compound) does not conduct electricity. (2)
3. Explain why solid lead(II) bromide does not conduct electricity but molten lead(II) bromide
   does. (2)

## 2. Predicting products and half-equations

4. Name the products at each electrode when these are electrolysed with inert electrodes:
   (a) molten zinc chloride; (b) potassium bromide solution; (c) silver nitrate solution. (6)
5. Write the half-equation for the reaction at the cathode during the electrolysis of copper(II)
   sulfate solution. State whether it is oxidation or reduction and why. (3)
6. Write the half-equation for the formation of chlorine at the anode. (1)
7. Write the half-equation for the formation of oxygen at the anode. (2)
8. In the electrolysis of dilute sulfuric acid, 30 cm³ of hydrogen is collected. State the volume of
   oxygen collected in the same time. (1)

## 3. Practical

9. Describe how to electrolyse sodium chloride solution and identify the products at both
   electrodes. (5)
10. During electrolysis of copper(II) sulfate solution with graphite electrodes, state two
    observations. (2)
11. Explain why molten lead(II) bromide is electrolysed in a fume cupboard. (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Anion — negative ion (1); cation — positive ion (1).
2. It is made of molecules / has no ions (1); no charged particles free to move (1).
3. Solid: ions in fixed positions / cannot move (1); molten: ions free to move (and carry charge)
   (1).
4. (a) zinc at cathode (1), chlorine at anode (1); (b) hydrogen at cathode (1), bromine at anode
   (1); (c) silver at cathode (1), oxygen at anode (1).
5. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (1); reduction (1); copper ions gain electrons (1).
6. 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (1).
7. 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ (species correct (1); balanced (1)).
8. 15 cm³ (1).
9. Solution in cell with inert / graphite electrodes (1); test tubes of solution inverted over the
   electrodes, d.c. supply connected (1); gas at cathode: squeaky pop with lighted splint →
   hydrogen (1); gas at anode: bleaches damp litmus → chlorine (1); solution becomes alkaline /
   test with indicator (1).
10. Pink-brown solid forms on the cathode (1); bubbles at the anode (1); (accept blue colour fades).
11. Bromine (vapour) is toxic (1).
