# Business 4BS1 — Content Style

The subject-specific standard for writing Business revision material in this repository.

**Read before writing anything:**

1. `rules/CONTENT_RULES.md` — the repository-wide content rule.
2. `subjects/Business_4BS1/Specification.pdf` is the scope authority;
   `specification.md` and `specification_map.csv` are the index.
3. `subjects/Business_4BS1/SUBJECT_RULES.md` — paper structure, AO weightings, course distinctions.
4. `subjects/Business_4BS1/EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` — the school's answering method. **All answer structure lives there,
   not here.**
5. `me/papers/Business_4BS1/` — the student's own past papers, if added.

---

## 1. Core rule

> Create the **smallest** revision resource that gives the student everything they need to remember
> and just enough understanding to answer specification-level exam questions.

Target:

**SPECIFICATION-COMPLETE + MINIMUM NECESSARY EXPLANATION + EXAM-USABLE + NO FLUFF**

The student has hundreds of topics across all their subjects. Every unnecessary sentence consumes
revision time and memory.

Do **not** include something merely because it is useful, interesting, normally in a textbook,
a good example, potentially helpful, something that could be analysed, or something that could
appear in a model answer.

Include it only if the student genuinely needs it to know, understand or apply the mapped
specification requirement.

## 2. Revision-first, not teaching-first

the topic PDF is primarily a **revision** resource: what the student needs to remember.

Do not turn a topic into a self-contained textbook chapter. The student already has
`EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` for *how to answer* and a mapped real past-paper bank for *practice*.
Content notes therefore do not pre-write full exam answers or demonstrate every possible
application.

## 3. Three levels

| Level | What it is | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Required** | Explicitly required by the specification (and every sub-bullet under the mapped point). | Always include. |
| **B — Minimum bridging** | The very small amount of explanation needed to understand or apply A. | Include only as much as necessary. |
| **C — Optional / fluff** | Examples, extra context, pre-written analysis, extended evaluation, background, repeated explanation, generic exam advice. | Normally remove. |

The test for any sentence:

> *"If this sentence disappeared, would the student still know and understand enough to answer
> specification-level questions?"*

If yes — remove it.

## 4. What Business notes should and should not contain

**Mainly contain:**

- definitions;
- required lists / factors;
- required formulae;
- required relationships;
- short cause → effect explanations where understanding is necessary;
- concise comparisons;
- the minimum context needed to understand a concept.

**Do not routinely include:**

- full contextual analysis chains;
- several hypothetical business examples;
- separate small-business and large-business examples;
- model answers inside the teaching notes;
- generic evaluation paragraphs;
- advantages/disadvantages written as long prose paragraphs (use the § 4a format instead);
- "how this is examined" sections;
- extensive traps sections;
- repeated definitions;
- repeated rapid-revision material.

## 4a. Advantages and disadvantages — required

The exam constantly asks for benefits, drawbacks and justified choices, so **every key term that is
a method, choice, structure or policy gets its advantages and disadvantages** in `topic.md`.
Examples: types of business organisation, sources of finance, recruitment and training methods,
payment and motivation methods, organisation structures, market research methods, pricing /
promotion / distribution methods, job / batch / flow production, labour- vs capital-intensive,
JIT, Kaizen, robotics/automation, quality control, TQM, growth (economies of scale), globalisation.

Format — kept short and scannable:

- a `::: grid` holding one `::: advantages` and one `::: disadvantages` panel per term, **or** a
  table `Term | Advantages | Disadvantages` when several terms are compared;
- 2–4 points per side, each one line: the benefit or drawback **plus its consequence** (e.g.
  "Lowest cost per unit → can charge competitive prices");
- only points that genuinely belong to that term — no generic filler.

Pure definitions or facts (e.g. *land*, *productivity*, *average total cost*) do not need them.

When several options are compared on the same attributes (e.g. job / batch / flow), give **each
option its own clearly separated column** with its own value in every row — never compress the
options into a single from→to arrow.

## 5. Knowledge vs exam technique vs practice

| File | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Content notes | **What** the student must know. |
| `EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` | **How** to turn that into a 3-, 6-, 9- or 12-mark answer. |
| `me/papers/Business_4BS1/` | **Where** the student practises doing it. |

Do not store the same function in all three places.

*Example.* "Survival means continuing to trade" is required knowledge — it belongs in the notes.
A chain such as *new business → low prices → more customers → cash inflow → suppliers paid →
survives* belongs in question practice, not the core notes.

Exception: where the specification itself requires understanding **why** something changes or
**what effect** it has, include one concise causal explanation.

## 6. Application

Do not pre-write application for every concept. Examples are optional. Use **one short example**
only where it materially improves understanding. Do not add separate Paper 1 / Paper 2 examples to
demonstrate scale unless the concept genuinely behaves differently and that difference is required.

## 7. Analysis

Do not store complete analysis chains for every possible question. Give only the underlying
relationship the student needs in order to build one.

Instead of *price cut → sales rise → market share rises → bargaining power rises → supplier prices
fall → unit costs fall → profit rises*, the note usually needs only:

> Lower prices may increase sales/market share, but reduce profit per unit.

The student develops the chain in the exam using `EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md`.

## 8. Evaluation

Do not pre-write evaluation unless the trade-off itself is required knowledge. Evaluation technique
lives in `EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md`. The notes supply the facts and relationships needed to make the
judgement — nothing more.

## 9. Definitions

Short and exam-accurate, in the awarding body's own wording where it exists. **One** full definition
per term, stated once. Do not follow a definition with a paragraph unless the explanation is
genuinely necessary.

## 10. Tables

Use a table when it **compresses** information. Prefer:

`Term | Meaning | Essential consequence`  or  `Factor | What it does`

Avoid tables carrying several examples, several applications, several consequences, or prose
duplicated from elsewhere in the topic.

## 11. Calculations

formula → one concise worked example if necessary → interpretation only if assessable.

- Show formula, substitution, answer, unit/%. The substitution is a mark in its own right.
- The six exam-provided formulae are in `EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md`; reproduce them exactly and do not
  claim that break-even or cash-flow formulae are provided.
- Keep the currency of the source data (£, €, $, AED, MYR, Ks, TRY, Rupees, DKK, SGD); never
  silently convert.
- Do not over-explain arithmetic.

## 12. Visuals

Follow `AGENTS.md` § Visual rule: plan visuals before writing. Anything with a shape gets a
diagram, not a table or bullet list — for example the break-even chart, cash-flow forecast chain,
product life cycle, organisation structures, distribution channels, supply and demand, the
recruitment process, motivation models, economies/diseconomies of scale. Tables only for
comparisons (e.g. sources of finance). Cut the prose a diagram replaces. No decorative visuals.

## 13. Scope discipline

- Cover every sub-bullet of the mapped specification point — the sub-bullets are the assessable
  list. Do not add items the specification does not name.
- Do not import material from Cambridge 0450/0986 or Edexcel UK 1BS0.
- No enrichment frameworks (Porter, Ansoff, extended motivation theory, A-level finance).
- The past-paper bank shows what is asked and how; it never expands the syllabus.
- Past-paper questions stay in `me/papers/Business_4BS1/`. Never merge one into `questions.md` with an
  invented answer, and never label an AI answer as a mark scheme.

## 14. File roles

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `topic.md` | Complete source of required knowledge — still concise. A short specification-point reference only; the full mapping already exists in `subjects/Business_4BS1/`. Do not reproduce official specification wording at the top. |
| `summary.md` | Ultra-compressed last-minute revision. Dramatically shorter than `topic.md`. |
| `questions.md` | Limited. Fill genuine practice gaps, test essential recall/calculation, and point to the mapped real past-paper questions. Do not generate 20–30 generic questions when real Pearson questions already exist. |
| topic PDF | `notes/Business_4BS1/NN_<Topic>.pdf` — the student's main revision resource: dense, clean, fast to revise. Built from `topic.md` only. |

### Topic PDF

Normally include:

- topic title / minimal specification reference;
- essential definitions;
- required knowledge;
- concise relationships and explanations;
- formulae if needed;
- one compact "remember" / traps area, only if useful.

Normally do **not** include:

- full official-specification tables;
- large "by the end you must be able to" boxes;
- long exam-language sections;
- checklists, self-tests, answers or marking pages;
- multiple contextual examples;
- a rapid-revision restatement of material already concise enough.

Practice lives in `questions.md` and the mapped real past-paper questions; the PDF contains none.

## 15. Length

No page count. But question every page aggressively. A small Business specification topic may
genuinely need only **1–3 pages**. Do not let a fixed booklet template inflate a small topic to 5–8
pages.

The target is not a number. It is:

> **Nothing important missing, nothing unnecessary present.**

## 16. Final test

Before calling a Business pack done, run `subjects/Business_4BS1/QA_CHECKLIST.md`, then inspect every major block and
ask:

1. Is this required by the specification?
2. If not, is it genuinely necessary to understand or apply something required?
3. Is it repeated somewhere else?
4. Does it belong instead in `EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` or past-paper practice?
5. Could the student still get full marks without memorising it?

If the answer to #5 is yes and it is not necessary bridging explanation — remove it.
