## Questions

1. **[2]** *Define* the term *enhanced greenhouse effect*.
2. **[3]** *Describe* how ice cores provide evidence of past climate.
3. **[3]** Use Figure 1 in the notes (global temperature). *Describe* the changes in global temperature
   between 1880 and 2024. Use figures in your answer.
4. **[2]** *Give* **two** limitations of historic writing and paintings as evidence of climate change.
5. **[4]** *Explain* how orbital changes (Milankovitch cycles) can cause climate change.
6. **[3]** *Explain* how a large volcanic eruption can affect global temperature.
7. **[2]** *Suggest* why sunspot activity cannot explain the warming of the last 40 years.
8. **[5]** *Explain* how human activities cause the enhanced greenhouse effect.
9. **[6]** "Recent climate change is mainly caused by natural factors." How far do you agree? Use
   evidence to *justify* your answer.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Guidance written for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. Human activity adds extra greenhouse gases (1) → more infrared/heat is trapped and the Earth warms (1).
2. Any three: drilled from ice sheets (Antarctica/Greenland); annual layers counted to date the ice;
   trapped air bubbles show past CO₂; isotopes/chemistry of the ice show past temperature; record goes
   back ~800 000 years; shows CO₂ and temperature rising and falling together.
3. Overall rise (1); little change/slight fall to ~1910, rise to ~1940, fairly flat to ~1975 (1);
   rapid rise since ~1980 to ~+1.3–1.55 °C by 2024 (1). Credit accurate figures and dates.
4. Any two: descriptive not measured; only where records were kept; may exaggerate; local, may not
   represent the globe.
5. Eccentricity: orbit changes from nearly circular to more elliptical (~100 000 yr), changing the
   difference in solar energy received (1). Tilt varies 22.1°–24.5° (~41 000 yr): more tilt → more
   extreme seasons (1). Precession: wobble (~26 000 yr) changes which hemisphere has summer when Earth
   is closest to the Sun (1). Together change the amount/distribution of sunlight → glacials and
   interglacials (1).
6. Eruption sends sulphur dioxide/ash into the stratosphere (1); forms aerosols that spread around the
   globe (1); reflect incoming sunlight so the surface cools, for 1–3 years (1). Example credit:
   Pinatubo 1991, ~0.5 °C cooler.
7. Solar output has not increased since the 1980s (1) while temperatures have risen fast (1); the effect
   of the 11-year cycle is small (1).
8. Named activity + gas + mechanism, up to 5: burning fossil fuels releases CO₂; deforestation releases
   stored carbon and removes a carbon sink; agriculture releases methane (cattle, rice) and nitrous oxide
   (fertiliser); methane is a stronger greenhouse gas; gases absorb long-wave/infrared radiation from the
   surface and re-emit it back down; so more heat is kept → global warming; CO₂ 280 → ~424 ppm.
9. Levels-style. Top answers: natural factors do change climate (Milankovitch → ice ages; sunspots →
   Little Ice Age; volcanoes → short cooling) **but** cannot explain recent warming — orbital change too
   slow, solar output flat since 1980s, volcanoes cool; rapid rise since 1980 matches rising CO₂
   (280 → ~424 ppm, higher than any time in 800 000 years). Clear judgement: mostly disagree.
