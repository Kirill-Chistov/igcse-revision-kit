# Feeding relationships — Questions

Specification points 4.6–4.9. Marking guidance is this repository's own, not an official Pearson
mark scheme. No past papers are stored for Biology yet.

Food web for Q5–Q6: grass → rabbit, grass → grasshopper, grasshopper → frog, grasshopper → blue tit,
oak → caterpillar, caterpillar → blue tit, rabbit → fox, rabbit → hawk, frog → hawk,
blue tit → hawk.

## 1. Recall

1. What is a producer? (1)
2. State the role of decomposers in an ecosystem. (2)
3. What does each bar in a pyramid of biomass represent? (1)

## 2. Understanding

4. Explain why a pyramid of numbers can have a narrow bottom bar but a pyramid of energy cannot. (3)
5. Name the trophic level(s) of the hawk. (2)
6. Blue tits are removed. Suggest and explain the effect on the caterpillar population and on the
   hawk population. (4)

## 3. Application

7. Producers in a pond contain 36 000 kJ/m²/year; primary consumers contain 3 240 kJ/m²/year.
   Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer. (2)
8. Explain why only about 10% of the energy in one trophic level is passed on to the next. (4)
9. Explain why food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels. (2)
10. Explain the difference between how energy and how carbon move through an ecosystem. (3)

## Marking guidance

1. An organism that makes its own food (organic substances) by photosynthesis (1).
2. Break down dead organisms and waste (faeces, urine) (1); release/recycle mineral ions and CO₂ for
   producers to reuse (1).
3. The dry mass (of organisms) at that trophic level per unit area (1).
4. Pyramid of numbers counts individuals regardless of size (1); one large producer (e.g. a tree) can
   feed many consumers (1); energy is lost at every level, so each level must have less energy than
   the one below (1).
5. Secondary consumer (eats rabbits) (1); tertiary consumer (eats frogs / blue tits) (1).
6. Caterpillars increase (1) — fewer predators (1). Hawks may decrease (1) — less food; or stay the
   same because they eat more frogs/rabbits instead (1). Any reasoned prediction credited.
7. 3 240 ÷ 36 000 × 100 (1) = 9% (1).
8. Not all of the organism is eaten, e.g. roots/bones (1); some food is not digested and is egested
   in faeces (1); energy lost in excretion/urea (1); most used in respiration, for movement/keeping
   warm, and lost as heat (1). Max 4.
9. Energy is lost at every level (1); after four or five transfers too little energy remains to
   support another population (1).
10. Energy flows in one direction / is not recycled (1) and is finally lost as heat (1); carbon is
    recycled — returned to the air by respiration/decomposition and reused in photosynthesis (1).
