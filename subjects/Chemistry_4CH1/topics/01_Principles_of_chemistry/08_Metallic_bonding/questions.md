# Metallic bonding — Questions

Specification points 1.52C–1.54C · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. **All Paper 2 only.** Marks
in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

1. Draw a labelled diagram to show the structure of a metal. (2)
2. Describe metallic bonding. (2)
3. Explain why copper conducts electricity. (2)
4. Explain why aluminium is malleable. (2)
5. Explain why iron has a high melting point. (2)
6. Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity but solid sodium does. Explain the
   difference. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Regular arrangement / rows of positive ions labelled (1); delocalised electrons between them,
   labelled (1).
2. (Strong electrostatic) attraction (1) between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons (1).
3. Delocalised electrons (1) can move through the structure / carry charge (1).
4. Layers of ions / atoms (1) can slide over each other (without the bonding breaking) (1).
5. Strong (electrostatic) attractions between positive ions and delocalised electrons (1); lots of
   energy needed to overcome them (1).
6. In sodium chloride the ions are fixed in the lattice / cannot move (1); there are no free
   electrons (1); sodium has delocalised electrons that can move (1).
