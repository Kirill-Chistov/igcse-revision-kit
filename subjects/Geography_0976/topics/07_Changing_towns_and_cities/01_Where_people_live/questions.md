## Answers and marking guidance

**AI-written for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.** One mark per creditable
point unless stated.

1. The increase in the proportion/percentage (1) of a country's population living in urban areas (1).
2. Urban growth is the increase in the **number** of people living in urban areas (1); urbanisation is
   the increase in the **proportion** (1).
3. The movement of people out of cities to smaller towns and rural areas (1).
4. Push: a reason that makes people want to leave the place where they live (1). Pull: a reason that
   attracts people to a new place (1).
5. Rural-to-urban migration (1); natural increase (1).
6. Growth rate falls as income rises (1); low-income countries grow about five times as fast as
   high-income (4.0% vs 0.8%) (1); the big fall is between lower-middle and upper-middle income — upper
   and high income are almost the same (1). Must use figures for full marks.
7. Few people are left in rural areas to move to cities (1); growth levels off near a ceiling, so it
   depends only on low natural increase / migration (1).
8. Any two, each explained (2 + 2): large rural population still to move (1) because only about a third
   urban (1); high birth rates / young population (1) so cities grow from natural increase (1);
   industrialisation puts new jobs in cities (1) attracting migrants (1); rural push such as land
   shortage or drought (1) forces people off the land (1).
9. People move out to small towns and villages (1), helped by better roads, commuting and home-working
   (1), so the core city grows slowly or loses population (1).
10. International migrants settle mainly in large cities (1); jobs in services continue to attract people
    / some natural increase continues (1).
11. Social: few schools / clinics / no piped water. Economic: low farm incomes / land shortage /
    mechanisation. Environmental: drought / desertification / soil erosion / floods. Political:
    conflict / persecution / little government spending in rural areas. (1 each)
12. Machines replace farm labourers (1), so fewer jobs are available in rural areas (1); unemployed
    workers move to the city to find work (1).
13. Migrants are mostly young adults of child-bearing age (1), so the birth rate in the city is high (1);
    better health care and clean water lower the death rate (1).
14. Migrants expect jobs and homes (1) but there are too few formal jobs and homes, so many end up in the
    informal sector / unplanned settlements (1).
15. Levels (6 marks): **L1 (1–2)** lists push/pull factors. **L2 (3–4)** explains factors from at least
    two categories, e.g. land shortage pushes people off farms, jobs pull them to cities.
    **L3 (5–6)** explains factors from all four categories, links push and pull, and includes natural
    increase as the second cause.
16. Levels (6 marks). For: LICs have high birth rates and young migrants, so cities grow from within;
    natural increase now adds as much as migration in many LIC cities. Against: share already urban,
    economic change, rural push, counter-urbanisation and policy all matter; high-income cities grow
    mainly by migration. **L3** needs a supported judgement, e.g. natural increase explains the
    *continuation* of fast growth, but stage of development explains the *difference* between countries.
