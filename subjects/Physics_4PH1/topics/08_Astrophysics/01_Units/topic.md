## 1. The units (8.1)

| Unit | Symbol | Used for in this topic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kilogram | kg | mass of a planet, moon, star or satellite |
| metre | m | orbital radius *r*, distances; wavelength λ in red-shift |
| metre/second | m/s | orbital speed *v*; velocity of a galaxy *v*; speed of light *c* |
| metre/second² | m/s² | acceleration of free fall (numerically equal to *g*) |
| newton | N | gravitational force, weight |
| second | s | time period *T* of an orbit |
| newton/kilogram | N/kg | gravitational field strength *g* |

1 N/kg is the same as 1 m/s²: a place where *g* = 10 N/kg is also a place where falling objects
accelerate at 10 m/s².

## 2. Conversions

Astronomical data are usually given in km, days or years. Convert to m and s **before** using
*v* = 2π*r*/*T*, and use standard form.

| Given as | Convert | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| km | × 10³ → m | 384 000 km = 3.84 × 10⁸ m |
| km/s | × 10³ → m/s | 30 km/s = 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s |
| hours | × 3600 → s | 1.5 h = 5400 s |
| days | × 86 400 → s | 27.3 days = 2.36 × 10⁶ s |
| years | × 365 × 86 400 → s | 1 year = 3.15 × 10⁷ s |

::: traps
| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| Substituting *r* in km or *T* in days into *v* = 2π*r*/*T*. | Convert to m and s first; the answer is then in m/s. |
| Giving *g* in newtons. | *g* is in **N/kg**; weight is in N. |
:::
