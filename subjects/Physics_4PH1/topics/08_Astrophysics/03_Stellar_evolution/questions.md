# Stellar evolution — Questions

Specification points 8.7–8.12P · Topic 8 Astrophysics. Marks in brackets.
Questions marked **P2** cover Paper 2 only content.

> **All questions, answers and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository and
> are not official Pearson questions or mark schemes.**

## 1. Recall

1. State what the colour of a star tells you about the star. (1)
2. Put these stars in order from hottest to coolest surface: yellow, red, blue, white. (1)
3. Name the stage in which the Sun is now, and the two stages it will pass through next. (3)
4. **P2** State what is meant by the absolute magnitude of a star. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

5. Explain why a main-sequence star stays the same size for billions of years. (3)
6. Describe how a star forms from a nebula and becomes a main-sequence star. (3)
7. Describe the evolution of a star of similar mass to the Sun after it leaves the main sequence. (3)
8. Describe the evolution of a star with a mass much larger than the Sun, after the main sequence. (3)

## 3. Application

9. **P2** Star A has an absolute magnitude of −2 and star B of +8. State which star is brighter
   and explain why the comparison is fair even though the stars are different distances from the
   Earth. (2)
10. **P2** A star is very hot but has a very dim absolute magnitude. Name the type of star and
    state where it appears on the HR diagram. (2)

## 4. Exam-style

11. **P2 [4 marks]** *Draw* a labelled sketch of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram. Label both axes
    and show the positions of the main sequence, red giants, supergiants and white dwarfs.

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. Its surface temperature (1).
2. blue, white, yellow, red (1).
3. Main sequence (1); red giant (1); white dwarf (1).
4. The brightness a star would have (1) if it were at a standard distance (10 parsecs) from the Earth (1).
5. Gravity pulls the star's material inwards (1); the pressure of the hot gas / energy from fusion
   pushes outwards (1); the two forces balance (1).
6. Gravity pulls the dust and gas together (1); the cloud / protostar heats up (1); hydrogen nuclei
   start to fuse into helium, releasing energy (1).
7. Hydrogen in the core runs out (1); the star swells and cools into a red giant (1); outer layers
   drift away leaving a white dwarf (1).
8. Becomes a red supergiant (1); explodes as a supernova (1); leaves a neutron star or, if massive
   enough, a black hole (1).
9. Star A — lower (more negative) magnitude means brighter (1); absolute magnitude compares all
   stars at the same standard distance, so distance does not affect it (1).
10. White dwarf (1); bottom left (1).
11. Vertical axis absolute magnitude with brighter / negative values at the top (1); horizontal axis
    surface temperature decreasing to the right (1); main sequence as a diagonal from top left to
    bottom right (1); red giants above the main sequence on the right, supergiants across the top
    and white dwarfs bottom left (1).
