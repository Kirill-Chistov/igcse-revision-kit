# Covalent bonding — Summary

Specification points 1.44–1.51 · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry.

## Essential facts

- **Covalent bond**: shared pair of electrons; electrostatic attraction between the shared pair and
  the nuclei of both atoms. Between non-metals.
- Bonds formed: H, halogens 1 · O 2 · N 3 · C 4. O₂ has a double bond, N₂ a triple bond, CO₂ two
  double bonds, C₂H₄ a C=C.
- Dot-and-cross: shared pairs in the overlap (one electron from each atom), lone pairs outside,
  full outer shells.
- **Simple molecular**: weak intermolecular forces → little energy to overcome → low mp/bp (gases,
  liquids, low-mp solids). Covalent bonds not broken on melting/boiling.
- Higher Mr → stronger intermolecular forces → higher mp/bp (Cl₂ gas, Br₂ liquid, I₂ solid).
- **Giant covalent**: many strong covalent bonds must be broken → high mp/bp.
- **Diamond**: 4 bonds per C, rigid → very hard; no free electrons → no conduction.
- **Graphite**: 3 bonds per C, layers slide → soft; delocalised electrons → conducts.
- **C₆₀**: simple molecule → soft, low mp; no conduction between molecules.
- Covalent compounds do not usually conduct.

## Common traps

- Melting a simple molecular substance does not break covalent bonds.
- Graphite's bonds within layers are strong.
