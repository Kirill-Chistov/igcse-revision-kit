# States of matter — Questions

Specification points 1.1–1.7C · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. Questions 9–14 are
**Paper 2 only**. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Describe the arrangement and movement of the particles in a liquid. (2)
2. Name the change of state from gas to liquid, and from solid directly to gas. (2)
3. Define *saturated solution*. (1)
4. Define *diffusion*. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

5. Explain, in terms of particles and energy, what happens when a solid melts. (3)
6. Give two differences between boiling and evaporation. (2)
7. A crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is dissolved in water and the solution diluted many times.
   The colour gets paler but is still visible. Explain what this shows about particles. (2)
8. Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia is put in one end of a long tube and cotton wool
   soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid in the other. A white ring forms nearer the acid end.
   Explain why. (3)

## 3. Paper 2 — solubility

9. Define *solubility*, including its unit. (2)
10. Use Figure 3 of the notes: state the solubility of potassium nitrate at 40 °C. (1)
11. A saturated solution of potassium nitrate in 100 g of water is cooled from 60 °C to 20 °C.
    Calculate the mass of crystals that forms. (2)
12. The same solution as in 11 but in 50 g of water. Calculate the mass of crystals formed. (2)
13. Describe how to find the solubility of a salt in water at 50 °C. Include the measurements taken
    and how the result is calculated. (5)
14. In an experiment: basin 25.20 g, basin + saturated solution 61.20 g, basin + dry salt 34.20 g.
    Calculate the solubility in g per 100 g of water. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Irregular / random arrangement, particles touching (1); moving around / sliding past each
   other (1).
2. Condensing (1); sublimation (1).
3. A solution in which no more solute can dissolve at that temperature (1).
4. Net movement of particles from a higher to a lower concentration (1); due to random movement of
   particles (1).
5. Particles gain (kinetic) energy / vibrate more (1); overcome the forces of attraction (1); leave
   fixed positions but stay close together / touching and can move past each other (1).
6. Boiling happens throughout the liquid, evaporation only at the surface (1); boiling at the
   boiling point only, evaporation at any temperature below it (1).
7. The crystal contains a very large number of particles (1); the particles are very small / spread
   out through the water (1).
8. Ammonia and hydrogen chloride particles diffuse along the tube and react where they meet (1);
   ammonia particles are lighter / lower Mr (1); so move faster and travel further in the same time
   (1).
9. Mass of solute that dissolves in 100 g of solvent to form a saturated solution at a stated
   temperature (1); g per 100 g of solvent (1).
10. About 64 g per 100 g water (1) (accept 62–66).
11. 110 − 32 (1) = 78 g (1).
12. 78 ÷ 2 (1) = 39 g (1).
13. Dissolve salt in water at 50 °C (water bath) until excess solid remains / saturated (1); weigh
    empty evaporating basin, then with some saturated solution (1); evaporate the water / heat (1);
    reweigh, heating to constant mass (1); solubility = mass of solid ÷ mass of water × 100 (1).
14. Salt = 9.00 g, water = 27.00 g (1); 9.00 ÷ 27.00 × 100 (1) = 33.3 g per 100 g water (1).
