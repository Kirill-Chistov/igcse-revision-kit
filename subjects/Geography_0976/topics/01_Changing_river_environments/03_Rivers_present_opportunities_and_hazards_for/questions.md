## Answers and marking guidance

*AI-generated for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme. One mark per creditable
point unless stated; longer answers level-marked.*

1. Any two, each stated (1) and explained (1): fertile alluvium for farming; flat land easy to build
   on; water supply for drinking/irrigation; transport/trade along the river.
2. Any two, each described (1+1): pollution spreading disease; bank erosion destroying land/buildings;
   water-related disease such as malaria from standing water.
3. The time between peak rainfall and peak discharge (1).
4. Any two, each named (1) and explained (1): prolonged rain saturates soil → overland flow; intense
   rain exceeds infiltration; snowmelt releases a lot of water quickly; impermeable rock/frozen
   ground prevents infiltration; steep slopes speed run-off.
5. Impermeable surfaces stop infiltration (1); more overland flow (1); drains carry water quickly to
   the river, shortening lag time / raising peak discharge (1).
6. A's lag time is 14 hours shorter (1) and its peak discharge higher (1); A floods, B does not
   (1). Reasons, up to 2: urban/impermeable surfaces; deforestation; steep slopes; saturated
   ground in A versus forest, permeable rock, gentle slopes in B.
7. Level-marked. Top level needs all three categories with developed points: e.g. deaths and
   homelessness, disease from contaminated water; damage to property and infrastructure, crops lost,
   repair costs; soil erosion, pollution spread, habitats damaged.
8. LIC: poorer housing, fewer warnings, less emergency support/health care → more deaths (2). HIC:
   more valuable property and infrastructure → higher economic losses; better warnings and defences →
   fewer deaths (2).
9. Fertile silt deposited on farmland (1); groundwater recharged / wetlands renewed (1).
10. Strategy: a plan, policy or agreement (1). Technique: the practical method used (1).
11. The reservoir stores flood water (1) and releases it slowly, reducing peak discharge downstream (1).
12. Moves water downstream faster, increasing flood risk further down (1+1); or destroys habitats (1)
    as the natural bed and banks are removed (1).
13. Trees intercept rain and increase infiltration, lengthening lag time (1); treats the cause —
    run-off — not the symptom (1); cheap, long-lasting, benefits wildlife and reduces soil erosion (1).
14. Level-marked, 7. Soft: treats the cause, cheap to maintain, environmental benefits — but slow,
    needs land, less effective in extreme floods. Hard: immediate, protects high-value areas — but
    costly, moves flooding downstream, environmental damage. Top level: a supported judgement, e.g.
    soft is more sustainable, but hard defences are still justified where valuable property is at risk,
    so a combined approach works best.
15. Any three: untreated sewage; fertiliser/pesticide run-off; industrial chemicals or heavy metals;
    mining waste; solid waste/plastic dumping; oil from urban run-off.
16. Nitrates/phosphates enter the river (1); algal bloom blocks light, plants die (1); bacteria
    decompose them, using oxygen (1); dissolved oxygen falls and fish suffocate (1).
17. Any two, each explained: higher cost of water treatment; fishing income lost; tourism lost;
    contaminated crops cannot be sold; health costs/lost working days.
18. Level-marked, 7. Strategies (laws, permits, fines, monitoring, international agreements) and
    techniques (sewage treatment, effluent treatment, buffer strips, less fertiliser, waste collection,
    clean-ups) with strengths and weaknesses. Top level: a judgement — prevention at source is most
    effective and sustainable, but depends on funding and enforcement.
19. Nairobi River (from class notes): untreated sewage — E. coli up to 1,000,000 units per 100 ml;
    industrial waste — 145 factories and slaughterhouses identified in October 2024, heavy metals
    (lead, copper, chromium, zinc, manganese) above WHO and NEMA limits; solid waste — 6,000 tonnes
    removed from a 2.5 km stretch; rapid urbanisation — about 5.5 million people at about 6,247 per
    km². 1 per cause, 1 per supporting figure.
20. Health: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, as people wash, clean and irrigate with the water (E. coli);
    ecosystem: low diversity of river-bed organisms, pollution-tolerant groups dominate; eutrophication:
    algae 100 at Ondiri Swamp → 947 at Ruai; contaminated urban farming → illness.
21. Level-marked, 7 (researched material): Nairobi Rivers Commission (October 2024); regeneration
    programme for ~175 km; Climate WorX youth jobs; NEMA enforcement of the 145 polluters; sewer project
    2019–2023, coverage 48% → 55%; Michuki Park with 6,357 trees and community management. Evaluation:
    sewers treat the cause; the park shows success where maintained; the 1999–2008 programme failed;
    depends on funding, maintenance and enforcement. Top level: a sustainability judgement.
