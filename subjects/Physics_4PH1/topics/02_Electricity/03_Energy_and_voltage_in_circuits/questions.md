# Energy and voltage in circuits — Questions

Specification points 2.7–2.21 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1.

> These questions and the marking guidance are **written for this repository by AI**. They are not
> official Pearson questions and the guidance is not an official mark scheme.

## 1. Charge, current, voltage and energy (2.14–2.16, 2.20, 2.21)

1. State what is meant by an electric current. (1)
2. Name the charged particles that move in a copper wire when there is a current, and state their charge. (2)
3. A current of 0.25 A flows for 3.0 minutes. Calculate the charge that passes. (3)
4. State what is meant by a voltage of 6 V across a lamp, in terms of energy and charge. (2)
5. 120 C of charge passes through a 230 V heater. Calculate the energy transferred. (2)

## 2. Resistance and I–V graphs (2.9, 2.10, 2.13)

6. State the equation linking voltage, current and resistance. (1)
7. There is a current of 0.15 A in a 40 Ω resistor. Calculate the voltage across it. (2)
8. Describe the effect on the current of increasing the resistance in a circuit, if the voltage stays the same. (1)
9. Sketch the current–voltage graph of a filament lamp and explain its shape. (4)
10. Describe how the current in a diode depends on the voltage across it. (3)
11. Describe an experiment to find how the current in a resistor varies with the voltage across it. Include a circuit diagram. (6)

## 3. LDRs, thermistors, lamps and LEDs (2.11, 2.12)

12. Describe how the resistance of an LDR changes as the light falling on it gets brighter. (1)
13. A thermistor is in series with an ammeter and a battery. Describe and explain what happens to the ammeter reading as the thermistor is heated. (2)
14. State how an LED can show that a current is flowing, and one condition for it to light. (2)

## 4. Series and parallel circuits (2.7, 2.8, 2.17–2.19)

15. A 3.0 Ω and a 9.0 Ω resistor are connected in series to a 6.0 V battery. Calculate (a) the total resistance, (b) the current, (c) the voltage across each resistor. (5)
16. A lamp and a 15 Ω resistor are in series with a 12 V supply. The current is 0.40 A. Calculate the resistance of the lamp. (3)
17. Two lamps are in series with a battery. A third identical lamp is added in series. Explain what happens to the brightness of the lamps. (2)
18. The current into a junction is 1.2 A. One branch carries 0.45 A. State the current in the other branch and explain why. (3)
19. Two resistors are connected in parallel across a 9.0 V battery. State the voltage across each resistor. (1)
20. Explain why lamps in a house are connected in parallel rather than in series. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. The rate of flow of charge (1).
2. Electrons (1); negative (1).
3. *t* = 180 s (1); *Q* = 0.25 × 180 (1) = 45 C (1).
4. 6 J of energy is transferred (1) for each coulomb of charge passing through the lamp (1).
5. *E* = 120 × 230 (1) = 27 600 J (1).
6. voltage = current × resistance (1).
7. *V* = 0.15 × 40 (1) = 6.0 V (1).
8. The current decreases (1).
9. Curve through the origin that gets less steep at higher voltages, same shape for negative values (1); as the current increases the filament gets hotter (1); the ions vibrate more, so the electrons collide with them more (1); the resistance increases, so the current rises less for each extra volt (1).
10. No current when the voltage is reversed / very high resistance in reverse (1); in the forward direction, little or no current until about 0.6 V (1); then the current increases rapidly (1).
11. Circuit: supply, variable resistor, ammeter and resistor in series, voltmeter across the resistor (2). Adjust the variable resistor to change the voltage (1); record voltage and current (1); take at least six pairs of readings, repeat and take means, and reverse the supply for negative values (1); keep the temperature constant with small currents / switching off between readings, and plot current against voltage (1).
12. The resistance decreases (1).
13. The ammeter reading increases (1); the resistance of the thermistor decreases as it gets hotter (1).
14. It lights only when there is a current through it (1); the current must be in its forward direction (1).
15. (a) 3.0 + 9.0 = 12 Ω (1); (b) *I* = 6.0 ÷ 12 (1) = 0.50 A (1); (c) 0.50 × 3.0 = 1.5 V (1); 0.50 × 9.0 = 4.5 V (1).
16. Voltage across resistor = 0.40 × 15 = 6.0 V (1); voltage across lamp = 12 − 6.0 = 6.0 V (1); *R* = 6.0 ÷ 0.40 = 15 Ω (1).
17. All three lamps are dimmer (1); total resistance increases so the current decreases / the supply voltage is shared among more lamps (1).
18. 0.75 A (1); current is conserved at a junction (1); charge is not used up or stored there, so the charge flowing in per second equals the charge flowing out per second (1).
19. 9.0 V (1).
20. Each lamp gets the full mains voltage / full brightness (1); each lamp can be switched on and off independently (1); if one lamp fails the others stay on (1).
