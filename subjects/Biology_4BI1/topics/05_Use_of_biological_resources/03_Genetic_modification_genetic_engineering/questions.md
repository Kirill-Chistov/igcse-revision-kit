# Genetic modification — Questions

**Specification points 5.12–5.16** · Papers 1 and 2

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme. No
Biology past papers are stored in the repository yet.

## Questions

1. **[1 mark]** State what is meant by the term *transgenic*.
2. **[2 marks]** State the role of (a) restriction enzymes, (b) DNA ligase.
3. **[2 marks]** *Explain* why the same restriction enzyme is used to cut the human DNA and the
   plasmid.
4. **[2 marks]** *Explain* what is meant by a vector, and give two examples.
5. **[6 marks]** *Describe* how genetically modified bacteria are used to produce large amounts of
   human insulin.
6. **[3 marks]** A crop plant has been modified to be resistant to a herbicide. *Explain* how this
   increases crop yield.
7. **[2 marks]** Give two concerns about growing genetically modified crops.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Transfer of genetic material / a gene from one species to a different species (1).
2. (a) Cut DNA at specific base sequences / sites (1). (b) Joins pieces of DNA together (1).
3. Produces matching / complementary sticky ends (1), so the gene and plasmid can join / base-pair
   (1).
4. Something that carries (recombinant) DNA into another cell (1); plasmids and viruses (1).
5. Insulin gene cut from human DNA with a restriction enzyme (1); plasmid cut with the same
   restriction enzyme (1); ligase joins gene into plasmid / forms recombinant plasmid (1); plasmid
   inserted into bacterium (1); bacteria grown in a fermenter, where they multiply rapidly (1);
   bacteria produce insulin, which is extracted and purified (1).
6. Herbicide can be sprayed to kill weeds without killing the crop (1); less competition (1) for
   light / water / minerals, so more photosynthesis and growth (1).
7. Any two: gene transferred to wild plants / herbicide-resistant weeds; harm to non-target insects
   / food chains; unknown long-term effects on health; seeds expensive.
