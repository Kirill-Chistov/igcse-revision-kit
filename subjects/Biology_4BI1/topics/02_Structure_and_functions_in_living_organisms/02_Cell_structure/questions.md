# Cell structure — Questions

**Specification points 2.2–2.6B** · questions marked (P2) are Paper 2 only

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Recall

1. State the function of (a) ribosomes, (b) mitochondria, (c) the nucleus.
2. Name three structures found in plant cells but not in animal cells.
3. What is the cell wall of a plant made of?
4. (P2) Define *stem cell*.

## Understanding and application

5. A cell has a nucleus, mitochondria and a cell wall but no chloroplasts. Suggest where in a
   plant it came from, and explain your answer.
6. Explain why a plant cell placed in water does not burst.
7. Muscle cells contain many more mitochondria than skin cells. Suggest why.
8. (P2) Explain why cell differentiation is important in the development of a multicellular
   organism.
9. (P2) Compare embryonic and adult stem cells.

## Exam-style

10. **[4 marks]** *Describe* the similarities and differences in structure between a plant cell and
    an animal cell.
11. (P2) **[6 marks]** *Discuss* the advantages and disadvantages of using stem cells to treat
    disease.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. (a) protein synthesis; (b) aerobic respiration / release energy; (c) controls the cell's
   activities / contains genetic material.
2. Cell wall, chloroplasts, large permanent vacuole.
3. Cellulose.
4. An unspecialised cell that can divide to form more stem cells (1) and can differentiate into
   specialised cells (1).
5. Root (or any non-green part) (1); it is a plant cell (cell wall) but has no chloroplasts, so it
   receives no light / does not photosynthesise (1).
6. The cellulose cell wall is strong (1) and resists the cell expanding further once it is full of
   water (1).
7. Muscle contraction needs a lot of energy (1); mitochondria are the site of aerobic
   respiration, which releases energy (1).
8. Multicellular organisms need cells with different functions (1); differentiation produces
   specialised cells with structures suited to one function (1), so each function is carried out
   efficiently (1); similar specialised cells form tissues and organs (1).
9. Embryonic: from embryos; can form any cell type (1). Adult: from tissues such as bone marrow;
   form only a limited range of cells (1).
10. Both have nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ribosomes (max 2). Only plant cells
    have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts, a large permanent vacuole (max 2).
11. Advantages (max 3): replace damaged/diseased cells; treat currently incurable conditions
    (e.g. paralysis, type 1 diabetes); embryonic cells form any cell type; patient's own cells not
    rejected; can be grown in large numbers. Disadvantages (max 3): embryos destroyed — ethical
    objections; donor cells rejected / need immunosuppressant drugs; risk of tumours from
    uncontrolled division; risk of viral infection; adult cells limited range. Full marks need
    points on both sides.
