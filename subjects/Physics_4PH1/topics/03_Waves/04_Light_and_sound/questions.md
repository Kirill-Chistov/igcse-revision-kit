## 1. Recall

1. State the law of reflection.
2. Define critical angle.
3. State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection.
4. Write the two equations involving refractive index. Are they given in the exam?

## 2. Understanding and ray diagrams

5. Draw a ray diagram for a ray reflecting from a plane mirror at *i* = 35°. Label the normal and
   both angles.
6. Draw a ray passing through a rectangular glass block at an angle. Describe what happens at each
   surface.
7. In the semi-circular block practical, why is the ray aimed at the centre of the flat face?

## 3. Application

8. Light in air hits water at *i* = 50° and refracts at *r* = 35°. Calculate the refractive index.
9. Diamond has *n* = 2.4. Calculate its critical angle.
10. A material has critical angle 38°. Calculate its refractive index.
11. Glass has *n* = 1.5. A ray in air hits it at 60°. Calculate the angle of refraction.
12. Describe how light is kept inside an optical fibre.

## 4. Paper 2 only

13. State the range of human hearing.
14. A student stands 85 m from a wall. She times 20 claps, each in time with the echo of the last:
    total time 10.0 s. Calculate the speed of sound.
15. Explain why timing 20 echoes is better than timing one.
16. An oscilloscope time-base is 2 ms/div. Three complete waves span 6 divisions. Calculate the
    frequency.
17. The sound is made louder and higher in pitch. Describe two changes to the trace.
18. Describe how a microphone and oscilloscope display a sound wave.

## 5. Exam-style prompts

19. **[6 marks]** *Describe* an experiment to find the refractive index of a glass block.
20. **[3 marks]** *Explain* why light entering a 45° glass prism (*n* = 1.5) as in a periscope is

## Answers and marking guidance

*Not an official mark scheme.*

1. Angle of incidence = angle of reflection (both measured from the normal).
2. The angle of incidence (in the denser material) at which the angle of refraction is 90°.
3. Light travels from a denser to a less dense material; angle of incidence greater than the
   critical angle.
4. *n* = sin *i* / sin *r* and sin *c* = 1/*n*; both must be recalled.
5. Normal at 90° to the mirror at the point of incidence; incident and reflected rays with arrows,
   each 35° from the normal.
6. Bends towards the normal entering; away from the normal leaving; emerges parallel to the
   incident ray.
7. The ray then enters along a radius (a normal), so it does not bend at the curved surface; it
   refracts only at the flat face.
8. *n* = sin 50° ÷ sin 35° = 0.766 ÷ 0.574 = 1.34
9. sin *c* = 1 ÷ 2.4 = 0.417 → *c* = 25°
10. *n* = 1 ÷ sin 38° = 1 ÷ 0.616 = 1.6
11. sin *r* = sin 60° ÷ 1.5 = 0.577 → *r* = 35°
12. Light meets the side of the fibre at more than the critical angle, so it is totally
    internally reflected, again and again along the fibre.
13. 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz.
14. Time for one echo = 10.0 ÷ 20 = 0.50 s; distance = 2 × 85 = 170 m; *v* = 170 ÷ 0.50 = 340 m/s
15. Reaction time (≈ 0.2 s) is large compared with one echo time (0.5 s); over 20 echoes the
    reaction-time error is shared, so the percentage error is smaller.
16. One wave = 2 div; *T* = 2 × 2 ms = 4 ms = 0.004 s; *f* = 1 ÷ 0.004 = 250 Hz
17. Taller trace (larger amplitude); more waves across the screen (higher frequency, shorter
    period).
18. The microphone turns the sound's pressure changes into an alternating voltage with the same
    frequency; the oscilloscope plots this voltage (vertical) against time (horizontal).
19. Ray box and single slit, darkened room (1); draw round block on paper; mark incident and
    emergent rays with crosses (1); join to find the ray in the block; draw the normal (1);
    measure *i* and *r* with a protractor (1); repeat for several angles (1); plot sin *i*
    against sin *r*, gradient = *n* — or calculate sin *i* / sin *r* for each and average (1).
20. Light meets the long face at 45° (1); critical angle = sin⁻¹(1/1.5) = 42° (1); 45° > 42°
    and light is going from glass to air, so it is totally internally reflected (1).
