# Group 7 (halogens) — Questions

Specification points 2.5–2.8C · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. **Q8 is Paper 2 only.** Marks in
brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Give the colour and state at room temperature of (a) chlorine; (b) bromine; (c) iodine. (3)
2. Explain why the boiling points of the halogens increase down the group. (2)

## 2. Displacement

3. Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution. State the colour change and write the
   ionic equation. (3)
4. Explain why there is no reaction when iodine solution is added to potassium bromide solution. (1)
5. In the reaction Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂, identify the species that is oxidised. Explain your
   answer. (2)
6. Describe how displacement reactions can be used to show the order of reactivity of chlorine,
   bromine and iodine. (4)

## 3. Application and Paper 2

7. Astatine is below iodine. Predict its state at room temperature and whether it would displace
   iodine from potassium iodide solution. (2)
8. Explain why chlorine is more reactive than bromine. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. (a) pale yellow-green gas; (b) red-brown liquid; (c) grey-black solid (1 each; colour and state
   both needed).
2. Molecules get bigger / Mr increases (1); stronger intermolecular forces, more energy to overcome
   (1).
3. Colourless (very pale green) → brown (1); Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂ — species (1), balanced (1).
4. Iodine is less reactive than bromine (1).
5. Iodide ions (1); they lose electrons (1).
6. Add each halogen solution to solutions of the other halides (1); look for a colour change (1);
   chlorine displaces bromine and iodine / bromine displaces only iodine / iodine displaces neither
   (1); so reactivity Cl > Br > I (1).
7. Solid (1); no — astatine is less reactive than iodine (1).
8. Chlorine's outer shell is closer to the nucleus / fewer shells / less shielding (1); stronger
   attraction for an incoming electron (1); gains an electron more easily (1).
