## Causes of the main impacts

- **Sea level rise** = oceans warm and **expand** + **land ice** melts (not sea ice).
- **Weather** — warmer air holds more water vapour and energy → more extreme events.
- **Food** — climate zones shift towards the poles.

## Present day → predicted (by 2100)

| | Now | By 2100 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature | +1.3–1.4 °C; 2024 ~+1.55 °C | +1.4 °C (fast cuts) to +4.4 °C (very high emissions) |
| Sea level | ~0.2 m since 1900; 4.7 mm/yr | +0.3–1.0 m; once-a-century floods yearly at over half of sites |
| Weather | 1-in-50-year heat extreme ~5× as often | ~14× as often at 2 °C; wet wetter, dry drier; stronger storms |
| Food | yield growth slowed; harvest failures | lower tropical yields, higher prices; farming spreads to Canada, Russia |

## Scales

- **Global:** sea level, temperature, world food prices.
- **Regional:** Arctic ice loss (shipping routes vs habitat, Inuit); Mediterranean drought; coral bleaching.
- **National:** Bangladesh (delta flooding, salt in rice fields); Maldives (most land < 1 m).
- **Local:** coastal town eroded; farm harvest lost or season lengthened.

LICs are hit hardest but emitted least.

## Positive impacts (mainly high latitudes)

Fewer cold deaths; longer growing seasons; Arctic shipping and resources; longer tourist seasons;
some species spread; faster plant growth with more CO₂.

## Common traps

- Melting **sea ice** does not raise sea level.
- Not all impacts are negative — but negatives far outweigh positives.
