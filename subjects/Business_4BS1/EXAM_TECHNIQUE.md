# Business 4BS1 — Exam technique

How to structure answers for each 4BS1 question type. Synthesised from a school course-companion
document and Pearson examiner reports, rewritten in this repository's own words; it is answer
structure, not official mark-scheme wording. If your school teaches a different method, record it
in `me/profile.md` (subject notes) — the student's own school method wins for answer structure; the
official specification always wins for content scope.

## 1. The exam

| Paper | Length | Weight | Scenario context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 — Investigating small businesses | 1 h 30 min | 50% | Examines **all topics**, but scenarios are based on small businesses of up to 49 employees in a local/national market |
| Paper 2 — Investigating large businesses | 1 h 30 min | 50% | Examines **all topics**, but scenarios are based on large businesses of more than 250 employees in a multinational/global market |

- Calculators are allowed. Formulae are given in the exam.
- Four compulsory questions per paper, **20 marks each, 80 marks per paper**.

### Question-by-question layout (identical on both papers)

| | Question 1 | Question 2 | Question 3 | Question 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | MCQ × 6 (1 mark each) | State one × 2 (1 mark each) | Define the term × 1 (1 mark) | Calculate × 1 (2 marks) |

## 2. Formulae provided in the examination

Learn these by heart anyway — it saves time in the exam.

**Gross profit margin**
- Gross profit = revenue − cost of sales
- Gross profit margin = (gross profit ÷ revenue) × 100

**Operating profit margin**
- Operating profit = gross profit − other operating expenses
- Operating profit margin = (operating profit ÷ revenue) × 100

**Markup**
- Markup = (profit per item ÷ cost per item) × 100

**Return on capital employed (ROCE)**
- ROCE = (operating profit ÷ capital employed) × 100

**Current ratio**
- Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities

**Acid test ratio**
- Acid test ratio = (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities

> The source's provided-formulae list contains only the six above. It says nothing about
> whether break-even or cash-flow formulae are supplied; do not assert either way.

## 3. How to structure each question type

### The context rule (the most important rule)

> Only two question types do not need the business context.

The command words where context is not required are **Define the term /1** and **Explain one /3**.
Every other question type — State, Calculate, Outline, Analyse, Justify, Evaluate — **must be in
the context of the business in the scenario.**

### Define the term — 1 mark *(context not required)*
Give the correct definition. (AO1)

### State one — 1 mark
State one reason, **no longer than a sentence**. This must be **in the context of the business**. (AO2)

### Calculate — 2 marks
- 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into the formula (contextualising your answer).
- 1 mark for the correct answer.
- **Full marks are given for the correct numerical answer given without working.** (AO2 × 2)

### Outline one — 2 marks
- 1 mark for identifying a reason.
- 1 mark for developing the point **in context**. (AO2 × 2)

### Explain one — 3 marks *(context not required)*
- 1 mark for identification.
- 2 marks for **two further links** that explain the point.
- Begin the second and third sentence with connecting words such as **'therefore'** and **'as a result.'**
- **Aim for three sentences.** (AO1 × 3)

### Analyse — 6 marks
- **No definition required.** Give analysis which is **fully in context**. (AO2 × 3 and AO3 × 3)
- Either: one impact/benefit/drawback with **5 fully linked strands** of analysis, in context;
- Or: **2** impacts/benefits/drawbacks with **3 linked strands each**, in context.

### Justify — 9 marks
The answer should include analysis, evaluation and be **in context throughout**. Pick an option
from the two provided and come to a supported judgement. (AO2 × 3, AO3 × 3, AO4 × 3)

1. **Paragraph 1:** argue *for* your chosen option — advantages, with a brief comparison to the other option.
2. **Paragraph 2:** argue *against* your chosen option — disadvantages, with a brief comparison to the other option.
3. **Paragraph 3:** final judgement, justified with a reason from the context that is **specific to this business**.

### Evaluate — 12 marks
The answer should include analysis, evaluation and be **in context**. Weigh up the options and come
to a supported judgement. (AO1 × 3, AO2 × 3, AO3 × 3, AO4 × 3)

The source gives **two different frameworks depending on the paper/question wording**:

**Paper 1 — 'Evaluate the importance of …… to the business'**
1. Define a key business term in the question. Include context.
2. Argue **For**… However, the limitation is….
3. Argue **Against**… However, the limitation is….
4. Make a supported judgement focused on the business context.

**Paper 2 — 'Evaluate the *factors* …should consider when re-locating its business.'**
1. Define a key business term in the question. Include context.
2. One factor — for and evaluate.
3. Another factor — for and evaluate.
4. Make a supported judgement focused on the business context.

> The source ties these two frameworks to Paper 1 and Paper 2 respectively, but the real
> distinction it demonstrates is the **question wording** ('importance of X' vs 'the factors').
> It does not state which framework to use if a paper carries the other wording. Preserve that
> ambiguity: choose the framework that matches the wording, and say so.

## 4. Patterns good answers show

Strong style guidance rather than rules.

- **Chains, not lists.** Each point runs *cause → mechanism → business consequence*, linked with
  "This is because", "Therefore", "As a result", "Consequently", "This would enable".
- **Scenario quotation.** Analyse/Justify/Evaluate answers quote short phrases from the stimulus in
  single quotes and name the business, its rivals and its market.
- **"However, …" inside a paragraph** carries the evaluation in a 12-mark answer; the limitation is
  attached to the argument, not saved for the end.
- **Judgements are conditional.** They finish with what the judgement *depends on* — the firm's
  objectives, financial position, market, or product type.
- **First person is used** in judgements ("Overall, I judge that…").
- **A judgement may go against the option's apparent merits** — the mark is for supporting the judgement, not for choosing
  a "correct" side.

## 5. Rules

From the exam-technique document itself:

- **'State one'** must be **no longer than a sentence** — and must be in context.
- **'Analyse'** needs **no definition**.
- **'Calculate'**: full marks for a correct numerical answer with no working; the substitution mark
  is what protects a wrong final answer.
- Context is required **everywhere except** 'Define the term' and 'Explain one'.
- Learn the provided formulae anyway, to save time.

From Pearson examiner reports:

- Candidates lose 'Define' marks by giving a description or an explanation instead of a definition:
  *"A number of responses mentioned 'it is a chain of businesses with same name, logo etc' which is
  not a definition."*
- *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term."*
- 'State' marks are lost by offering a generic reason and **not applying it to the named business**.
- 12-mark answers lose marks for an unbalanced conclusion that does not relate back to the issues
  the business would actually face.
