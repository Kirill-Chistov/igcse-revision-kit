# Covalent bonding — Questions

Specification points 1.44–1.51 · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. What is a covalent bond? (1)
2. Explain, in terms of electrostatic attraction, what holds two atoms together in a covalent
   bond. (2)
3. State the number of covalent bonds formed by carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and hydrogen. (2)

## 2. Dot-and-cross diagrams (outer electrons only)

4. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of ammonia, NH₃. (2)
5. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of carbon dioxide. (2)
6. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of ethene, C₂H₄. (2)
7. Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for hydrogen bromide, HBr. (2)

## 3. Structure and properties

8. Explain why methane is a gas at room temperature. (3)
9. The boiling points of the alkanes increase from methane to butane. Explain why. (2)
10. Explain why diamond has a very high melting point. (3)
11. Explain why graphite conducts electricity and is soft. (4)
12. Explain why diamond does not conduct electricity. (1)
13. C₆₀ fullerene and diamond are both pure carbon. Explain why C₆₀ has a much lower melting
    point. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. A shared pair of electrons between two atoms (1).
2. Attraction between the shared pair of electrons (1) and the nuclei of both atoms (1).
3. C 4, N 3 (1); O 2, H 1 (1).
4. N with three shared pairs to three H atoms (1); one lone pair on N, all correct numbers of dots and
   crosses (1).
5. Two double bonds, each with 2 dots + 2 crosses (1); two lone pairs on each O (1).
6. C=C double bond (two shared pairs) (1); each C with two single bonds to H and no lone pairs (1).
7. One shared pair (1); three lone pairs on Br (1).
8. Simple molecular / small molecules (1); weak intermolecular forces (1); little energy needed to
   overcome them (1).
9. Larger molecules / higher Mr have stronger intermolecular forces (1); more energy needed to
   overcome them (1).
10. Giant covalent structure (1); many strong covalent bonds (1); lots of energy needed to break
    them (1).
11. Each carbon forms 3 bonds, leaving one delocalised electron (1); delocalised electrons move
    through / along the layers (1); layers held by weak forces (1); so layers can slide over each
    other (1).
12. No delocalised / free electrons — all outer electrons are used in covalent bonds (1).
13. C₆₀ is simple molecular (1); only weak intermolecular forces need overcoming when it melts (1);
    melting diamond requires breaking many strong covalent bonds (1).
