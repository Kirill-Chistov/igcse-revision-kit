# Reversible reactions and equilibria — Questions

Specification points 3.17–3.22C · Topic 3 Physical chemistry. Questions 4–9 are **Paper 2 only**.
Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Reversible reactions

1. State the meaning of the symbol ⇌ in an equation. (1)
2. Describe what is seen when blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated and then, after cooling,
   water is added to the product. (3)
3. Solid ammonium chloride is heated at the bottom of a test tube. Describe and explain what is
   seen near the top of the tube. Include equations. (4)

## 2. Equilibrium — Paper 2 only

4. State the condition needed for a reversible reaction to reach dynamic equilibrium. (1)
5. Give the two characteristics of a reaction at dynamic equilibrium. (2)
6. Explain why adding a catalyst does not change the position of equilibrium. (2)
7. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)   ΔH = −196 kJ/mol
   (a) State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the yield of SO₃. (2)
   (b) State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of SO₃. (2)
8. N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g). The forward reaction is endothermic. State the effect on the amount of NO₂
   of (a) decreasing the temperature and (b) decreasing the pressure. (2)
9. Explain why changing the pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium in
   H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. The reaction is reversible / can go in both directions (1).
2. Blue crystals turn white (1); steam / water vapour given off (1); on adding water the white
   powder turns blue and gets hot (1).
3. White solid (re)forms near the top (1); NH₄Cl → NH₃ + HCl when heated (1); gases react again
   where the tube is cooler (1); NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl (1). (Accept one equation with ⇌ for both
   equation marks if the directions are explained.)
4. A sealed container / closed system (1).
5. Forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate (1); concentrations of reactants and
   products remain constant (1).
6. It increases the rates of the forward and reverse reactions (1) by the same amount / equally (1).
7. (a) Yield decreases (1); equilibrium shifts in the endothermic direction, which is the reverse
   reaction (1).
   (b) Yield increases (1); equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas: 3 mol → 2 mol
   (1).
8. (a) Decreases (1). (b) Increases (1).
9. Same number of moles of gas on each side (2 and 2) (1).
