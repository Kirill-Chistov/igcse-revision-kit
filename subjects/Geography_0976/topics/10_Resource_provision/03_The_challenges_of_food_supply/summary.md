# The challenges of food supply — Summary

Specification points 10.3.1–10.3.6 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976

**Factors reducing supply (10.3.1)** — natural: drought, floods/storms, extreme temperatures,
pests and diseases, poor soils/steep slopes, climate change. Human: conflict, poverty, poor
storage and transport, land degradation, population growth, land used for cities/cash
crops/biofuels, prices, corruption, HIC subsidies.

**Food insecurity (10.3.2)** — food security = all people, at all times, access to sufficient,
safe, nutritious food. Cycle: insecurity → malnutrition → weak health → low productivity →
poverty → insecurity.
LICs: undernutrition, stunting, famine, aid dependence, migration. MICs: double burden
(undernutrition + obesity), price riots. HICs: food poverty, food banks, obesity-related disease.

**Increasing supply locally (10.3.3)** — strategies: land reform, microfinance, training,
roads/storage/markets, research, fairer trade. Techniques: drip irrigation, rainwater
harvesting, improved seed, manure/rotation, airtight storage, appropriate technology.

**Food aid (10.3.4)** — relief (emergency, saves lives), programme (sold by governments —
undercuts local farmers), project (school meals, food-for-work). Judgement: vital short-term;
weak long-term — dependency, treats symptoms; best bought locally and linked to development.

**Desertification and soil erosion (10.3.5)** — causes: overgrazing, overcultivation,
deforestation, drought → bare soil → topsoil lost → yields fall → land abandoned.
Terraces · contour strips · stone lines/half-moons · shelterbelts and agroforestry · grazing
control · cover crops · efficient stoves. Most sustainable = cheap, local, community-run, tackles
the cause.

**Nigeria (10.3.6)** — >220 m people; LMIC since 2014; farming 23% of GDP, >50% of jobs; 37% in
poverty; 2023 food-security emergency; 26.5 m facing acute hunger (2024 lean season).
- Supply factors: 2022 floods damaged >675,000 ha; desertification in 15 of 36 states; low
  irrigation and yields; post-harvest losses >10%; poor storage, roads, markets.
- Causes (millions affected): conflict ≈15 (NE, >2 m displaced; herder–farmer clashes),
  economic ≈12 (inflation; food ≈50% of spending), climate change ≈10, infrastructure ≈8.
- Problems: malnutrition → stunting, weak immunity, lifelong ill-health; worst in the NE;
  stabilisation centres; pressure on health care; low productivity.
- Strategies: storage/processing plants, R&D, mechanisation, microfinance, training, sustainable
  practices, diversification, agro-processing, land tenure reform. WFP: food for under-fives,
  livelihood skills, school feeding, air transport, internet for advisers. Many policies failed —
  funding, coordination, political will.
