## 1. The units (6.1)

| Unit | Symbol | Quantity | Used in this topic for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ampere | A | current | current in a wire, coil, electromagnet or motor; primary and secondary currents *I*<sub>p</sub>, *I*<sub>s</sub> |
| volt | V | voltage | induced voltage; primary and secondary voltages *V*<sub>p</sub>, *V*<sub>s</sub> |
| watt | W | power | power into and out of a transformer, *V* × *I* |

1 W = 1 V × 1 A, because power = current × voltage *(→ 2.4)*.

Transformer questions often use prefixes:

```
1 kV = 1000 V      1 kW = 1000 W      1 MW = 1 000 000 W      1 mA = 0.001 A
```

::: note | Method
Convert to V, A and W before using power = voltage × current. In the turns-ratio equation
(→ 6.19) the two voltages only need the **same** unit — both in kV is fine.

Example: 2 MW is sent along a cable at 400 kV. *I* = *P* ÷ *V* = 2 000 000 W ÷ 400 000 V = **5 A**.
:::

::: traps
A turns ratio (*N*<sub>p</sub> : *N*<sub>s</sub>) is a pure number — it has **no unit**.
:::
