## Answers and marking guidance

*AI-generated for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme. One mark per creditable
point unless stated.*

1. Steep sides (1); narrow floor, occupied by the river (1).
2. River erodes vertically (1) by hydraulic action/abrasion (1); weathering loosens the valley sides
   (1); mass movement moves material down into the river, which transports it away (1).
3. Ridges of higher land projecting alternately from each side of a V-shaped valley (1). The river
   erodes vertically rather than laterally (1), lacking the energy to cut sideways through them, so
   it flows round them (1).
4. Level-marked guide: hard rock over soft rock (1); soft rock eroded faster by hydraulic action and
   abrasion (1); undercutting (1); plunge pool deepened (1); overhang collapses, blocks aid abrasion
   (1); repeated, the waterfall retreats upstream leaving a gorge (1). Up to 2 of the 6 from a clear
   labelled diagram.
5. Alternating bands of hard and soft rock (1); soft rock eroded faster, making steps in the bed that
   the water tumbles over (1).
6. Pebbles caught in hollows/cracks in the bed (1); swirled round by eddies in turbulent water (1);
   abrasion drills the hollow deeper and wider (1).
7. River cliff on the outer bank (1); slip-off slope/point bar on the inner bank (1); deepest water
   near the outer bank (1).
8. Fastest flow on the outer bend (1) erodes the bank by hydraulic action/abrasion → steep river cliff
   (1); slower, shallower water on the inner bend (1) deposits sediment → gentle slip-off slope (1).
9. Erosion on the outer bends (1) narrows the neck of the meander (1); during a flood the river cuts
   through the neck, taking the shortest route (1); deposition seals the ends of the old meander,
   leaving an oxbow lake (1).
10. Wide, flat land either side of the river (1); covered in layers of alluvium/silt; floods when the
    river overflows (1).
11. River floods and water spills over the banks (1); it slows immediately because of friction (1);
    coarsest/heaviest sediment deposited first, beside the channel (1); repeated floods build up
    raised banks (1).
12. Deltas need a large sediment load (1) and a sheltered sea or lake with weak tides and currents
    (1), so sediment is deposited faster than it is removed (1). Where currents are strong, the
    sediment is carried away.
13. Meanders (1); oxbow lakes / floodplain (1); middle or lower course (1).
