# The main landforms associated with river processes — Summary

Specification point 1.2.1 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1.

## Upper course — vertical erosion

- **V-shaped valley:** steep sides, narrow floor. Vertical erosion cuts down; weathering and mass
  movement bring the sides down; the river carries the debris away.
- **Interlocking spurs:** ridges from alternate sides; the river winds round them because it erodes
  down, not sideways.
- **Waterfall → gorge:** hard rock over soft → soft rock undercut (hydraulic action, abrasion) →
  plunge pool deepens → overhang collapses → fall retreats upstream → gorge.
- **Rapids:** alternating hard and soft bands → steps → fast, turbulent, shallow water.
- **Potholes:** pebbles swirled by eddies drill circular hollows into the bed (abrasion).

## Middle and lower course — lateral erosion and deposition

- **Meander:** fastest flow on the outer bend → erosion → **river cliff**; slow, shallow inner bend →
  deposition → **slip-off slope** (point bar). Asymmetric cross-section.
- **Oxbow lake:** outer-bend erosion narrows the neck → a flood cuts through it → deposition seals
  the ends.
- **Floodplain:** wide, flat valley floor; widened by migrating meanders, built up by alluvium.
- **Levées:** flood water leaves the channel and slows → coarse sediment dropped first beside it →
  raised banks after many floods.
- **Delta:** velocity falls at the mouth → deposition; clay flocculates in salt water; sediment
  builds faster than tides remove it; distributaries. e.g. Nile (fan-shaped).

## Common traps

- Waterfall answers must say *why* the soft rock erodes faster, and name the processes.
- River cliff = outer, steep, erosion. Slip-off slope = inner, gentle, deposition.
- Levées: coarsest sediment nearest the channel.
