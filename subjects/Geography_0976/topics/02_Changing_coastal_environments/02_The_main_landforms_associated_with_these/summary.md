## Coastline types

- **Discordant:** hard and soft bands **at right angles** to the coast → soft eroded into **bays** (sheltered, beaches), hard left as **headlands** (cliffs, caves, arches, stacks). Irregular coast.
- **Concordant:** bands **parallel** → straight coast; sea breaks through outer hard band, scoops out soft rock → **cove** with narrow entrance (Lulworth Cove).

## Erosional landforms

- **Cliff → wave-cut platform:** waves erode base between high and low tide (hydraulic action, corrasion) → **notch** → overhang collapses → cliff retreats → gently sloping rocky **platform**, exposed at low tide.
- **Crack → cave → arch → stack → stump:** on headlands (refraction + joints/faults). Arch roof collapses → stack; stack undercut → stump, covered at high tide. Durdle Door (arch), Old Harry Rocks (stack).

## Depositional landforms

- **Beach:** constructive waves deposit in low-energy bays. Sand = gentle (strong backwash); shingle = steep (water drains, weak backwash). Berms at high-tide lines.
- **Spit:** longshore drift + coast changes direction → deposition into open water; secondary wind → **recurved end**; **salt marsh** behind; river stops it crossing (Spurn Head).
- **Bar:** spit grows across a bay, joins two headlands → **lagoon** (Loe Bar).
- **Sand dunes:** wide beach, lots of sand, onshore wind; debris traps sand → embryo → **marram grass** binds → yellow → humus → grey → slack → mature (older, more vegetated inland).

## Traps

- Wave-cut platform is erosional.
- Stack above high tide; stump covered at high tide.
- Discordant = right angles; concordant = parallel.
