# The global patterns of energy supply and demand — Summary

Specification points 10.5.1–10.5.3 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976

**Rising production and consumption (10.5.1)** — use up more than six-fold since 1950, ~⅘ fossil
fuels. Consumption: population growth, industrialisation (China, India), rising incomes (cars,
appliances, air-con), urbanisation, new technology, electricity access in LICs. Production:
demand and prices, new extraction technology (deep-sea, fracking), cheaper renewables, energy
security.

**Surplus and deficit (10.5.2)** — surplus = produces more than uses (net exporter): Middle East,
Russia, Norway, Canada, Australia, Nigeria/Angola/Algeria; USA small surplus (shale). Deficit =
net importer: Europe (Germany, Italy), Japan, South Korea, China, India, many LICs.
**Energy security** = uninterrupted energy at an affordable price. Deficit → imports → exposed to
supply cuts, conflict, price spikes → lost output and jobs, fuel poverty, inflation, blackouts
(e.g. Russian gas cuts to Europe, 2022).

**Why energy types vary (10.5.3)** — HICs: oil, gas, nuclear, growing renewables; wealth,
technology, climate targets. MICs: coal and oil for industrialisation, big HEP, fast-growing
renewables. LICs: fuelwood, charcoal, dung, kerosene — poverty, no grid. Also: physical
resources (reserves, rivers, plate boundaries, sun, wind), policy and public opinion (German
nuclear closure 2023). Within a country: urban grid vs rural fuelwood/off-grid solar; rich vs
poor; energy produced where the resource is.
