# Market research — Questions

Specification points 4.1.1–4.1.3 · both papers

> Marking guidance below is written for this repository. It is **not** an official Pearson
> mark scheme. Answer structure for each command word: `subjects/Business_4BS1/EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md`.

**Past papers:** your own, in `me/papers/Business_4BS1/` if you have added them (`/test` uses them).

## 1. Recall

1. Define the term *market research*. (1)
2. Define the term *secondary research*. (1)
3. State two purposes of market research. (2)
4. State two methods of primary research and two sources of secondary research. (2)

## 2. Explain (3 marks each — context not required)

5. Explain one disadvantage of using a focus group.
6. Explain one advantage of test marketing a new product.
7. Explain one benefit to a business of collecting quantitative data.
8. Explain one reason why social media data may be unreliable.

## 3. Applied

9. A café owner plans to open in a new town. State one primary and one secondary method she
   could use, each in context. (2)
10. A clothing retailer surveyed 20 customers in one store and found 80% wanted a new range.
    Outline one reason why this result may not be reliable. (2)
11. A phone manufacturer is choosing between (1) buying a market report and (2) holding focus
    groups. Justify which one it should choose. (9)

## Marking guidance

1. Gathering, analysing and interpreting information about customers, competitors and the market.
2. Using information that already exists / was collected by someone else for another purpose.
3. Any two: identify/understand customer needs; identify gaps in the market; reduce risk;
   inform business decisions.
4. Primary: survey, questionnaire, focus group, observation, test marketing. Secondary: internet,
   market reports, government reports.
5. Identify (small group) → not representative of the whole market → decisions based on it may
   be wrong / product may not sell. Alternatives: costly per person; dominant members sway views.
6. Identify (sells in one small area first) → shows real demand / problems before full launch →
   reduces risk of a costly failed launch.
7. Identify (numerical) → easy to analyse, compare and show trends in graphs → helps set sales
   forecasts or prices.
8. Identify (users not typical of all customers / fake or extreme reviews) → sample biased →
   business may misjudge demand.
9. Primary: e.g. questionnaire to people in the town's high street about what they would buy.
   Secondary: e.g. government population data for the town; competitors' prices online.
   One mark each, must fit the café.
10. Sample of 20 is small / one store only → not representative of all customers (1); the
    retailer may produce a range that does not sell elsewhere (1).
11. Level-marked. Market report: quick, expert, large-scale data but costly, general and
    available to rivals. Focus groups: detailed reasons why customers want features, tests
    prototypes, not seen by rivals; but small, may be unrepresentative, costly. Judgement must
    use the context (e.g. a new, innovative phone → focus groups give specific feedback that a
    report cannot).
