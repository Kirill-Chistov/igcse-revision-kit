# Style Guide

Working rules for every piece of study material in this repository.

## Audience

A Year 10–11 IGCSE/GCSE student preparing to sit the examination. Assume intelligence and
motivation; do not assume prior knowledge of the topic beyond what earlier specification points
cover.

## Depth

- Deep enough to **sit the exam with confidence** — not a summary of a summary.
- Difficult ideas must be **explained**, not merely named. If a term is introduced, the reader
  must be able to say what it means and why it matters by the end of the paragraph.
- Short enough to actually revise from. Length is earned, never assumed.

## Accuracy and terminology

- Use the exam board's own terminology and spelling.
- Definitions that carry marks must be **precise and complete** — write them as they would be
  credited in a mark scheme, and mark them clearly as definitions.
- Never state something as examinable that the specification does not require.

## Structure

- Equations, processes, sequences and rules get their own clearly labelled block, not a
  sentence buried in prose.
- Only **must know** (specification content) plus the minimum bridging explanation. Enrichment is
  omitted, not labelled.
- Examples earn their place by making an idea clearer. Cut examples that only add words.
- Use tables for comparisons, lists for sequences, prose for reasoning.

## Tone

- Direct and plain. No filler, no motivational padding, no textbook throat-clearing.
- No "as we have seen", "it is important to note", "in this section we will".
- Address the reader as *you* when giving topic-specific exam advice; no generic exam tips.

## Format

- **Markdown is the master.** Every `.md` is the authoritative version.
- **The topic PDF is the derived, polished study version** (`notes/<Subject>/NN_<Topic>.pdf`). Regenerate it
  whenever a `topic.md` changes; never edit a PDF directly. It carries no recap, self-test or checklist.
- Every topic file states, at the top, exactly which specification points it covers.
- Keep line length reasonable and headings shallow (`##` / `###`) so files are diff-friendly and
  easy for an AI to chunk.
