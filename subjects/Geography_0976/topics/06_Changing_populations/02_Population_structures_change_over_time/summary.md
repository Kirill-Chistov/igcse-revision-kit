# Population structures change over time — Summary

Specification points 6.2.1–6.2.3 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Population pyramid:** % of population in each 5-year age group; males left, females right.
- **Natural increase shapes it:** birth rate → width of the **base**; death rate and life expectancy → slope of the **sides** and width of the **top**; baby booms → **bulges** that move up over time; war or famine → **notches**.
- **Net migration shapes the working ages:** immigration → bulge at ~20–45, often mostly men (Qatar: 71% male); emigration → gap in young adults, fewer births later.
- **Over time** (DTM): wide triangle → straighter sides, narrower base → base narrower than the middle.
- **Youthful** (Niger: 47% under 15) — caused by high birth rate and falling child mortality. **Ageing** (Japan: 30% aged 65+) — caused by low birth rate, longer life, emigration of the young, baby boomers reaching 65.
- **Youthful impacts:** − high dependency ratio, schools and child healthcare, too few jobs → unemployment and emigration, continued growth; + large future workforce, attracts investment.
- **Ageing impacts:** − fewer workers per pensioner, pension and care costs, labour shortages, services close; + savings and spending, childcare and volunteering, experience.
- **Evaluation:** youthful = burden now, **demographic dividend** later if birth rates fall and there are education and jobs. Ageing = manageable in rich countries (later retirement, immigration, automation); most serious where a country ages before it is rich (China). Decider: dependency ratio + a productive, employed workforce + early planning.

## Definitions

**Population structure** — the make-up of a population by age and sex.

**Dependency ratio** — (0–14 + 65+) ÷ (15–64) × 100.

**Demographic dividend** — faster economic growth when a large generation reaches working age with few dependants.
