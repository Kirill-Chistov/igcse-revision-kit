# Radioactivity — Summary

Specification points 7.2–7.16 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1 · both papers.

## Atoms and isotopes

- Nucleus: **protons (+1, mass 1)** and **neutrons (0, mass 1)**; **electrons (−1, tiny mass)** orbit it. Neutral atom: protons = electrons.
- **Z, atomic (proton) number** = protons. **A, mass (nucleon) number** = protons + neutrons. Neutrons = A − Z.
- **Isotopes:** same protons, different neutrons.

## Decay and the three radiations

- Unstable nuclei emit **α, β⁻, γ** — all **ionising**; decay is **random** (can't predict which nucleus or when).
- **α** = 2p + 2n (helium nucleus), +2 · strongly ionising · stopped by **paper / few cm air**.
- **β⁻** = fast electron from the nucleus, −1 · moderate · stopped by **few mm aluminium**.
- **γ** = EM wave, 0 · weakly ionising · reduced by **several cm lead**.
- **Practical:** background first → source at fixed distance from GM tube → add paper, Al, lead → subtract background, repeat, average. Tongs, short time, large distance, lead box.

## Nuclear changes and equations

- **α:** A −4, Z −2 · **β⁻:** A same, Z +1 (neutron → proton) · **γ:** no change · **neutron:** A −1, Z same.
- Balance **top numbers (mass)** and **bottom numbers (charge)**.

## Detection and background

- **GM tube + counter**, or **photographic film** (darkens).
- Background: **radon** gas from rocks (biggest), rocks/soil/buildings, food and drink; **cosmic rays** from space (more at altitude). Always subtract it.

## Activity and half-life

- **Activity** = decays per second, **Bq**; falls with time.
- **Half-life** = time for **half the unstable nuclei to decay** / **activity to halve**. Different for each isotope.
- Count halvings; on a graph, read the time for any value to halve. **Subtract background before halving.**

## Uses, contamination, dangers

- γ: radiotherapy, tracers (short half-life), sterilising, pipe leaks. β: thickness gauging. α: smoke detectors.
- **Irradiation:** exposed to an outside source, not made radioactive. **Contamination:** radioactive material on/in it — keeps irradiating until removed or decayed.
- Dangers: **DNA mutations → cancer**; **cells and tissue damaged or killed**. Inside the body α is worst; outside, β and γ.
- Waste stays radioactive for thousands of years → sealed in glass/steel, stored deep underground in stable rock, shielded, monitored, secure.
