## 2. Calculate (2 marks: substitution + answer)

A company makes bicycles. Fixed costs are 45,000 MYR a month; variable cost is 250 MYR per bicycle;
each sells for 400 MYR.

4. Calculate the break-even level of output. (2)
5. Calculate the profit if 360 bicycles are sold in a month. (2)
6. The company sells 360 bicycles a month. Calculate the margin of safety. (2)

## 3. Explain and analyse

7. Explain the effect on the break-even level of output of a rise in the price of raw materials. (3)
8. Explain one limitation of break-even charts. (3)
9. Analyse the benefits to a small juice bar of using break-even analysis before launching a new
   smoothie. (6)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Costs that change directly with the level of output.
2. The level of output where total revenue equals total costs — no profit or loss.
3. E.g. rent of the salon / salaries / insurance (must not vary with number of customers).
4. 45,000 ÷ (400 − 250) (1) = 300 bicycles (1).
5. Revenue 400 × 360 = 144,000; total costs 45,000 + (250 × 360) = 135,000 (1); profit = 9,000 MYR (1).
6. 360 − 300 (1) = 60 bicycles (1).
7. Raw materials are a variable cost, so variable cost per unit rises (1) → each unit sold
   contributes less towards fixed costs / the TC line becomes steeper (1) → so more units must be
   sold to break even (1).
8. It assumes all output is sold (1) → in reality some products may not sell and become unsold
   inventory (1) → so revenue and profit are overstated and the break-even point is misleading (1).
   *Also credit: constant price/variable cost per unit; fixed costs may change; estimates.*
9. Look for in-context chains, e.g. shows how many smoothies must be sold each day to cover costs →
   owner can judge whether that is realistic for the bar's customer numbers → avoids launching a
   loss-making product; and/or tests price changes → sees how a higher price lowers break-even →
   helps set a price that covers the cost of fresh fruit. Two points × three links, or one × five.
