# Atomic structure — Questions

Specification points 1.14–1.17 · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. State the relative mass and relative charge of a proton, a neutron and an electron. (3)
2. What is meant by the term *molecule*? (1)
3. Define *mass number*. (1)
4. Define *isotopes*. (2)

## 2. Understanding and application

5. An atom of potassium is ³⁹₁₉K. State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons. (3)
6. Explain why an atom has no overall charge. (1)
7. Magnesium-24 and magnesium-26 are isotopes. Explain why they have the same chemical
   properties. (2)
8. Explain why the relative atomic mass of chlorine is not a whole number. (2)

## 3. Calculations

9. Boron is 20% boron-10 and 80% boron-11. Calculate its relative atomic mass. (2)
10. Copper has two isotopes: copper-63 (69.2%) and copper-65 (30.8%). Calculate Ar to one
    decimal place. (2)
11. A sample of neon contains neon-20, neon-21 and neon-22 in the ratio 90 : 1 : 9. Calculate the
    relative atomic mass of neon. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Proton 1, +1 (1); neutron 1, 0 (1); electron 1/1836 (accept negligible / 1/2000), −1 (1).
2. Two or more atoms joined by covalent bonds (1).
3. Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom (1).
4. Atoms of the same element / same number of protons (1); different numbers of neutrons (1).
5. 19 protons (1); 20 neutrons (1); 19 electrons (1).
6. Equal numbers of protons (positive) and electrons (negative) (1).
7. Same number of electrons / same electronic configuration (1); chemical reactions involve
   electrons (only) (1).
8. It is a weighted mean of the masses of its isotopes (1); chlorine exists as chlorine-35 and
   chlorine-37 in different abundances (1).
9. (10 × 20 + 11 × 80) ÷ 100 (1) = 10.8 (1).
10. (63 × 69.2 + 65 × 30.8) ÷ 100 (1) = 63.6 (1).
11. (20 × 90 + 21 × 1 + 22 × 9) ÷ 100 (1) = 20.2 (1).
