# Extraction and uses of metals — Questions

Specification points 2.22C–2.27C · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. **All Paper 2 only.** Marks in
brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall and understanding

1. Explain why gold is found as the uncombined element. (1)
2. Explain why iron can be extracted by heating its oxide with carbon but aluminium cannot. (3)
3. Write the half-equation for the formation of aluminium at the cathode. (1)
4. What is an alloy? (1)
5. Explain why an alloy is harder than the pure metal. (3)

## 2. Uses

6. Explain why aluminium is used for aircraft bodies. (2)
7. Explain why copper is used for electrical wiring. (2)
8. Give **one** use each of mild steel and stainless steel, and the property that makes each
   suitable. (4)

## 3. Commenting on a process

9. Titanium is more reactive than carbon. It is extracted by heating titanium chloride with
   sodium, which is made by electrolysis. Suggest **two** reasons why titanium is expensive. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. It is unreactive / does not react with oxygen or water (1).
2. Iron is less reactive than carbon (1), so carbon can remove the oxygen / reduce iron oxide (1);
   aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so it needs electrolysis (1).
3. Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (1).
4. A mixture of a metal with one or more other elements (usually metals or carbon) (1).
5. Atoms of different sizes (1) distort the layers (1); layers cannot slide over each other as
   easily (1).
6. Low density (1); resists corrosion (1).
7. Very good conductor of electricity (1); ductile / can be drawn into wires (1).
8. Mild steel — e.g. car bodies (1), malleable / easily shaped / strong (1); stainless steel — e.g.
   cutlery (1), resists corrosion (1).
9. Any two: the sodium is made by electrolysis, which uses a lot of electricity (1); heating uses
   energy (1); titanium is too reactive to be extracted cheaply with carbon (1).
