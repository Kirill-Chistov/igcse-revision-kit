## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. Slow growth until about 1950, then a steep rise (1); more than six-fold since 1950, to about 180,000 TWh (1); mostly oil, coal and gas, with renewables small but growing (1).
2. Large surpluses in the Middle East, Russia, Norway, Canada and Australia (1); some African oil and gas producers, e.g. Nigeria, Angola (1); surplus countries have large fossil-fuel reserves compared with use / Europe and East Asia are mainly in deficit (1).
3. A country uses more energy than it produces (1), so it must import energy (1).
4. Two of: population growth; industrialisation in emerging economies; rising incomes → cars, appliances; urbanisation; new technology — 1 + 1 development each.
5. Relies on imports (1), so it is exposed to supply cuts, conflict or price spikes (1); shortages or high prices cut industrial output and jobs (1) and raise household bills / fuel poverty (1). Credit an example such as Russian gas cuts to Europe in 2022.
6. Rural areas often not connected to the grid (1); wood is free to collect / people are poorer (1); cities have grid electricity and bottled gas (1); off-grid solar in rural areas (1).
7. HIC: wealth and technology for nuclear, gas, renewables and a national grid (1–2). LIC: poverty, little grid, so reliance on fuelwood, charcoal and kerosene (1–2).
8. Level marking. *L1 (1–2):* simple statements. *L2 (3–4):* explains differences between HICs and LICs with reasons. *L3 (5–6):* covers HICs, MICs and LICs with several reasons — wealth, technology, resources, industrialisation, policy — and examples.
