# Alcohols — Questions

Specification points 4.29C–4.33C (Paper 2 only) · Topic 4 Organic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Give the functional group of the alcohols. (1)
2. Draw the displayed formula of ethanol. (1)
3. Name the alcohol CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH. (1)

## 2. Oxidation

4. Write the equation for the complete combustion of ethanol. (2)
5. Give the reagents and condition for oxidising ethanol to ethanoic acid in the laboratory, and the
   colour change seen. (3)
6. A bottle of wine is left open to the air. Explain why it turns sour. (2)

## 3. Manufacture

7. Give the conditions used to make ethanol from ethene. (3)
8. Write the equation for the fermentation of glucose. (2)
9. Explain why fermentation is carried out (a) in the absence of air (b) at about 30 °C rather than
   60 °C. (4)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. –OH (1).
2. CH₃–CH₂–O–H with all bonds shown (1).
3. Butan-1-ol / butanol (1).
4. C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O — formulae (1), balancing (1).
5. Potassium dichromate(VI) (1) in dilute sulfuric acid, heat (1); orange to green (1).
6. Microbes (1) oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid using oxygen from the air (1).
7. Steam (1); phosphoric acid catalyst (1); about 300 °C and 60–70 atm (1).
8. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ — formulae (1), balancing (1).
9. (a) Oxygen would allow ethanol to be oxidised to ethanoic acid (1) (accept: yeast would respire
   aerobically, making CO₂ and water, not ethanol). (b) 30 °C is the optimum temperature for the yeast
   enzymes (1); at 60 °C the enzymes would be denatured (1) so fermentation would stop (1).
