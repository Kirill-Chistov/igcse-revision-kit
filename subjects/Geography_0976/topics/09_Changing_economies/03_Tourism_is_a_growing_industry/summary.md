# Tourism is a growing industry — Summary

Specification points 9.3.1–9.3.5 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Growth:** 25 million international arrivals (1950) → 1.47 billion (2019) → 0.40 billion (2020,
  COVID-19) → 1.52 billion (2025).
- **Why:** *economic* — rising incomes (now MIC middle classes), cheap package holidays and
  low-cost airlines · *social* — paid holidays, active retired people, media and advertising ·
  *technological* — bigger, faster jets, online booking, cars · *political* — easier visas, open
  borders, governments promoting tourism, stability.
- **Butler model:** exploration → involvement → development → consolidation → stagnation (at
  carrying capacity) → A rejuvenation · B reduced growth · C stabilisation · D decline ·
  E immediate decline.
- **Butler strengths:** simple, widely fits, links growth to carrying capacity, warns planners,
  several futures. **Limitations:** describes not predicts, stages hard to spot and of varying
  length, not all places follow it, visitor numbers only, shocks only as E.
- **Benefits:** jobs, income, multiplier, infrastructure, foreign currency, tax, conservation
  funding, cultural exchange; globally US$11.6 trillion, 9.8% of GDP, 366 million jobs (2025).
- **Problems:** seasonal low-paid jobs, rising prices, crowding, culture changed, footpath erosion,
  habitat damage, water shortage, **leakage**, dependence on a fragile industry, aviation emissions.
- **Scales:** local · regional · national · global — name the scale in every answer.
- **Management:** global (Code of Ethics, World Heritage listing) · national (protected-area
  laws, Bhutan's US$100-a-night fee, ecotourism) · local (zoning, fees and quotas — Venice day-tripper
  fee since 2024 — footpath management, education).
- **Judgement:** local techniques treat symptoms; limiting numbers and keeping income local treat
  causes; global strategies are voluntary; best when enforced, self-financing and shared with locals.

## Definitions

**Tourism** — the temporary movement of people to places outside where they normally live and
work, for leisure, business or other purposes, staying at least one night.

**Carrying capacity** — the maximum number of visitors a place can support before its environment,
community or visitor experience is damaged.

**Leakage** — the share of tourist spending that leaves the destination, to foreign-owned firms or
imports.

**Sustainable tourism** — tourism that takes full account of its current and future economic,
social and environmental impacts, meeting the needs of visitors, industry, environment and hosts.

**Ecotourism** — responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, benefits local
people and educates visitors.
