# Magnetism — Questions

Specification points 6.2–6.7 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1.

> These questions and the marking guidance are **written for this repository by AI**. They are not
> official Pearson questions and the guidance is not an official mark scheme. No 4PH1 past papers
> are stored in the repository yet.

## 1. Forces and materials (6.2, 6.3, 6.5)

1. Name **two** magnetic substances. (2)
2. A steel bar is attracted to both ends of a bar magnet. A student says the bar must be a magnet. Explain why the student is wrong and describe a test that would show whether the bar is a magnet. (3)
3. Explain why an iron nail is attracted to the S pole of a magnet. (2)
4. Describe the difference between a magnetically hard and a magnetically soft material, giving an example of each. (4)
5. Suggest why the core of an electromagnet used in a scrapyard crane is made of iron, not steel. (2)

## 2. Field lines and patterns (6.4, 6.6, 6.7)

6. State what is meant by a magnetic field line. (1)
7. Sketch the magnetic field pattern around a single bar magnet. (3)
8. Describe how to use a plotting compass to plot the magnetic field pattern of a bar magnet. (4)
9. Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using iron filings instead of a plotting compass. (2)
10. Two bar magnets are placed end to end with their N poles facing, 4 cm apart. Sketch the field between them and label the neutral point. (3)
11. Describe how to use two permanent magnets to produce a uniform magnetic field, and state how the field lines show that it is uniform. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. Any two of: iron, steel, nickel, cobalt (1)(1).
2. A magnet attracts any magnetic material, so attraction does not show the bar is a magnet (1); magnetism is induced in the bar / the near end becomes the opposite pole (1); bring each end of the bar near a pole of a magnet — only a magnet will **repel** one end (1).
3. Magnetism is induced in the nail (1); the end nearest the S pole becomes a N pole, and unlike poles attract (1).
4. Soft: easily magnetised and loses its magnetism easily / temporary (1), e.g. iron (1). Hard: difficult to magnetise but keeps its magnetism / permanent (1), e.g. steel (1).
5. Iron is magnetically soft (1); it loses its magnetism when the current is switched off, so the scrap is released (1).
6. The direction of the force on a north pole placed in the field / the direction a compass points (1).
7. Lines leaving N and entering S (1); closed smooth curves that do not cross, closest together at the poles (1); arrows from N to S (1).
8. Place the magnet on paper and draw round it (1); put the compass near one pole and mark the position the needle points to with a dot (1); move the compass so its other end is on the dot and repeat until reaching the other pole (1); join the dots and add an arrow N → S; repeat from other starting points (1).
9. Advantage: shows the whole pattern quickly (1). Disadvantage: does not show the direction of the field (1).
10. Lines from each N pole curving away from each other, not crossing (1); arrows pointing away from the N poles (1); neutral point marked midway between the poles (1).
11. Opposite poles (N and S) facing each other (1); close together with flat faces parallel (1); lines in the gap are straight, parallel and equally spaced (1).
