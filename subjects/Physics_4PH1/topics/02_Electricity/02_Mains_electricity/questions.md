# Mains electricity — Questions

Specification points 2.2–2.6 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1.

> These questions and the marking guidance are **written for this repository by AI**. They are not
> official Pearson questions and the guidance is not an official mark scheme.

## 1. Safety features (2.2)

1. State why the fuse is connected in the live wire. (1)
2. A washing machine has a metal case. Explain how the earth wire and the fuse together protect the user if the live wire touches the case. (4)
3. A hairdryer is double insulated. Explain what this means and why it needs no earth wire. (2)
4. Give two advantages of a circuit breaker over a fuse. (2)

## 2. Heating effect (2.3)

5. Explain why a current in a resistor makes its temperature rise. (3)
6. Name two appliances in the home that use the heating effect of a current. (2)

## 3. Power, fuses and energy (2.4, 2.5)

7. State the equation linking power, current and voltage. (1)
8. A 1.2 kW microwave oven is connected to the 230 V mains. Fuses of 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available. Calculate the normal current and choose the fuse. Explain your choice. (4)
9. Explain why a 13 A fuse should not be fitted to a 60 W lamp. (2)
10. A 12 V car heater draws 4.0 A for 20 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. (3)
11. A toaster transfers 99 000 J in 2 minutes from the 230 V mains. Calculate the current. (3)

## 4. a.c. and d.c. (2.6)

12. Describe the difference between the current supplied by the mains and the current supplied by a battery. (2)
13. Sketch voltage–time graphs for a d.c. supply and an a.c. supply. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. So that when it melts, the appliance is disconnected from the high voltage / the appliance is not left live (1).
2. The earth wire connects the metal case to earth (1); a fault gives a large current through the low-resistance earth wire (1); this current melts the fuse (1); the live supply is disconnected, so the case is not live and the user gets no shock (1).
3. The case is made of an insulator (plastic) and there are no exposed metal parts that could become live (1); the case can never become live, so there is nothing to earth (1).
4. It can be reset rather than replaced (1); it acts faster (1).
5. The current is a flow of electrons (1); the electrons collide with the ions in the metal (1); energy is transferred to the ions, which vibrate more, so the temperature rises (1).
6. Any two of: kettle, toaster, electric heater, hairdryer, iron, oven, immersion heater (1 each).
7. power = current × voltage (1).
8. *I* = 1200 ÷ 230 (1) = 5.2 A (1); 13 A fuse (1); it is the lowest rating above the normal current — a 5 A fuse would melt in normal use (1).
9. The normal current is only about 0.26 A (1); a 13 A fuse would not melt if a fault made the current too big, so the cable could overheat (1).
10. *t* = 1200 s (1); *E* = 4.0 × 12 × 1200 (1) = 57 600 J (1).
11. *t* = 120 s (1); *I* = 99 000 ÷ (230 × 120) (1) = 3.6 A (1).
12. Mains is a.c.: the current repeatedly reverses direction (1); a battery gives d.c.: the current flows in one direction only (1).
13. d.c.: horizontal line at a constant voltage (1); a.c.: a wave alternating above and below zero (1).
