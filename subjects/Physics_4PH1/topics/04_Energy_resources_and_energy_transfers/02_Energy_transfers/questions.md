## 1. Recall

1. Name the eight energy stores in the specification.
2. State the principle of conservation of energy.
3. Write the equation for efficiency.
4. Which type of surface is the best emitter of infrared radiation?
5. Why can convection not happen in a solid?

## 2. Understanding and explanation

1. Describe the energy transfers when a stretched rubber band fires a paper pellet into the air.
2. Explain why metals are better thermal conductors than plastics.
3. Explain why the heating element of an electric kettle is at the bottom.
4. Explain how a sea breeze forms on a sunny day.
5. A cup of tea is left on a table. Explain, in terms of emission and absorption of radiation, why
   its temperature falls and then stays constant.

## 3. Application

1. A lamp is supplied with 60 J each second and gives out 12 J of light. Calculate its efficiency.
2. An electric motor has an efficiency of 0.75. It is supplied with 800 J. How much energy is
   wasted?
3. On squared paper, draw a Sankey diagram for a television supplied with 200 J: 80 J light,
   30 J sound, the rest heating. Use 1 small square = 10 J.
4. Two identical cans, one matt black and one shiny silver, hold equal volumes of hot water.
   Predict which cools faster and explain why.

## 4. Practical

1. Describe how to use a Leslie cube to compare the infrared emitted by different surfaces. Name
   the independent, dependent and one control variable.
2. In the conduction experiment, state two variables that must be kept the same and why.
3. Describe how to show a convection current in water. Give one safety precaution.

## 5. Exam-style prompts

1. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how the vacuum flask keeps a drink hot. Refer to conduction, convection
   and radiation.
2. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how a radiator heats all the air in a room.
3. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how cavity wall insulation reduces energy loss from a house.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Not an official mark scheme — written to the specification wording.*

**Recall.** 1. chemical, kinetic, gravitational, elastic, thermal, magnetic, electrostatic,
nuclear. 2. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred between stores; the total
stays the same. 3. efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy output × 100%. 4. Dark, matt.
5. Particles in a solid cannot move from place to place, so they cannot flow and carry energy.

**Understanding.** 1. Elastic store of the band → mechanically → kinetic store of the pellet → as
it rises, gravitational store; some wasted by heating/sound to the thermal store of the
surroundings. 2. Metals have free electrons that move through the metal and carry energy quickly,
as well as vibrating particles. 3. Water heated by the element expands, becomes less dense and
rises; cooler, denser water sinks to the element; a convection current heats all the water.
4. The land warms faster than the sea; air over the land is heated, expands, becomes less dense and
rises; cooler, denser air from over the sea moves in to replace it. 5. The tea is hotter than the
room so emits more radiation than it absorbs → temperature falls; once at room temperature it emits
and absorbs at the same rate → constant.

**Application.** 1. 12 ÷ 60 × 100% = 20%. 2. Useful = 0.75 × 800 = 600 J; wasted = 200 J.
3. Input 20 squares wide; light 8, sound 3, heating 9 (bending away); outputs add to 20.
4. Matt black: it is a better emitter of infrared, so it transfers energy away faster.

**Practical.** 1. Fill the cube with hot water; place the detector a fixed distance from one face,
record the reading; repeat for each face quickly. IV: type of surface; DV: detector reading;
control: distance (or same detector, same water temperature). 2. Any two: rod length, rod
thickness, amount of wax, distance from the flame — so only the material affects the time.
3. Drop one crystal of potassium manganate(VII) to the bottom edge of a beaker of cold water; heat
gently below it; the colour rises, crosses the top and sinks on the other side. Precaution: handle
the crystal with tweezers (harmful, stains).

**Exam-style.** 1. Vacuum: no particles, so no conduction or convection across it (1); silvered
walls: poor emitters and reflect infrared back, reducing radiation (1); stopper: poor conductor and
stops convection/evaporation from the top (1). 2. Air next to the radiator is heated (1), expands
and becomes less dense, so rises (1); it moves across the ceiling, cools, becomes denser and sinks
(1); it flows back to the radiator — a convection current circulates through the room (1).
3. The foam fills the cavity so air cannot circulate — no convection current (1); it traps air in
small pockets (1); air is a poor conductor, so conduction is reduced (1).
