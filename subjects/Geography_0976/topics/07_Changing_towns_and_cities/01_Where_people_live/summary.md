# Where people live — Summary

Specification points 7.1.1–7.1.2 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Urban growth rates (World Bank, 2024):** low income **4.0%** a year · lower-middle 2.3% ·
  upper-middle 0.9% · high income **0.8%**. Fastest region sub-Saharan Africa (3.6%); slowest Europe
  (EU 0.5%).
- **Urbanisation curve:** slow → steep → flat. Growth is fastest on the steep middle — where LICs are now
  (35–42% urban). HICs (80% urban) are on the flat top.
- **Why LICs grow fast and HICs slowly:** share already urban · natural increase · economic change
  (industrialisation vs post-industrial) · rural push · direction of movement (rural → urban vs
  counter-urbanisation) · government policy.
- **Two causes of rapid urban growth in LICs:** rural-to-urban migration **+** natural increase.

| | Push (rural) | Pull (city) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social | few schools, clinics, no piped water | better schools, hospitals, services; family there |
| Economic | low farm incomes, land shortage, mechanisation | more jobs, higher wages, remittances |
| Environmental | drought, desertification, soil erosion, floods | more reliable food and water |
| Political | conflict, persecution, rural neglect | safety; investment concentrated in the city |

- **Natural increase in the city:** migrants are young adults → many births; health care, clean water →
  death rate falls faster than birth rate.

## Definitions

**Urban growth** — the increase in the number of people living in towns and cities.

**Urbanisation** — the increase in the proportion of a country's population living in urban areas.

**Counter-urbanisation** — the movement of people out of cities to smaller towns and rural areas.

**Push factor** — a reason that makes people want to leave where they live. **Pull factor** — a reason
that attracts people to a new place.

## Common traps

- Urban growth = number; urbanisation = proportion.
- Forgetting natural increase as a cause.
- "No jobs in the countryside" is a **push**, not a pull.
