## Answers and marking guidance

1. Items a business owns and expects to keep for more than one year, e.g. property, machinery.
2. Debts a business must pay within one year, e.g. trade payables, overdraft.
3. Revenue minus cost of sales.
4. E.g. inventory of food / cash in the tills / money owed by customers (trade receivables).
5. 2,400 − 1,560 (1) = £840,000 (1).
6. 840 − 610 (1) = £230,000 (1).
7. It shows what the business owns and owes on a date (1) → so its liquidity can be judged by
   comparing current assets with current liabilities (1) → which helps a bank decide whether to lend
   or suppliers whether to give credit (1).
8. Gross profit is before other operating expenses are deducted (1) → if overheads such as rent
   and salaries are high (1) → most of the gross profit is used up, leaving little operating profit
   (1).
9. Look for in-context chains, e.g. compares operating profit with previous years → rising profit
   shows the existing stores are successful → suggests demand for a new store; and/or retained
   profit available → can fund the store without borrowing → no interest costs; or cost of sales
   rising → margins squeezed → store may add less profit than expected.
