# The market — Questions

Specification points 4.2.1–4.2.3 · both papers

## 1. Recall

1. Define the term *market share*. (1)
2. Define the term *niche marketing*. (1)
3. State two ways a market can be segmented. (2)

## 2. Explain (3 marks each — context not required)

4. Explain one advantage for a business of being market orientated.
5. Explain one disadvantage for a business of mass marketing.
6. Explain one benefit to a business of using market segmentation.

## 3. Calculate and apply

7. A bakery chain's sales were £1.8m. Total sales in its market were £12m. Calculate its
   market share. (2)
8. Next year the market grows to £15m and the bakery's sales rise to £2.1m. Calculate the new
   market share and state what has happened to its position. (3)
9. A local gym faces a new low-cost rival. State one way the gym could respond. (1)
10. A car manufacturer is considering (1) targeting a niche market for luxury electric cars or
    (2) mass marketing a low-price model. Justify which one it should choose. (9)

## Marking guidance

1. One business's sales as a percentage of total sales in the market.
2. Selling to a small, specific segment of a market with particular needs.
3. Any two: location, demographics, lifestyle, income, age.
4. Identify (products based on research into customer needs) → customers want the product →
   higher sales / lower risk of failure.
5. Identify (many competitors) → pressure to cut prices → lower profit margins. Or: mass-media
   promotion is costly → higher costs.
6. Identify (marketing mix matched to a segment) → customers' needs met more closely → more
   sales/loyalty; or promotion only to target market → less wasted spending.
7. (1.8 ÷ 12) × 100 (1) = **15%** (1).
8. (2.1 ÷ 15) × 100 = **14%** (2). Sales rose but share fell — rivals grew faster (1).
9. E.g. improve customer service or classes; loyalty scheme; lower membership price; promote
   a feature the rival lacks. Must fit a gym.
10. Level-marked. Niche: less competition, premium price, higher margins, fits a luxury brand;
    but small sales volume, relies on one segment. Mass: high volume, economies of scale;
    but intense price competition, costly promotion, lower margins. Judgement in context
    (e.g. brand image, resources, what rivals do).
