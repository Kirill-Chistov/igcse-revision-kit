# Motivation and rewards — Summary

Specification points 2.4.1–2.4.2.

- **Why motivate:** attract applicants; retain staff (lower labour turnover → lower recruitment
  and training costs); productivity (more output, fewer mistakes, lower unit cost).
- **Taylor:** money motivates; simple repetitive tasks, supervision, piece rate. Weakness: boring
  work, ignores other needs.
- **Maslow** (bottom → top): physiological (pay) → safety (job security, safe conditions) →
  social (teamwork) → esteem (promotion, praise) → self-actualisation (challenge, enrichment).
  Lowest unmet need motivates.
- **Herzberg:** hygiene factors (pay, conditions, policies, supervision, colleagues, security)
  only prevent dissatisfaction; motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work
  itself, promotion, growth) motivate.

## Methods (+ / −)

- **Remuneration** (wage / salary): attracts, meets basic needs / costly, short-lived.
- **Bonus** (target met): rewards performance / costly, quality may fall.
- **Commission** (per sale): cost linked to sales / pressure selling, uncertain income.
- **Promotion:** keeps good staff, esteem / few posts, others demotivated.
- **Fringe benefits** (discount, car, insurance): staff feel valued / cost, not valued by all.
- **Job rotation** (same level, different tasks): variety, cover / no extra responsibility.
- **Job enrichment** (more responsibility): lasting motivation / training, stress.
- **Autonomy** (freedom to decide): trust, creativity / mistakes, inconsistency.
- Financial = costly, short-term; non-financial = cheaper, longer-lasting.

**Traps:** rotation ≠ enrichment; bonus ≠ commission; pay is a hygiene factor.
