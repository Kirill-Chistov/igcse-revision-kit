# Selective breeding — Questions

**Specification points 5.10–5.11** · Papers 1 and 2

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme. No
Biology past papers are stored in the repository yet.

## Questions

1. **[4 marks]** *Describe* how a farmer could use selective breeding to produce cows with a high
   milk yield.
2. **[4 marks]** A plant breeder has one variety of wheat with a high yield and another that is
   resistant to a fungal disease. *Describe* how she could produce a variety with both
   characteristics.
3. **[2 marks]** *Explain* why selective breeding must be carried out over many generations.
4. **[2 marks]** *Explain* one disadvantage of selective breeding.
5. **[2 marks]** Give two characteristics that farmers selectively breed for in (a) crop plants,
   (b) farm animals.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Choose a cow with a high milk yield (1) and a bull whose mother / female offspring had a high
   milk yield (1); breed them (1); select the female offspring with the highest milk yield and
   breed from them (1); repeat over many generations (1). Max 4.
2. Cross the two varieties / transfer pollen from one to the other (1); grow the seeds (1); select
   offspring with both high yield and disease resistance (1); breed these together and repeat for
   many generations (1).
3. Each generation only increases the proportion of offspring with the desired alleles (1); it
   takes many generations until all offspring show the characteristic (1).
4. Reduced genetic variation (1), so all may be killed by a new disease / cannot cope with a change
   in environment (1). **or** Inbreeding (1) so harmful recessive characteristics / health problems
   appear (1).
5. (a) Any two: high yield, disease resistance, short stems, drought tolerance, better taste.
   (b) Any two: high milk yield, more meat, faster growth, more eggs, more wool, disease
   resistance, calm temperament.
