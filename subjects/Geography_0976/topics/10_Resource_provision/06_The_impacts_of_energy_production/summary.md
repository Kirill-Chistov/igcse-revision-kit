# The impacts of energy production — Summary

Specification points 10.6.1–10.6.4 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976

**Advantages and disadvantages (10.6.1)** — non-renewables: reliable, cheap, but finite and
polluting. Renewables: clean, never run out, but intermittent, site-specific, costly to install.
- Coal: cheap, reliable · most CO₂, acid rain. Oil: energy-dense, transport · spills, supply risk.
  Gas: half coal's CO₂ · methane, imports.
- Nuclear: ~no CO₂, reliable · waste, accidents (Chernobyl, Fukushima), cost.
- Biomass: renewable · competes with food. Fuelwood: free · deforestation, indoor smoke.
- Geothermal: reliable · plate boundaries only. HEP: reliable, water store · displaces people, cost.
- Solar: off-grid · night/cloud. Wind: cheap · intermittent, visual. Tidal: predictable · costly,
  habitats. Wave: potential · unproven, storms.

**Increasing supply (10.6.2)** — strategies: energy plans, renewable subsidies, exploration
licences, imports/pipelines. Techniques: deep-sea/Arctic drilling, fracking (high-pressure water,
sand, chemicals crack shale), new wind/solar/HEP/geothermal/nuclear, better recovery, storage,
off-grid solar, micro-hydro, biogas.

**Managing supplies — evaluation (10.6.3)** — renewables (sustainable; intermittent) · fracking
(fast, not sustainable) · nuclear (low-carbon; waste) · diversification (security) · efficiency
and conservation (cheapest, most sustainable) · carbon taxes/targets (fuel poverty risk) ·
cookstoves/biogas in LICs. Judgement: cut demand + renewables with storage.

**Sweden (10.6.4)** — 55–69°N; high energy use per person (northerly, high incomes); no
significant fossil fuels; 2nd lowest CO₂ per GDP and per person (IEA).
- Mix 2022: hydro 28.5%, other renewables 24.6%, oil 21.9%, nuclear 20.2%, coal 3.1%, gas 1.3%.
  Nuclear + hydro = 80% of electricity. Use per person 229.8 → 215.7 GJ (2010–2022).
- Impacts: fast-flowing rivers → HEP; forests 63% → bioenergy, district heating, transport
  biofuels, CHP 9%; nuclear 3 stations / 8 reactors, controversial; wind ≈11%, rising; solar
  limited by latitude (1,090 MW, 2020); heat pumps cut grid demand; power made in north, people in
  south → transmission corridors.
- Management: carbon tax since 1991 (polluter pays); green-electricity certificates; ~500 district
  heating schemes; efficiency (50% more efficient by 2030); links to Norway, Poland, Germany, gas
  from Denmark; storage for seasonal demand. Targets: 100% renewable electricity by 2040,
  zero-carbon economy by 2045. Since the 1980s: +25% people, GDP doubled, less energy used.
