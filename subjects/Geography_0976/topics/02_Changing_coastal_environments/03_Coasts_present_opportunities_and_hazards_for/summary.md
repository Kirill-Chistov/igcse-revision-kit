## Opportunities and hazards

- **Opportunities:** ports and trade, fishing, tourism, industry and energy (cooling water, offshore oil/gas, wind/wave/tidal), flat fertile land, quality of life.
- **Hazards:** erosion, flooding (surges, high tides, sea-level rise), tropical storms, tsunamis.

## Coastal management

- **Strategy:** hold the line / managed retreat / do nothing — value at risk vs cost. **Techniques** = methods.
- **Hard:** sea wall (reflects energy; strong but very costly, scours beach) · groynes (trap drift sediment; starve beaches further along) · rock armour (absorbs energy; cheaper) · gabions (cheap, short life).
- **Soft:** beach nourishment (natural, needs repeating) · dune regeneration (marram, cheap) · managed retreat (salt marsh absorbs energy; land lost).
- **Judge:** value vs cost; cause or symptom; moves problem?; copes with sea-level rise? Soft = more sustainable.

## Tropical storms

- Winds ≥ 119 km/h: **hurricane** (Atlantic, NE Pacific), **typhoon** (NW Pacific), **cyclone** (Indian Ocean, S Pacific).
- Sea ≥ 27 °C; 5°–30° N and S (no spin at Equator); move west then poleward; weaken over land/cool sea.
- Hazards: winds, heavy rain (floods, landslides), **storm surge** (low pressure + onshore wind → coastal flooding).
- Impacts: deaths (mostly drowning), homelessness, disease; repair costs, crops, tourism; erosion, salt on farmland. Positive: rebuilding jobs, rain ends droughts. LICs more deaths, HICs higher costs.
- **4Ps:** prediction (satellites, warnings — most lives saved) · preparation (kits, drills — cheap) · planning (zoning, codes, shelters) · protection (surge barriers, strong buildings, mangroves — costly). Bangladesh: ~300,000 deaths (1970) → ~3,400 (Sidr, 2007).

## Coral reefs and mangroves

- **Reefs:** >18 °C, shallow, clear, salty; 30°N–30°S; not near big river mouths or cold west coasts. Coral Triangle, GBR, Caribbean, Red Sea.
- **Mangroves:** sheltered muddy tropical coasts/estuaries. Indonesia, Brazil, Nigeria, Australia, Sundarbans.
- **Importance:** biodiversity, fish nurseries, tourism, coastal protection; mangroves also filter sediment, store carbon, give timber/fuel.
- **Threats:** reefs — bleaching, acidification, run-off, destructive fishing, tourism; mangroves — shrimp farms, development, fuelwood.
- **Bleaching:** +1–2 °C for weeks → algae expelled → white → starves → dies.
- **Management:** protected areas/zoning, fishing controls, less run-off, mooring buoys, restoration; mangrove replanting, community management. Most sustainable = local people earn from a healthy ecosystem; reefs also need global emission cuts.

## 2.3.7 USA Eastern Seaboard

- East (Atlantic) coast; ~40% of USA population near the coast.
- **Causes:** sea-level rise +47 cm NYC since 1925; subsidence ~10 mm/yr; low relief.
- **Impacts:** Massachusetts beaches almost gone in places; Louisiana (Gulf coast) loses ~40 ha wetland/day; flood risk; Hurricane Andrew 1992 (Cat 5): 65 deaths, >$27 bn.
- **Management:** sea walls (less sustainable) · beach nourishment (more sustainable, repeated) · raised roads (mid) · Hurricane Loss Mitigation Program (mid, impacts not causes) · trap bags/sandbags (less, temporary).

## 2.3.8 Great Barrier Reef

- Off Queensland, NE Australia; ~2,300 km long.
- **Importance:** 9,000 species (400 coral, 1,500 fish, ~4,000 mollusc); >2 million tourists/yr, >$8 bn; >60,000 jobs.
- **Threats:** climate change (bleaching); water quality (sediment, acidification, run-off, pesticides); coastal development; crown-of-thorns starfish (>90% of a reef's coral).
- **Management:** zoning (green no-take zones ~33%) · Reef 2050 Plan (Australian + Queensland governments) · starfish removal by hand. Zoning and Reef 2050 long-term; starfish control treats the symptom.
