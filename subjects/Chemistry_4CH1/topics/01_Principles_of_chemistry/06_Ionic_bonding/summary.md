# Ionic bonding — Summary

Specification points 1.37–1.43 · Topic 1 Principles of chemistry.

## Essential facts

- Metals **lose** outer electrons → positive ions; non-metals **gain** → negative ions; both reach a
  full outer shell.
- Charges: Group 1 +1, Group 2 +2, Group 3 +3, Group 5 −3, Group 6 −2, Group 7 −1.
  Ag⁺, H⁺, NH₄⁺ · Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Pb²⁺, Zn²⁺ · Fe³⁺ · OH⁻, NO₃⁻ · CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻.
- Formula: charges cancel to zero; brackets round compound ions used more than once
  (Ca(OH)₂, (NH₄)₂SO₄, Al₂(SO₄)₃).
- Dot-and-cross: outer shells only, arrows for transfer, ions in square brackets with charges.
- **Ionic bond**: strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
- Giant lattice: many strong attractions in all directions → lots of energy to overcome → high mp
  and bp. Higher ion charges → stronger attraction (MgO > NaCl).
- Conduct when **molten or aqueous** (ions free to move), not when solid (ions fixed).

## Common traps

- The ions move, not electrons. Ionic compounds have no intermolecular forces.
