# Electromagnetism — Questions

Specification points 6.8–6.14 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1. Questions on
6.9P, 6.10P and 6.11P are Paper 2 only.

## 1. Fields and electromagnets (6.8–6.10P)

1. State what is produced around a wire when a current flows in it. (1)
2. **[P2]** Draw the magnetic field pattern around a long straight wire carrying a current into the page. (3)
3. **[P2]** Draw the magnetic field pattern of a current-carrying solenoid. (3)
4. **[P2]** Describe the construction of an electromagnet. (3)
5. **[P2]** Explain why the core of an electromagnet is made of soft iron. (2)

## 2. The force on a current (6.11P–6.14)

6. **[P2]** State the condition for a charged particle moving in a magnetic field to experience no force. (1)
7. Explain why a current-carrying wire placed between the poles of a magnet experiences a force. (3)
8. A wire carries a current **into** the page between a N pole on the left and a S pole on the right. Use the left-hand rule to state the direction of the force on the wire. (1)
9. State **two** ways to increase the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field. (2)
10. The current and the magnetic field are both reversed. State the effect on the force. (1)

## 3. Motors and loudspeakers (6.12)

11. Explain why the coil of a simple d.c. motor rotates when a current flows. (3)
12. Explain the purpose of the split-ring commutator in a d.c. motor. (3)
13. Give **two** ways to make a d.c. motor turn faster. (2)
14. Explain how a loudspeaker produces sound from an alternating current. (4)
15. Suggest what happens to the sound from a loudspeaker when the frequency of the a.c. is increased. (1)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. A magnetic field (1).
2. Concentric circles centred on the wire (1); spacing increases with distance from the wire (1); arrows clockwise (1).
3. Parallel, equally spaced lines inside the solenoid (1); outside, lines loop from one end to the other like a bar magnet (1); consistent arrows, leaving the N end (1).
4. A coil of (insulated) wire / solenoid (1); wrapped around a (soft) iron core (1); connected to a (d.c.) supply with a switch (1).
5. Soft iron is easily magnetised, so it makes the field much stronger (1); it loses its magnetism when the current is switched off (1).
6. It moves parallel to the magnetic field (lines) (1).
7. The current produces a magnetic field around the wire (1); this interacts with the magnet's field — adding on one side, cancelling on the other (1); the wire is pushed towards the weaker side / there is a force at right angles to the field and current (1).
8. Downwards (field → right, current into page: force down) (1).
9. Increase the current (1); use a stronger magnet / stronger field (1).
10. The force stays in the same direction (1).
11. Current in the two sides of the coil flows in opposite directions (1); so the forces on them are in opposite directions (one up, one down) (1); this gives a turning effect / moment about the axle (1).
12. It reverses the current in the coil (1) every half turn (1); so the forces keep turning the coil in the same direction (1).
13. Larger current (1); stronger magnet / more turns on the coil (1).
14. The a.c. flows in a coil attached to the cone, in the field of a permanent magnet (1); there is a force on the coil (1); the force reverses each time the current reverses, so the coil and cone vibrate (1); the cone makes the air vibrate, producing sound waves (1).
15. The pitch of the sound is higher (1).
