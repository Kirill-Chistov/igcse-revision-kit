## 1. Types of training

**Definition — Training:** developing employees' skills and knowledge so they can do their
job well.

| Type | What it is | Main purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Induction** | introductory training for **new** employees when they start | settle in and work safely quickly: meet colleagues, learn the layout, rules and procedures, health and safety, the business's aims |
| **On-the-job** | training **while doing the job** at the workplace, e.g. being shown and coached by an experienced employee | learn the exact skills of this job in real working conditions |
| **Off-the-job** | training **away from the job** — at a college, training centre, conference or online course | learn new, specialist or general skills (often a qualification) from expert trainers |

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Induction** | new staff productive sooner; fewer mistakes and accidents; feel welcome → less likely to leave early | delays the start of real work; costs trainers' and managers' time; too much information at once may be forgotten |
| **On-the-job** | cheap (no fees or travel); trainee produces while learning; skills fit the job exactly | trainer's own output falls; trainee's mistakes affect real customers or products; may copy the trainer's bad habits; no new ideas from outside |
| **Off-the-job** | expert trainers → high-quality training, new ideas, qualifications; no disruption at the workplace | expensive (fees, travel); no output while away; skills may not fit the job exactly; newly qualified staff may leave for better-paid jobs |

## 2. Compliance with health and safety laws

Health and safety laws require employers to provide a **safe working environment**: safe
premises and equipment, protective clothing where needed, risk assessments, first aid, and
**training** so employees know safe procedures (e.g. fire drills, safe use of machinery, handling
hazardous substances). Employees must follow these procedures. Health and safety training is
usually part of induction.

:::: grid
::: advantages | Complying — benefits
- Fewer accidents and injuries → less absence and lost output.
- Avoids fines, legal action, compensation claims or closure.
- Good reputation with customers and job applicants.
- Staff feel safe → better motivation *(→ 2.4, Maslow's safety needs)*.
:::
::: disadvantages | Complying — costs
- Training time and trainers' costs.
- Safety equipment, protective clothing and changes to premises.
- Administration: risk assessments and records.
:::
::::

## 3. Benefits and limitations of training

:::: grid
::: advantages | Benefits
- **Business:** more skilled staff → higher productivity and quality, fewer mistakes and less
  waste; better customer service; staff can use new technology and methods; multi-skilled
  staff can cover for each other.
- **Business:** staff feel valued → motivation, lower labour turnover → lower recruitment costs.
- **Workers:** new skills and qualifications → better chance of promotion and higher pay; more
  confidence, job satisfaction and job security; safer work.
:::
::: disadvantages | Limitations
- Cost: course fees, trainers, wages paid while training.
- Lost output and disruption while staff train.
- Trained staff may be poached by competitors or leave for better pay → the business loses its
  investment.
- Training may not improve performance if it is poorly planned or does not match the job.
:::
::::

::: traps
**Induction** is only for new starters. **On-the-job** and **off-the-job** describe *where*
training happens and apply to any employee — off-the-job can even be in the business's own
training room, as long as it is away from the employee's normal work.
:::
