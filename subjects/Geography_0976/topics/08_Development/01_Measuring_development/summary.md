## Essential facts and ideas

- **Economic indicators** (wealth): GDP, GNP, GNI — always **per capita** to compare countries.
- **Social indicators** (how people live): literacy, life expectancy, infant mortality, calorie
  intake, doctors per 1,000 people.
- **Composite**: HDI = life expectancy + years of schooling + GNI per capita (PPP), scored 0–1.
  Very high ≥ 0.800; low < 0.550.
- Higher = more developed, **except** infant mortality and people per doctor (lower = more developed).
- Indicators are **positively correlated** but not perfectly: life expectancy rises steeply with
  income in LICs, then levels off. Anomalies: South Africa (upper-MIC, life expectancy 66), USA
  (richer than Japan, shorter lives).
- Usefulness: economic = easy to compare but an average, misses informal economy and inequality;
  single social = one aspect only, unreliable data in LICs, some max out in HICs; HDI = fuller but
  still an average and leaves out freedom, equality, environment. **Use several, incl. a composite.**
- Standard of living ⊂ quality of life. They can diverge (rich but stressed and polluted; poor but
  safe and close-knit).

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| GDP | value of goods and services produced within a country in a year |
| GNP | value of goods and services produced by a country's people and firms, at home and abroad, in a year |
| GNI | total income of a country's people and firms, incl. from abroad, in a year |
| Infant mortality rate | deaths before age 1 per 1,000 live births per year |
| HDI | composite 0–1 index of life expectancy, education and income |
| PPP | money adjusted for local cost of living |
| Standard of living | material wealth: income and the goods and services a household can afford |
| Quality of life | overall well-being, incl. non-material factors (health, safety, freedom, environment); partly subjective |

## Common traps

- GDP = within the borders; GNP/GNI = the nation's people and firms wherever they are.
- A national average hides inequality — say so in any "evaluate" answer.
- Standard of living is not the same as quality of life.
