# 6.2 Robotics — practice

Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Computer Science 0984 · points 6.2.1–6.2.3 · Paper 1

## Questions

1. State what is meant by robotics. **[2]**
2. Give **three** examples of robots. **[3]**
3. Describe the **three** characteristics of a robot. **[6]**
4. A website has a chatbot that answers customers' questions. Explain why the chatbot is not a
   robot. **[2]**
5. A car manufacturer uses robots to paint car bodies. Describe **two** advantages and **two**
   disadvantages of using robots for this task. **[4]**
6. Describe **two** roles robots can perform in agriculture. **[2]**
7. A hospital is considering using robots to take blood samples from patients. Give **one**
   advantage and **one** disadvantage. **[2]**
8. A family buys a robot vacuum cleaner. Describe how it uses sensors, a microprocessor and
   actuators to clean a room without hitting furniture. **[4]**
9. Explain **two** disadvantages of using autonomous (driverless) buses. **[4]**
10. Identify **one** role of robots in entertainment. **[1]**

## Marking guidance

1. A branch of computer science (1) that incorporates the design, construction and operation of
   robots (1).
2. Any three, e.g. robot arm in a factory (welding / painting); robot vacuum cleaner or lawn mower;
   drone; autonomous car; surgical robot.
3. 2 marks each (characteristic + description): mechanical structure / framework — physical body
   (arms, joints, wheels, end effector) that lets it move / carry out the task; electrical
   components — sensors to sense the surroundings, a microprocessor to process data and decide,
   actuators to move the parts; programmable — controlled by a stored program that can be changed
   so it can do different tasks.
4. It has no mechanical structure / physical body (1), so it has only one of the three
   characteristics / it is software only (1).
5. Advantages (1 each, max 2): works 24/7; consistent, even finish every time; workers not exposed
   to paint fumes; cheaper in the long run (no wages); higher productivity. Disadvantages (1 each,
   max 2): expensive to buy / set up / maintain; cannot deal with non-standard situations
   (e.g. a damaged panel); job losses; deskilling of painters.
6. Any two: harvesting / picking only ripe crops; removing weeds; planting seeds; drones spraying
   fertiliser; monitoring crop health.
7. Advantage, e.g. staff not exposed to infectious patients / staff freed for skilled work / more
   accurate at finding a vein (1). Disadvantage, e.g. a malfunction could injure a patient /
   very expensive / patients may not trust it (1).
8. One mark per point, max 4: proximity / infra-red sensors or cameras detect objects; data sent to
   microprocessor; microprocessor decides whether an object is too close; sends signal to
   actuators / motors to change direction or stop; process repeats continuously so the whole room
   is covered.
9. Two disadvantages, 2 each: control system could be hacked (1) so the bus could be taken over /
   passengers put at risk (1); software fault could cause an accident (1) because there is no
   driver to take over (1); passengers may be reluctant to use them (1) so fewer people use the
   service (1); bus drivers lose their jobs (1) causing unemployment (1); sensors may fail in bad
   weather / when dirty (1) giving wrong data (1).
10. e.g. theme-park robots that interact with visitors; robots controlling cameras in filming;
    special effects / stunts in films.
