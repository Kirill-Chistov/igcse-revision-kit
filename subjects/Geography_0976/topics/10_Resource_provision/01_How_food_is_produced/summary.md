# How food is produced — Summary

Specification points 10.1.1–10.1.2 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976

**Farming types (10.1.1)** — every farm gets two labels: *why* + *what* (e.g. commercial arable).

- **Subsistence** — food for the farmer's family; little or no surplus. **Commercial** — produced to sell for profit.
- **Arable** — crops. **Pastoral** — animals (meat, milk, wool, eggs). **Mixed** — both.
- **Soil-less** (indoors, water recycled): **hydroponics** — roots in nutrient solution;
  **aeroponics** — roots in air, misted with nutrients; **aquaponics** — fish waste → nitrates feed
  the plants, plants clean the water (outputs: plants + fish).
  High yields all year, little water, no farmland needed · but high capital, energy and skill.

**Farming systems (10.1.2)** — inputs → processes → outputs, with feedback.

- Inputs: **physical** (climate, relief, soil) and **human** (labour, capital, machinery, seed, fertiliser).
- Processes: ploughing, sowing, spraying, irrigating, harvesting, rearing, milking.
- Outputs: crops, livestock products, waste (manure), profit or family food.
- Feedback: profit reinvested, manure to fields, seed saved.
- Subsistence = mainly family labour, output eaten; commercial = high capital, output sold.
