## Marking guidance

*Written for this repository — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. Communication between the business and people/organisations outside it (e.g. customers,
   suppliers).
2. Any two: email, text/instant messaging, video conferencing, intranet, website, social media.
3. Any one: not listening, information overload, language/cultural differences, distance/time
   zones, distrust.
4. Any one: overload/ignored, tone misread, needs IT, no instant feedback.
5. Identify (face-to-face contact without travel) → link (saves travel time and cost) → link
   (e.g. managers in different countries can meet more often, so decisions are faster).
6. Identify a barrier and matching removal (e.g. jargon → plain language), then two linked
   consequences (message understood → fewer mistakes → lower costs).
7. Barrier (time zones / language differences) developed in context (e.g. managers in different
   countries cannot talk at the same time, so instructions are delayed).
8. Email: fast, cheap, same message and record for all 3 000 staff; but may be ignored or misread,
   no feedback. Meetings: two-way, questions answered, better understanding; but slower, costly
   in managers' time, message may differ between shops. Judgement must use the context
   (e.g. 3 000 staff and a policy needing consistency → email, perhaps followed up by managers).
