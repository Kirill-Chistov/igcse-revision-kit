## Answers and marking guidance

1. Any three: climate change; fishing (krill / toothfish); resource exploitation / mining; tourism;
   research stations / pollution. (3)
2. Visitors concentrate at the few ice-free sites on the Peninsula (1) where wildlife breeds (1);
   trampling damages slow-growing mosses and lichens (1); disturbance of penguins and seals (1); seeds
   carried on clothing introduce non-native species (1); fuel spills / ship emissions (1). Max 4.
3. Krill are eaten by fish, penguins, seals and whales (1); removing large amounts reduces their food
   (1); fishing is concentrated near the Peninsula where predators feed (1); the 620,000 t limit has
   been reached (1). Max 3.
4. Warming reduces sea ice (1), so fewer ice algae and fewer krill (1), so less food for penguins,
   seals and whales (1); species that breed on sea ice lose their platform (1); ocean acidification
   harms shelled plankton (1); non-native species can survive (1). Max 4.
5. Coal, iron ore and possibly oil lie under the ice / shelf (1); the mining ban can be reviewed from
   2048, and demand for resources is rising (1).
6. Ice shelves break up (1), so the glaciers behind them flow faster into the sea (1), raising global
   sea level (1), which floods low-lying coasts and increases erosion elsewhere (1).
7. Ice and snow have a high albedo and reflect sunlight (1); darker sea and rock absorb more (1),
   heating the area further and melting more ice — a positive feedback (1).
8. Level-marked. Local: biodiversity loss, food web shrinks, slow recovery, non-native species.
   Regional: fewer krill affect whales, seals and penguins across the Southern Ocean. Global: sea-level
   rise, faster warming (albedo), lost scientific record, lost fishing and tourism income. 5–6: both
   scales, developed and linked; 3–4: both scales, partly developed; 1–2: list.
9. Strategy: an agreement or plan, e.g. the Antarctic Treaty (1). Technique: a practical method, e.g. an
   environmental impact assessment or a catch limit (1).
10. Antarctica used for peace and science only (1); military activity, nuclear explosions and waste
    banned (1); territorial claims frozen (1); scientific results shared (1). Max 3.
11. Every activity is assessed before it starts (1); damaging plans can be changed or stopped (1);
    forces planning for waste, fuel and wildlife (1). Limitation: judged by the operator's own
    government / rarely refused / small impacts add up unnoticed (1).
12. Sets catch limits for krill and toothfish (1) that leave enough food for predators — an ecosystem
    approach (1); monitors vessels / measures against seabird bycatch (1); marine protected areas such
    as the Ross Sea (1). Max 3.
13. Strengths: limits of 100 people ashore, one ship per site, no landings from ships over 500 (1);
    operators comply because their business depends on a pristine Antarctica (1). Weaknesses: largely
    self-regulated (1); total numbers over 100,000 and rising (1). Judgement required for full marks.
14. Level-marked. Agree: no mining, no conflict, waste cleaned up, seabird bycatch cut, whales
    recovering, preventive so sustainable. Disagree: consensus blocks new MPAs, krill limit reached,
    tourism growing, enforcement is national, climate change cannot be controlled by any Antarctic
    agreement. 6–7: balanced, uses evidence, clear judgement including sustainability; 3–5: some
    evaluation; 1–2: description only.
