# Populations grow and decline — Summary

Specification points 6.1.1–6.1.4 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Trend:** slow growth until ~1800; death rates fell while birth rates stayed high → population explosion. 1 bn 1804 → 8 bn 2022. Growth rate peaked ~**2.2% in the 1960s**, now **below 1%**: still rising, but more slowly. UN: peak ~**10.3 bn mid-2080s**.
- **Pattern:** fastest in **sub-Saharan Africa** (~2.5% a year, fertility > 4); slowing in South Asia and Latin America; **natural decrease** in Europe, Russia, Japan, South Korea, China. 63 countries have already peaked.
- **Population change = (births − deaths) + (immigrants − emigrants).** Niger: 41 − 9 = 32 per 1,000 = 3.2% a year.
- **High birth rate:** high infant mortality, children as farm labour and old-age support, girls leave school and marry young, no contraception, pro-large-family beliefs. Reverse each for a low birth rate.
- **Death rate falls** with food, clean water, sanitation, vaccination, healthcare; **rises** with war, famine, disease — or ageing.
- **Migration** can outweigh natural change: Germany (natural decrease since 1972, still grew); Bulgaria (9.0 m → 6.4 m, emigration + natural decrease).
- **Anti-natalist:** contraception, education of girls, later marriage, rewards/penalties, coercion. **Pro-natalist:** birth payments, child benefit, parental leave, childcare, housing help.
- **Evidence:** China one-child 1980–2015 — 2.8 → ~1.6, but fall began before it; 118 boys : 100 girls, ageing; births still fell after it ended. Iran (voluntary, 1989) — 5.2 → 2.0 by 2000. France — near 2.0 for years, now 1.56. South Korea — over US$200 bn, still 0.72 (2023).
- **Judgement:** anti-natalist policies work (coercion fast but harmful; voluntary + education just as fast); pro-natalist policies are costly and only slow the fall; development moves birth rates more than policy.
- **DTM:** Stage 1 high BR and DR · 2 DR falls, rapid growth · 3 BR falls, growth slows · 4 both low · 5 BR below DR, decline.
- **DTM strengths:** simple, comparable, predicts and aids planning. **Limitations:** Eurocentric, ignores migration, policy, culture, war and disease, no time scale.

## Definitions

**Birth rate / death rate** — births / deaths per 1,000 people per year.

**Natural increase** — birth rate minus death rate.

**Fertility rate** — average number of children per woman; replacement level 2.1.

**Anti-natalist / pro-natalist policy** — a policy to reduce / raise the birth rate.
