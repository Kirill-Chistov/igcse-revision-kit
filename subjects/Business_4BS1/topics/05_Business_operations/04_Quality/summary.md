## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Quality | how well a good or service meets customers' needs and expectations — fit for purpose, reliable, fault-free, every time |
| Quality control | inspecting products at the end (or set stages) to find and remove those below standard |
| TQM | every employee responsible for quality at every stage; right first time, zero defects |
| Competitive advantage | something that makes a product more attractive to customers than rivals' products |

## Essential facts

- Goods: fit for purpose, reliable, fault-free, safe. Services: on time, good staff, clean surroundings. Both: consistent.
- QC: catches faults before customers / faults already made (waste), inspector cost, workers leave quality to inspectors.
- TQM: prevents faults, less waste, strong reputation, motivation / expensive training, needs everyone's commitment, slow to pay off.
- Quality → reputation and loyalty (repeat sales) · premium price (higher margin) · fewer faults and returns (lower costs) → **competitive advantage**.
- Poor quality → returns, refunds, recalls, bad reviews → lost sales and reputation.

## Common traps

- High quality ≠ expensive.
- QC inspects faults out; TQM builds quality in.
