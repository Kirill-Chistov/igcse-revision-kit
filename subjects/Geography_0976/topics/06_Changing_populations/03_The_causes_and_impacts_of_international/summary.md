# The causes and impacts of international migration — Summary

Specification points 6.3.1–6.3.5 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **304 million** international migrants (2024), 3.7% of the world. **42.7 million refugees** (end 2024): two-thirds in a neighbouring country, 73% in low- and middle-income countries.
- **Voluntary:** economic migrant. **Forced:** asylum seeker → refugee if protection is granted.
- **Push / pull** by category — economic: unemployment, low wages / jobs, higher wages · social: poor services / better healthcare and education, family there · political: war, persecution / safety, freedom · environmental: drought, floods, hazards / safer environment.
- Economic migrants: pull matters most. Refugees: push; most go to the nearest safe country.
- **Migrant:** + income, safety, education; − dangerous journeys, exploitation, discrimination, separation.
- **Origin:** + **remittances** (US$685 bn in 2024, more than investment and aid combined; 45% of Tajikistan's GDP), less unemployment, returning skills; − **brain drain**, loss of young adults → ageing, split families.
- **Destination:** + fills labour shortages, taxes support ageing population, diversity; − pressure on housing and services, tension, integration costs.
- **Management:** points-based (chooses who, not how many — UK record ~1 million a year in 2023) · quotas and temporary visas (exploitation risk) · free movement (no control of numbers, drains poorer members) · border controls (diverts to dangerous routes) · agreements (EU–Turkey 2016: Greek sea arrivals 857,000 → 30,000, but depends on partner, camps) · resettlement and integration (fewer than 1 in 100 refugees resettled a year) · reducing push factors (only one tackling causes — most sustainable, but slow).
- **Judgement:** controls decide who enters but not why people move; best = legal routes for real shortages + integration + development and peace at origin.

## Definitions

**Economic migrant** — moves voluntarily to find work or higher wages.

**Asylum seeker** — has fled and applied for protection; decision pending.

**Refugee** — forced to flee persecution, war or violence; recognised and protected, cannot be sent back.

**Push / pull factor** — a reason to leave the origin / a reason to go to the destination.

**Remittances** — money migrants send home. **Brain drain** — emigration of skilled, educated people.
