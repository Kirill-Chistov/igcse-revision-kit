# Energy and voltage in circuits — Summary

Specification points 2.7–2.21 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1 · both papers.

## Definitions and equations (all recalled)

- **Current** = rate of flow of charge. **Q = I × t** (C, A, s).
- In metals the current is a flow of **negative electrons** (− to +, opposite to conventional current).
- **Voltage** = energy transferred per unit charge; **1 V = 1 J/C**. **E = Q × V** (J, C, V).
- **V = I × R** (V, A, Ω). Bigger resistance → smaller current for the same voltage.
- Ammeter in **series**; voltmeter in **parallel**.

## I–V graphs and the practical

- Wire/resistor (constant temperature): straight line through origin — constant *R*.
- Filament lamp: curve, less steep as *V* rises — hotter filament, *R* increases.
- Diode: current one way only; very high *R* in reverse; conducts forwards above ≈ 0.6 V.
- *R* = *V* ÷ *I* at a point (not the gradient).
- Practical: supply + variable resistor + ammeter + component in series, voltmeter across the component; ≥ 6 readings, repeat, reverse the supply for negative values; plot *I* against *V*; small currents to keep temperature constant; protective resistor with a diode.

## Components

- **LDR:** more light → lower *R*. **Thermistor:** hotter → lower *R*.
- Lamps and LEDs light only when a current flows — indicators. LED lights only in its forward direction.

## Series and parallel

- **Series:** same current everywhere; *V* = *V*₁ + *V*₂; *R* = *R*₁ + *R*₂; *I* = *V* ÷ *R*total. More voltage → more current; more components or bigger *R* → less current; reversed diode → none.
- **Parallel:** same voltage across each branch; *I* = *I*₁ + *I*₂. Current conserved at a junction because charge is not used up, created or stored there.
- **Domestic lighting is parallel:** full mains voltage to each lamp, independent switches, one failure doesn't affect the others. Series: all go out if one breaks, all dim when one is added.
