# Food production — Questions

**Specification points 5.1–5.9B** · Q9 is Paper 2 only (5.9B)

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme. No
Biology past papers are stored in the repository yet.

## Questions

1. **[3 marks]** *Describe* three ways a glasshouse can increase the yield of tomato plants.
2. **[3 marks]** A grower adds carbon dioxide to a glasshouse. The yield rises at first, then stops
   rising. *Explain* this.
3. **[2 marks]** *Explain* why raising the temperature of a glasshouse too high reduces yield.
4. **[2 marks]** *Explain* how adding nitrate fertiliser increases crop yield.
5. **[4 marks]** *Discuss* the use of biological control rather than pesticides to control aphids
   on a crop.
6. **[3 marks]** *Explain* the role of yeast in making bread.
7. **[3 marks]** *Explain* why milk goes thick and sour when Lactobacillus is added.
8. **[6 marks]** *Describe* an investigation into the effect of temperature on anaerobic respiration
   in yeast.
9. **[P2] [4 marks]** *Explain* how fish farmers increase the mass of fish they produce.
10. **[5 marks]** *Explain* why an industrial fermenter has (a) a water jacket, (b) a supply of
    sterile air, (c) a stirrer, (d) a pH probe, and (e) why it is sterilised before use.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Any three: heating / traps heat → higher temperature (1); artificial light → longer day (1);
   extra CO₂ from paraffin burners (1); pests kept out / biological control easier (1); water
   supplied (1). Each needs a link to faster photosynthesis or less damage.
2. CO₂ is a raw material / limiting factor of photosynthesis (1), so more CO₂ increases the rate
   and growth (1); then another factor, e.g. temperature or light, becomes limiting (1).
3. Enzymes (of photosynthesis) denature above the optimum (1), so the rate of photosynthesis falls
   and less glucose is made for growth (1).
4. Nitrate is used to make amino acids / proteins (1), so more growth (1). Accept: replaces minerals
   removed by harvesting.
5. For biological control, up to 3: specific to aphids / does not kill other insects; no chemical
   residues or accumulation in food chains; aphids do not become resistant; no need to reapply.
   Against, up to 3: slower; does not remove all aphids; predator may become a pest / eat other
   species. Max 4; both sides needed for full marks.
6. Yeast respires anaerobically (1) producing carbon dioxide (1); the gas bubbles make the dough
   rise (1). Accept: ethanol evaporates / yeast killed on baking.
7. Lactobacillus respires anaerobically / ferments lactose (1) to lactic acid (1); lowered pH
   makes milk proteins clot / thicken, and gives a sour taste (1).
8. Up to 6 from: yeast in (boiled, cooled) glucose solution (1); layer of oil to keep oxygen out (1);
   water bath at a range of temperatures, e.g. 20–60 °C (1); leave to reach temperature (1); count
   bubbles / measure gas volume over a set time (1); control same mass of yeast and volume and
   concentration of glucose (1); repeat and find mean (1); limewater shows gas is CO₂ (1).
9. Any four, each linked: keep water clean / aerated → oxygen for respiration, less disease (1);
   separate fish by size → no intraspecific predation (1); nets → no interspecific predation (1);
   antibiotics / vaccines / remove sick fish → less disease (1); remove waste → no toxic build-up
   (1); small, frequent, high-protein feeds → fast growth without polluting water (1); selectively
   breed fast-growing fish (1).
10. (a) Removes heat released by respiration, keeping the optimum temperature so enzymes are not
    denatured (1). (b) Oxygen for aerobic respiration (1). (c) Mixes microbes with nutrients and
    oxygen / even temperature (1). (d) Keeps pH at optimum for enzymes (1). (e) Kills other microbes
    that would compete for nutrients or contaminate the product (1).
