# Crude oil — Summary

Specification points 4.7–4.18 · Topic 4 Organic chemistry.

## Essential facts

- Crude oil: mixture of hydrocarbons. **Fraction**: hydrocarbons with similar boiling points.
- Fractional distillation: heat → vapour enters bottom of column → column hot at bottom, cool at top
  → vapours rise and cool → each condenses where temperature < its boiling point → drawn off.
- Top to bottom: **refinery gases** (bottled gas, heating/cooking) · **gasoline** (cars) ·
  **kerosene** (aircraft) · **diesel** (lorries, buses) · **fuel oil** (ships, power stations) ·
  **bitumen** (roads, roofing).
- Going down: longer chains → higher boiling point, more viscous, darker.
- **Fuel**: releases heat energy when burned.
- Complete combustion → CO₂ + H₂O. Incomplete (limited O₂) → CO and/or C + H₂O.
- **CO**: reduces the capacity of the blood to carry oxygen.
- Engines: high temperature → N₂ + O₂ react → oxides of nitrogen.
- Sulfur impurities in fuel burn: S + O₂ → SO₂.
- SO₂ and NOₓ dissolve in rain water → sulfuric and nitric acids → **acid rain**.
- **Cracking**: long-chain alkanes → shorter alkanes + alkenes; silica or alumina catalyst,
  600–700 °C. C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄.
- Why: supply of long-chain fractions > demand; demand for gasoline > supply. Also makes alkenes.

## Common traps

- NOₓ come from air, not the fuel.
- Cracking products must balance and include an alkene.
