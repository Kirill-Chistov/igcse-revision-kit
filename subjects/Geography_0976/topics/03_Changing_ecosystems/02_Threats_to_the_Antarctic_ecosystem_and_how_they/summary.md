# Threats to the Antarctic ecosystem and how they can be managed — Summary

Specification points 3.2.1–3.2.3 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1. No separate detailed
example is required: the whole topic is about Antarctica.

## Threats (3.2.1)

- **Climate change** — Peninsula **+3 °C** since the mid-20th century; less sea ice; **Larsen B** ice
  shelf (3,250 km²) collapsed 2002; ocean acidification.
- **Fishing** — krill for fish feed and omega-3; limit of **620,000 t** reached in 2025 and 2026;
  illegal toothfish fishing; seabird bycatch on longlines.
- **Resource exploitation** — past sealing and whaling; coal, iron ore, possibly oil — mining banned
  but reviewable from **2048**.
- **Tourism** — over **118,000** visitors in 2024–25, 98% to the Peninsula: trampling, disturbance,
  non-native seeds, spills.
- **Research stations** — over 70; fuel spills, waste, building on scarce ice-free land.

## Impacts of destruction (3.2.2)

- Warming → less sea ice → fewer ice algae → fewer krill → penguins, seals, whales decline.
- Warming → ice shelves break up → glaciers speed up → global sea-level rise (ice sheet ≈ **58 m** of
  sea level).
- Less ice → lower albedo → more warming (feedback).
- Local: biodiversity loss, very slow recovery, non-native plants. Global: sea level, climate, lost
  science (ice cores), lost fishing and tourism income.

## Management (3.2.3)

| Strategy / technique | Key point | Judgement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Antarctic Treaty** 1959 (58 countries) | Peace and science only; claims frozen | Works, but no enforcement body; consensus |
| **Madrid Protocol** 1991/1998 | Mining ban; EIAs; waste removed | No mining so far; reviewable from 2048 |
| **Environmental impact assessments** | Every activity assessed first | Stops big damage; misses many small impacts |
| **CCAMLR** 1982 | Ecosystem catch limits; **Ross Sea MPA** 1.55 million km² | Seabird deaths <100 a year; new MPAs blocked |
| **Tourism rules / IAATO** | ≤100 ashore; ships >500 can't land; 5 m from wildlife | Good per visit; total numbers keep rising |
| **Whaling ban** 1986 | Southern Ocean sanctuary 1994 | Humpbacks recovering |
| **Global climate action** | Cut emissions (5.3) | Only answer to the biggest threat; outside Antarctica's control |

**Judgement:** preventive agreements make local threats sustainable to manage; the weakness is
consensus and national enforcement; climate change, the biggest threat, comes from outside.

## Common traps

- Saying melting **sea ice** or ice shelves raises sea level — it is the land ice they hold back that does.
- Treating the Antarctic Treaty as the mining ban — the ban is in the Madrid Protocol.
