## Key terms

- **Mitigation** — reduce the **cause**: cut emissions, increase carbon sinks.
- **Adaptation** — reduce the **harm**: adjust to the impacts.
- **Strategy** = plan or agreement; **technique** = method.
- **Net zero** — emissions balanced by removals.

## International agreements

| | Kyoto (1997) | Paris (2015) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who cuts | HICs only, ~5% below 1990 by 2008–12 | every country |
| Targets | binding; carbon trading | own targets (NDCs), updated every 5 years; aim well below 2 °C, try for 1.5 °C |
| + | first binding targets | all major emitters; climate finance (US$100 bn/yr → US$300 bn/yr by 2035) |
| − | USA never ratified; China, India no targets; emissions rose | voluntary, no penalties; pledges → ~2.3–2.8 °C; USA left (2020, again Jan 2026) |

## National strategies

Law with targets (UK Climate Change Act 2008, net zero 2050) · carbon tax / emissions trading ·
subsidies and bans · national adaptation plan.

## Techniques

- **Mitigation:** renewables (intermittent) · CCS (costly, few projects) · forests (slow, can burn) ·
  using less energy (up-front cost) · changing farming (slow to change).
- **Adaptation:** hard defences (costly, must be raised) · mangroves (cheap, slow to grow) · raising
  buildings / managed retreat (breaks communities) · new crops, irrigation (water use) · water storage
  (costly) · early warnings (save lives, not property).

## Evaluation

Mitigation: tackles the cause, lasting and most sustainable, but slow, costly, needs every country
(free-rider problem). Adaptation: immediate, local benefit, but treats symptoms, costs rise, has limits,
LICs can least afford it. **Judgement:** both are needed — mitigation limits future change, adaptation
copes with change already locked in. Criteria: cause or symptom · how long it lasts · who pays and who
benefits · social, economic, environmental costs.
