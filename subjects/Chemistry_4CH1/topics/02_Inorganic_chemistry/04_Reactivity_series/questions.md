# Reactivity series — Questions

Specification points 2.15–2.21 · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. Marks in brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. Put these metals in order of reactivity, most reactive first: copper, calcium, zinc, potassium,
   iron, magnesium. (2)
2. State the conditions needed for iron to rust. (2)
3. Define (a) oxidation and (b) reducing agent, in terms of electrons. (2)

## 2. Reactions

4. Write word equations for (a) calcium with cold water; (b) magnesium with steam. (2)
5. Zinc is added to copper(II) sulfate solution. Give **two** observations and the ionic equation.
   (3)
6. In the reaction Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, identify the oxidising agent. Explain. (2)
7. Metal X displaces iron from iron(II) sulfate solution but not zinc from zinc sulfate solution.
   Where does X fit in the reactivity series? (1)
8. Explain why iron coated with zinc does not rust even when the zinc coating is scratched. (3)
9. Explain why painting a bicycle frame prevents rusting. (2)

## 3. Practical

10. Describe how to compare the reactivity of magnesium, zinc and iron using dilute hydrochloric
    acid. Include how you would make it a fair test and how you would identify the gas. (5)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. potassium, calcium, magnesium, zinc, iron, copper (2; one pair wrong = 1).
2. Water (1) and oxygen / air (1).
3. (a) Loss of electrons (1). (b) A substance that reduces another / loses electrons / is itself
   oxidised (1).
4. (a) calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen (1). (b) magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide +
   hydrogen (1).
5. Blue colour fades (1); pink-brown solid / copper forms on the zinc (1); (accept temperature
   rises); Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu (1).
6. Cu²⁺ (1); it gains electrons / is reduced / oxidises zinc (1).
7. Between zinc and iron (1).
8. Zinc is more reactive than iron (1); zinc loses electrons / is oxidised instead of iron (1);
   sacrificial protection (1).
9. Paint is a barrier (1); keeps water and oxygen away from the iron (1).
10. Same volume (1) and concentration of acid (1); same mass / size of metal (1); compare rate of
    bubbling / temperature rise / gas volume in a set time (1); lighted splint gives squeaky pop =
    hydrogen (1). (Max 5.)
