## 1. Recall

1. Write the equations linking (a) work done, force and distance; (b) GPE, mass, *g* and height;
   (c) kinetic energy, mass and speed.
2. Describe what is meant by power.
3. What is the work done on an object equal to?

## 2. Understanding

1. A waiter carries a tray across a room at constant height. Explain why the upward force on the
   tray does no work.
2. Two balls, 0.1 kg and 1.0 kg, are dropped from the same height. Ignoring air resistance, explain
   why they hit the ground at the same speed.
3. Explain, in terms of energy, why a car's brakes get hot when it stops.

## 3. Application

1. A crane lifts a 500 kg load through 12 m. Calculate the GPE gained.
2. A 0.060 kg tennis ball leaves a racket at 40 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.
3. A 2.0 kg rock has 400 J of kinetic energy. Calculate its speed.
4. A motor does 4500 J of work in 15 s. Calculate its power.
5. A 3.0 kW kettle is switched on for 2.0 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred.

## 4. Higher demand

1. A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 20 m. (a) Calculate its speed just before it lands, ignoring air
   resistance. (b) It actually lands at 18 m/s. Calculate the work done against air resistance.
2. A 900 kg car travelling at 25 m/s brakes to a stop in 50 m. Calculate the average braking force.
3. A cyclist of total mass 80 kg freewheels from rest down a hill 15 m high and reaches 12 m/s at
   the bottom. The road down the hill is 300 m long. Calculate the average resistive force.

## 5. Exam-style prompts

1. **[3 marks]** A student of mass 55 kg runs up a flight of stairs 4.0 m high in 5.0 s.
   *Calculate* the student's useful power.
2. **[4 marks]** A roller-coaster car starts from rest at the top of a 30 m drop. *Explain* how
   conservation of energy can be used to predict its speed at the bottom, and why the real speed is
   lower.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Not an official mark scheme — written to the specification wording.*

**Recall.** 1. (a) *W* = *F* × *d* (b) GPE = *m* × *g* × *h* (c) KE = ½ × *m* × *v*².
2. The rate of transfer of energy (or rate of doing work). 3. The energy transferred.

**Understanding.** 1. Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force; the tray
moves horizontally, at right angles to the upward force, so no distance is moved along the force.
2. *mgh* = ½*mv*², so *v* = √(2*gh*): mass cancels, so the speed depends only on height.
3. The car's kinetic energy is transferred by the work done by the braking force (friction) to the
thermal store of the brakes.

**Application.** 1. 500 × 10 × 12 = 60 000 J. 2. 0.5 × 0.060 × 40² = 48 J.
3. *v* = √(2 × 400 ÷ 2.0) = √400 = 20 m/s. 4. 4500 ÷ 15 = 300 W.
5. 3000 × 120 = 360 000 J.

**Higher demand.** 1. (a) *v* = √(2 × 10 × 20) = 20 m/s. (b) GPE lost = 0.50 × 10 × 20 = 100 J;
KE = 0.5 × 0.50 × 18² = 81 J; work against air resistance = 19 J. 2. KE = 0.5 × 900 × 25² =
281 250 J; *F* = 281 250 ÷ 50 = 5625 N (≈ 5600 N). 3. GPE lost = 80 × 10 × 15 = 12 000 J;
KE = 0.5 × 80 × 12² = 5760 J; work against resistance = 6240 J; *F* = 6240 ÷ 300 = 20.8 N (≈ 21 N).

**Exam-style.** 1. GPE = 55 × 10 × 4.0 = 2200 J (1); *P* = 2200 ÷ 5.0 (1) = 440 W (1).
2. GPE at the top is transferred to KE (1); GPE lost = KE gained, *mgh* = ½*mv*² (1), giving
*v* = √(2*gh*) ≈ 24.5 m/s (1); in reality work is done against friction and air resistance,
transferring energy to thermal stores, so KE and speed are lower (1).
