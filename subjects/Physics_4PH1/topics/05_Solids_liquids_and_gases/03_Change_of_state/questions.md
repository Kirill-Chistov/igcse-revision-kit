# Change of state — Questions

Specification points 5.8P–5.14P · Topic 5 · all Paper 2 only. Marks in brackets.

## 1. Recall

1. Describe the arrangement and motion of the particles in a solid and in a gas. (4)
2. State what is meant by specific heat capacity. (2)
3. State the unit of specific heat capacity. (1)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

4. Explain why the temperature of melting ice stays at 0 °C even though it is being heated. (3)
5. Describe what happens to the particles when a liquid boils. (3)
6. Give two differences between evaporation and boiling. (2)
7. Explain why sweat evaporating from the skin cools you down. (3)

## 3. Application

8. Calculate the energy needed to heat 2.0 kg of water from 15 °C to 65 °C
   (*c* = 4200 J/kg °C). (2)
9. 13 500 J raises the temperature of a 0.75 kg copper block by 46 °C. Calculate the specific
   heat capacity of copper. (3)
10. A 0.40 kg iron block (*c* = 450 J/kg °C) at 20 °C is given 9000 J. Calculate its final
    temperature. (3)

## 4. Practical

11. **[6 marks]** *Describe* an experiment to find the specific heat capacity of an aluminium block.
    Include the measurements and how you would use them to calculate the result.
12. A student's value for the specific heat capacity of aluminium is higher than the accepted value.
    Explain why, and suggest one improvement. (3)
13. Sketch the temperature–time graph obtained when crushed ice at −10 °C is heated until the water
    has boiled for a few minutes. Label the melting and boiling sections with their temperatures. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. Solid: regular pattern / close together, touching (1); vibrate about fixed positions (1). Gas:
   random arrangement, far apart (1); move quickly and randomly in all directions (1).
2. The energy required to change the temperature of an object by 1 °C (1) per kilogram of mass (1).
3. J/kg °C (1).
4. Energy supplied is used to weaken/break the bonds (forces) between particles (1), not to increase
   their kinetic energy (1); temperature depends on (average) kinetic energy, so it stays constant (1).
5. Particles throughout the liquid gain enough energy (1) to overcome the forces between them
   completely (1); they become far apart and move freely/randomly; bubbles of gas form in the liquid (1).
6. Any two (1 each): evaporation at the surface only, boiling throughout the liquid; evaporation at
   any temperature, boiling only at the boiling point; evaporation cools the liquid.
7. The fastest / most energetic particles escape from the surface (1); the particles left have a
   lower average kinetic energy (1); so the temperature of the sweat and skin falls (1).
8. Δ*Q* = 2.0 × 4200 × 50 (1) = 420 000 J (1).
9. *c* = Δ*Q* ÷ (*m*Δ*T*) (1) = 13 500 ÷ (0.75 × 46) (1) = 391 ≈ 390 J/kg °C (1).
10. Δ*T* = 9000 ÷ (0.40 × 450) (1) = 50 °C (1); final temperature = 70 °C (1).
11. Measure mass of block on a balance (1); heater and thermometer in the holes, block insulated (1);
    record start temperature (1); switch on, measure energy supplied with a joulemeter / *I*, *V* and *t*
    with *E* = *IVt* (1); record highest temperature, find Δ*T* (1); *c* = *E* ÷ (*m*Δ*T*) (1).
12. Energy lost to the surroundings (1), so Δ*T* is smaller than it should be, giving a larger *c* (1);
    improvement: add (more) insulation / a lid / oil or water in the thermometer hole (1).
13. Rising line from −10 °C (1); flat section at 0 °C labelled melting, then rising line (1); flat section
    at 100 °C labelled boiling (1).
