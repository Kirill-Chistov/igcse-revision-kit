# Energetics — Questions

Specification points 3.1–3.8 · Topic 3 Physical chemistry. Questions 7–10 are **Paper 2 only**.
Marks in brackets. Use c = 4.2 J/g/°C.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall

1. State what is meant by an *exothermic* reaction and an *endothermic* reaction. (2)
2. When ammonium nitrate dissolves in water, the temperature falls. State whether the change is
   exothermic or endothermic and give the sign of ΔH. (2)

## 2. Calorimetry calculations

3. 2.0 g of ammonium nitrate (Mr = 80) is dissolved in 50.0 cm³ of water. The temperature falls
   from 21.0 °C to 18.0 °C. Calculate ΔH in kJ/mol. (4)
4. Excess zinc powder is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ copper(II) sulfate solution. The
   temperature rises from 20.0 °C to 44.5 °C. Calculate ΔH per mole of copper(II) sulfate. (4)
5. Burning 0.64 g of methanol (Mr = 32) heats 200 g of water from 19.0 °C to 34.0 °C.
   (a) Calculate ΔH of combustion of methanol in kJ/mol. (4)
   (b) The data-book value is −726 kJ/mol. Give two reasons why the experimental value is
   less exothermic. (2)
   (c) Suggest one improvement to the apparatus. (1)

## 3. Practical

6. Describe how to measure the temperature change when hydrochloric acid neutralises sodium
   hydroxide solution. (4)

## 4. Energy level diagrams and bond energies — Paper 2 only

7. Draw an energy level diagram for an endothermic reaction. Label the reactants, the products
   and ΔH. (3)
8. Explain, in terms of bonds, why the combustion of methane is exothermic. (2)
9. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Bond energies (kJ/mol): N≡N 945, H–H 436, N–H 391.
   Calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction. (3)
10. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Bond energies (kJ/mol): H–H 436, O=O 498, O–H 464.
    Calculate the enthalpy change and state whether the reaction is exothermic. (4)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Exothermic — heat energy given out (to the surroundings) (1); endothermic — heat energy taken
   in (from the surroundings) (1).
2. Endothermic (1); ΔH positive (1).
3. Q = 50.0 × 4.2 × 3.0 = 630 J (1); = 0.630 kJ (1); n = 2.0 ÷ 80 = 0.025 mol (1);
   ΔH = +25.2 kJ/mol (value 1, positive sign needed).
4. Q = 50.0 × 4.2 × 24.5 = 5145 J (1); n = 0.500 × 50.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0250 mol (1);
   5.145 ÷ 0.0250 = 206 (1); ΔH = −206 kJ/mol (sign 1).
5. (a) Q = 200 × 4.2 × 15.0 = 12 600 J = 12.6 kJ (1); n = 0.64 ÷ 32 = 0.020 mol (1);
   12.6 ÷ 0.020 = 630 (1); ΔH = −630 kJ/mol (sign 1).
   (b) Any two: heat lost to the surroundings / air / can (1); incomplete combustion (1);
   evaporation of methanol from the wick (1).
   (c) Draught shield / lid on the can / flame closer to the can / insulate the can (1).
6. Measured volume of acid in a polystyrene cup (1); record starting temperature (1); add measured
   volume of sodium hydroxide solution, stir (lid on) (1); record the highest temperature and
   subtract (1).
7. Reactants level lower than products level, both labelled (1); ΔH arrow from reactants **up** to
   products (1); axis labelled energy (1).
8. Energy taken in to break bonds (C–H, O=O) is less than (1) energy given out when bonds form
   (C=O, O–H) (1).
9. Broken: 945 + 3(436) = 2253 kJ (1); made: 6(391) = 2346 kJ (1); ΔH = 2253 − 2346 = −93 kJ/mol (1).
10. Broken: 2(436) + 498 = 1370 kJ (1); made: 4(464) = 1856 kJ (1); ΔH = −486 kJ/mol (1);
    exothermic, because ΔH is negative / more energy released making bonds (1).
