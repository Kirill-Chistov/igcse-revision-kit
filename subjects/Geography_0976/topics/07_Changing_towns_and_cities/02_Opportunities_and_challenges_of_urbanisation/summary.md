# Opportunities and challenges of urbanisation — Summary

Specification points 7.2.1–7.2.3 · Paper 2.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Why cities offer more:** many people close together → enough customers to support services and
  businesses. Opportunities: economic development (jobs, higher wages, ~80% of world GDP), services,
  housing, culture, leisure, consumption.
- **Rapid urban growth:** people arrive faster than homes, jobs, pipes, roads and bins can be provided.

| Aspect | Opportunity | Challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inequality | growing middle class | rich–poor gap widens; poor get worst services |
| Service provision | cheaper per person | mains/sewers can't keep up; water vendors |
| Housing | construction boom; homes improved over time | shortage, high rents, overcrowding |
| Employment | new jobs; informal sector absorbs newcomers | too few formal jobs; insecure informal work |
| Transport | density makes public transport viable | congestion, pollution, accidents |
| Waste | recycling and waste picking give income | dumped/burnt waste blocks drains, spreads cholera |
| Unplanned settlements | cheap housing near work; community | hazardous sites, no tenure, fire, disease |

- **1.16 billion** people — almost **1 in 4** urban residents — live in slums/informal settlements (UN,
  2024).
- **Recognise an unplanned settlement in a photo:** self-built, dense, no street plan, marginal land
  (steep slope, floodplain), unrendered brick, water tanks on roofs.
- **Why sprawl happens:** cheap land at the edge, large sites for firms, roads and cars; in LICs,
  unplanned settlements at the edge.
- **Fringe land uses:** housing estates, retail parks, business parks, warehouses, landfill and sewage
  works, airports, country parks, commuter villages.
- **Sprawl impacts:** −farmland and habitat loss, flood risk, pollution, farmers' losses, city-centre
  shops lose trade, commuter villages lose services and become unaffordable; +jobs, land sales, tax,
  housing choice, new services, green space.

## Definitions

**Inequality** — the uneven distribution of income, services and opportunities between groups of people.

**Informal sector** — work that is not officially registered or taxed.

**Unplanned (informal) settlement** — housing built by residents on land they do not legally own or
rent, without planning permission and usually without basic services.

**Urban sprawl** — the unplanned, often low-density, outward spread of a built-up area into the
surrounding countryside.

**Rural–urban fringe** — the zone at the edge of a city where urban and rural land uses meet and mix.

## Common traps

- Sprawl ≠ urbanisation.
- 7.2.2 wants opportunities **and** challenges.
