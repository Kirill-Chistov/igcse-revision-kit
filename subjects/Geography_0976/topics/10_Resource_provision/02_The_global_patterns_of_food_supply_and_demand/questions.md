## 1. Resource-based

Use Figure 3 in the topic PDF (daily calorie supply per person, 2023).

1. **(3)** Describe the global pattern of calorie supply shown.
2. **(2)** Identify **one** region where supply is mostly under 2,600 kcal and **one** where it is mostly over 3,200 kcal.
3. **(4)** Suggest **two** reasons why calorie supply in much of sub-Saharan Africa is lower than in Europe.

## 2. Explain

4. **(4)** Explain why global demand for food is increasing faster than population growth alone.
5. **(4)** Explain **two** strategies used to increase food supply.
6. **(3)** Explain how the Green Revolution increased food supply.
7. **(4)** Explain **two** reasons for the globalisation of food supplies.

## 3. Extended

8. **(6)** Describe the impacts of the globalisation of food supplies on countries that export food.
9. **(9)** "The globalisation of food supplies benefits importing countries more than exporting countries." How far do you agree?

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. Highest in North America, Europe, Russia/China, Australia (1); lowest in sub-Saharan Africa and a few countries such as Haiti and Yemen (1); broadly higher in HICs and lower in LICs / uses data (1).
2. e.g. central/southern Africa (1); North America or Europe (1).
3. Two developed from: poverty — cannot buy or import food; low-input subsistence farming with low yields; drought and poor soils; conflict; poor storage/transport losses; rapid population growth. 1 per reason + 1 per development.
4. Rising incomes in emerging economies (1) → more meat and dairy (1), which need several kg of crop feed per kg of food (1); urbanisation → processed food / globalisation → more varied diets (1).
5. Any two strategies with how they increase supply (1 + 1 each): e.g. Green Revolution HYVs; GM drought-resistant crops; irrigation of dry land; aquaculture; reducing waste.
6. High-yielding varieties of rice/wheat (1) grown with fertiliser, pesticides and irrigation (1) → higher yields and more harvests per year (1).
7. Two from transport (refrigeration, air freight), ICT supply chains, trade liberalisation, TNCs/supermarkets, consumer demand for out-of-season food, cheap land and labour abroad — 1 + 1 development each.
8. Level marking. *L1 (1–2):* simple impacts. *L2 (3–4):* positive and negative impacts explained. *L3 (5–6):* economic, social and environmental impacts, both positive and negative, e.g. jobs and foreign currency vs low wages, cash crops replacing food crops, deforestation and water use.
9. Level marking. *L1 (1–3):* simple points for one side. *L2 (4–6):* benefits and costs for both importing and exporting countries explained. *L3 (7–9):* balanced, with a supported judgement — e.g. importers gain cheap food and choice at little cost, while exporters gain jobs and currency but carry most environmental costs and risk food insecurity, so benefits are uneven.
