# Fission and fusion — Questions

Specification points 7.17–7.26 · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1.

## 1. Fission (7.17–7.20)

1. Name three types of nuclear reaction that can be a source of energy. (3)
2. Describe what happens when a nucleus of uranium-235 undergoes fission. Include what starts it, what is produced and the form in which energy is released. (4)
3. Describe how a chain reaction is set up in uranium-235. (3)
4. Complete the fission equation: <sup>1</sup><sub>0</sub>n + <sup>235</sup><sub>92</sub>U → <sup>141</sup><sub>56</sub>Ba + <sup>?</sup><sub>36</sub>Kr + 3 <sup>1</sup><sub>0</sub>n. (1)

## 2. The reactor (7.21, 7.22)

5. Describe the role of the moderator in a nuclear reactor. (2)
6. Explain how the control rods are used to reduce the rate of fission in a reactor. (3)
7. Explain why a nuclear reactor is surrounded by thick concrete. (2)
8. A student says "the control rods and the moderator both slow the reaction down". Evaluate this statement. (2)

## 3. Fusion (7.23–7.26)

9. Describe nuclear fusion. (3)
10. State the energy source for stars. (1)
11. Explain why nuclear fusion does not happen at low temperatures and pressures. (4)
12. Explain two differences between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. (4)

## Answers and marking guidance

One mark per creditable point (1).

1. Fission (1); fusion (1); radioactive decay (1).
2. A (slow) neutron is absorbed by / collides with a U-235 nucleus (1); the nucleus splits into two smaller daughter nuclei (1), which are radioactive, and releases 2 or 3 neutrons (1); energy is released as kinetic energy of the fission products (1).
3. Each fission releases 2 or 3 neutrons (1); these neutrons strike / are absorbed by other U-235 nuclei (1); which also undergo fission and release more neutrons, so the process continues (1).
4. 92 (mass: 1 + 235 = 141 + 92 + 3) (1).
5. Slows down the fast neutrons released by fission (1); so they are more likely to be absorbed by U-235 nuclei and cause further fission (1).
6. Control rods absorb neutrons (1); lowering them further into the core absorbs more neutrons (1); so fewer neutrons are available to cause fission / fewer fissions occur and less energy is released (1).
7. The core emits ionising radiation (neutrons and gamma) (1); the concrete absorbs it, protecting workers and the environment (1).
8. Incorrect for the moderator: it slows the neutrons, which makes fission more likely / keeps the reaction going (1); the control rods absorb neutrons, which reduces the reaction rate (1).
9. Small / light nuclei join (1) to form a larger nucleus (1); the mass of the products is less than the mass of the original nuclei and the lost mass is released as energy (1).
10. Nuclear fusion (of hydrogen into helium) (1).
11. Nuclei contain protons so are positively charged (1); they repel each other (electrostatic repulsion) (1); at low temperatures they move too slowly to get close enough to fuse / at very high temperatures they move fast enough to overcome the repulsion (1); high pressure makes collisions frequent enough (1).
12. Any two, each with both sides: fission splits a large nucleus, fusion joins small nuclei (1)(1); fission is started by absorbing a neutron, fusion needs very high temperature and pressure (1)(1); fission products are radioactive, fusion product (helium) is not (1)(1); fission in reactors, fusion in stars (1)(1).
