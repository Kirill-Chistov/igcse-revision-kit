## Answers and marking guidance

*AI-generated for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme. One mark per creditable
point unless stated.*

1. Drainage basin: the area of land drained by a river and its tributaries (1). Watershed: the
   boundary of a drainage basin, along high ground (1).
2. Tributary: a smaller river that joins a larger one (1). Confluence: the point where two rivers
   meet (1).
3. The length of the bed and banks in contact with the water (in a cross-section) (1).
4. Cross-sectional area = 8 × 1.5 = 12 m² (1); discharge = 12 × 0.4 = 4.8 (1); m³/s / cumecs (1).
5. Steep near the source (1), becoming gentler towards the mouth — a smooth concave curve (1).
6. (a) Both increase (1); width from 1.8 m to 9.2 m (1); velocity from 0.21 to 0.55 m/s (1).
   (b) Channel becomes wider/deeper and smoother (1); smaller proportion of water in contact with the
   wetted perimeter / less friction (1); discharge increases as tributaries join (1). Not credited:
   "the gradient is steeper downstream".
   (c) Attrition — pebbles knock together and break into smaller pieces (1); abrasion against the bed
   also wears them down / they have travelled further (1).
7. Any two: band of hard rock (waterfall/rapids) (1); tributary joining adds discharge suddenly (1);
   dam or channel straightening (1); local geology or vegetation changing the bed (1).
8. It has inputs and outputs across its boundary (1): water enters as precipitation and leaves by
   evapotranspiration/river discharge to the sea (1).
9. Interception: precipitation caught and held by vegetation before reaching the ground (1).
   Percolation: water moving down from the soil into the rock (1).
10. Throughflow moves through the soil (1); overland flow moves over the surface, when water cannot
    infiltrate (1).
11. Evaporation / transpiration / evapotranspiration / river discharge to the sea (any two).
12. Saturated soil has no space for infiltration (1), so water becomes overland flow (1), the fastest
    route to the channel (1).
13. Any two, each named (1) and described (1): hydraulic action — force of water, air forced into
    cracks breaks rock away; abrasion — load scrapes the bed and banks; attrition — load collides,
    becoming smaller and rounder; solution — acidic water dissolves soluble rock.
14. Abrasion wears away the bed and banks using the load (1); attrition wears down the load itself as
    pieces collide (1).
15. Any three: traction — large boulders rolled along the bed; saltation — pebbles bounced along the
    bed; suspension — fine particles carried within the water; solution — minerals dissolved.
16. Velocity/energy falls so the river can no longer carry its load (1); heaviest particles dropped
    first (1). Places, any two: inside of a meander bend; on the floodplain in a flood; at the mouth
    where it enters the sea or a lake; after a flood when discharge falls (1 + 1).
