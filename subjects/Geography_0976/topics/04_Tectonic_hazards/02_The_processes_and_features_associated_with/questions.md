## Questions

1. **[2]** *Define* the focus and the epicentre of an earthquake.
2. **[5]** *Explain* the processes at a convergent/destructive plate boundary which cause earthquakes
   and volcanic eruptions.
3. **[3]** *Explain* why volcanoes form at divergent/constructive boundaries.
4. **[2]** *Explain* why a shallow-focus earthquake usually causes more damage than a deep-focus one.
5. **[4]** *Compare* the shape and eruptions of a strato-volcano and a shield volcano.
6. **[3]** *Describe* a cinder cone.
7. **[3]** *Define* active, dormant and extinct volcanoes.
8. **[4]** Draw and label a cross-section of a volcano to show the crater, vent, magma chamber and
   a secondary cone.
9. **[2]** *Explain* the difference between magma and lava.
10. **[4]** *Explain* why pyroclastic flows and lahars cause more deaths than lava flows.
11. **[4]** *Explain* why the frequency and spread of volcanic hazards affect how dangerous they are.

## Marking guidance

1. Focus: point underground where the rock breaks / the earthquake starts (1). Epicentre: point on
   the surface directly above the focus (1).
2. Denser oceanic plate moves towards/under continental plate — subduction (1); friction as it
   sinks, pressure builds and is released → earthquakes, shallow to deep (1–2); plate heats and
   melts in the mantle → magma (1); magma less dense, rises through cracks (1); explosive eruptions
   / strato-volcanoes (1). Max 5.
3. Plates move apart (1); pressure on the mantle below falls, so it partly melts (1); magma rises
   into the gap / forms new crust / gentle eruptions (1).
4. Less distance for waves to travel / less energy lost (1) → stronger shaking at the surface (1).
5. Strato: steep, tall, symmetrical; shield: wide, gentle slopes (1–2). Strato: infrequent,
   explosive, ash and lava; shield: frequent, gentle lava flows (1–2). Must compare for full marks.
6. Small (usually < 300 m) (1); steep, straight sides with a bowl-shaped crater (1); built from
   loose cinders around a single vent / short-lived (1).
7. Active: erupted recently and likely to erupt again (1). Dormant: not erupted for a long time but
   could erupt again (1). Extinct: not erupted for at least 10 000 years / not expected to erupt (1).
8. 1 mark per correct label, max 4; secondary cone must be on the side with its own vent.
9. Magma: molten rock below the surface (1); lava: molten rock once erupted onto the surface (1).
10. Pyroclastic flows very fast (> 100 km/h) and very hot — cannot be outrun (1–2); lahars fast,
    travel far along valleys, can arrive with little warning (1); lava usually slow — people can
    move away (1).
11. Frequency: frequent → people prepared / monitored (1); rare → risk underestimated, people
    unprepared, live close (1). Spread: ash and gas carried long distances affect more people /
    other countries (1); lahars follow valleys far downstream (1).
