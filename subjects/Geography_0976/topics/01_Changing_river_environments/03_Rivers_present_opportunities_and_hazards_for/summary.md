# Rivers present opportunities and hazards for people — Summary

Specification points 1.3.1–1.3.9 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976 · Paper 1.

## Essential facts and ideas

- **Opportunities:** water supply · fertile floodplain farmland · flat land for building · transport
  and trade · HEP and cooling water · fishing and leisure. **Hazards:** flooding · pollution · bank
  erosion · water-related disease.
- **Flood** = discharge exceeds channel capacity. **Lag time** = peak rainfall → peak discharge.
  Short lag + high peak = flood risk.
- **Natural causes:** prolonged or intense rain · snowmelt · impermeable or frozen ground · steep
  slopes. **Human:** urbanisation · deforestation · farming · upstream straightening · building on
  the floodplain · climate change. All add water or speed it to the channel.
- **Impacts:** negative social (deaths, homelessness, disease), economic (property, crops,
  infrastructure), environmental (erosion, pollution spread); positive (fertile silt, groundwater
  recharged, wetlands renewed). Deaths higher in LICs, economic losses higher in HICs.
- **Strategies** = plans and agreements; **techniques** = methods. Hard: dam and reservoir ·
  channelisation · embankments/flood walls · flood relief channel. Soft: afforestation · floodplain
  zoning · river restoration/wetlands · prediction, warnings and preparation.
- **Judge flood management on:** value protected · cause or symptom · moves the flood downstream? ·
  cost over time · copes with climate change.
- **Pollution causes:** untreated sewage · farming (fertiliser, pesticides) · industry (chemicals,
  heavy metals, hot water) · mining · solid waste · urban run-off.
- **Eutrophication:** nutrients → algal bloom → light blocked, plants die → bacteria use oxygen →
  oxygen falls → fish die.
- **Pollution management:** sewage treatment and connections · effluent permits and fines (polluter
  pays) · buffer strips · less fertiliser · waste collection and clean-ups. Prevention at source beats
  clean-up; enforcement and funding are the weak link.

**1.3.9 Nairobi River, Kenya — the memorable core.** Source near Ondiri Swamp; basin ~**40 km**;
through Kangemi, CBD, Eastleigh, Kariobangi, Dandora → Athi River. **Causes:** untreated sewage
(**E. coli up to 1,000,000 per 100 ml**) · industry (**145 factories and slaughterhouses**, Oct 2024;
lead, copper, chromium, zinc, manganese) · solid waste (**6,000 t** from a **2.5 km** stretch) ·
urbanisation (~**5.5 million**, **6,247/km²**). **Impacts:** cholera, typhoid, dysentery · low
biodiversity, pollution-tolerant species · algae **100 → 947** (Ondiri → Ruai) · contaminated crops.
**Management** *(researched)*: Nairobi Rivers Commission (**Oct 2024**) · regeneration programme
(**175 km**) · Climate WorX jobs (~**39,400**/month) · NEMA enforcement · sewer project **2019–2023**
(**445 km**, coverage **48% → 55%**) · Michuki Park (**6,357** trees). **Judgement:** sound mix, but
the **1999–2008** programme failed — lasting success needs funding, maintenance and enforcement.

## Common traps

- Pros and cons with no judgement is not an evaluation.
- Channelisation and embankments can worsen flooding downstream.
- Clean-ups treat the symptom; sewers and regulation treat the cause.
