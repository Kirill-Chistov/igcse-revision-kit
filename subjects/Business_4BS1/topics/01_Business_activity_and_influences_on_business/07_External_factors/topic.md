# External factors

## Specification coverage

| Point | Section |
| --- | --- |
| 1.7.1 External factors affecting business decisions: social, technological, environmental, political | §1, §2 |

## 1. The four external factors (1.7.1)

::: definition
**External factors** — influences from outside a business that it cannot control, but which affect its decisions.
:::

| Factor | What changes | Effect on business decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Social** | population size and age, lifestyles, tastes and fashions, attitudes (e.g. to health or ethics) | demand for some products rises and for others falls → change the product range, marketing and target market |
| **Technological** | new production methods (e.g. automation), new products, the internet, social media, online selling | chances to cut costs, improve quality, launch products and sell online; but investment and training costs, and products go out of date faster |
| **Environmental** | concern about pollution, climate change, waste and the use of resources | pressure from customers, pressure groups and laws → sustainable materials, less waste and energy, less packaging |
| **Political** | government policies, laws, taxes (→ 1.6) and political stability | new laws and taxes change costs; instability makes investment and location decisions risky |

## 2. Becoming more environmentally friendly (1.7.1)

:::: grid
::: advantages | Advantages
- Attracts customers who care about the environment → more sales and loyalty.
- Less waste and energy use → lower costs.
- Stays ahead of environmental laws → avoids fines; better reputation helps recruitment.
:::
::: disadvantages | Disadvantages
- Sustainable materials and new equipment cost more → higher prices or lower profit.
- Changes take time and investment to pay back.
:::
::::
