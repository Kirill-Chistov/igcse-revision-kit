## Questions

1. **[2 marks]** *Define* the development gap.
2. **[1 mark]** *State* the measure the World Bank uses to classify countries as LICs, MICs and HICs.
3. **[4 marks]** Using the world map of income groups, *describe* the distribution of LICs.
4. **[3 marks]** *Describe* the distribution of HICs.
5. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how two environmental factors can hold back a country's development.
6. **[4 marks]** *Explain* why dependence on exporting primary products can keep a country poor.
7. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how debt slows development.
8. **[6 marks]** *Explain* why the development gap between HICs and LICs is difficult to close.

## Answers and marking guidance (not official)

1. The difference in levels of development (wealth and quality of life) (1) between the richest /
   most developed and poorest / least developed countries (1).
2. GNI per capita (Atlas method, US$).
3. Concentrated in Africa (1), especially sub-Saharan Africa, 21 of 25 (1); named examples, e.g.
   Niger, Chad, DR Congo, Ethiopia (1); few elsewhere — Afghanistan, Syria, Yemen, North Korea (1);
   none in the Americas or Europe (1).
4. North America, most of Europe, Japan, South Korea, Australia/NZ (1–2); Gulf states (1); few in
   Latin America, e.g. Chile, Uruguay (1); mostly northern hemisphere (1). Max 3.
5. Each factor (1) + mechanism (1): drought → crop failure → food shortages, low farm income;
   hazards → destroy infrastructure → money spent rebuilding; landlocked → high transport costs →
   fewer exports; malaria in hot, wet climates → sick workforce.
6. Low prices (1); prices unstable, so income unpredictable (1); value added (and profit) goes to
   the country that processes the goods (1); low export earnings → little tax and money to invest
   in services and industry (1).
7. Repayments and interest (1) take money that could be spent on schools, health care,
   infrastructure (1) → weaker workforce / less investment → slower growth (1).
8. Cycle of poverty: low income → little saving and tax → little investment in education, health,
   infrastructure → low productivity → low income (up to 3). Drains: debt, low export prices,
   hazards, corruption, conflict (up to 2). HICs grow from a larger base / reverse cycle, so the
   absolute gap widens (1–2). Max 6.
