## Waves

- Made by wind over the sea; bigger with stronger wind, longer duration, longer **fetch**.
- **Constructive:** low, long wavelength, 6–8 per minute, **swash > backwash** → beach built up.
- **Destructive:** high, steep, short wavelength, 10–14 per minute, **backwash > swash** → beach eroded. Storms.

## Erosion — four processes

- **Hydraulic action:** wave force; air compressed in cracks widens them.
- **Corrasion:** sand and pebbles thrown at the cliff.
- **Attrition:** load knocks together → smaller, rounder (not the cliff).
- **Corrosion:** seawater dissolves limestone and chalk.

## Transport and deposition

- **Traction** (boulders rolled) · **saltation** (pebbles bounced) · **suspension** (fine sand, silt carried) · **solution** (dissolved).
- **Longshore drift:** prevailing wind → swash up at an angle → backwash straight down (gravity) → zigzag → net movement along the coast.
- **Deposition** when energy falls: constructive waves; sheltered bays and estuaries; where the coastline changes direction; large sediment supply.

## Wave refraction

Shallow water off a headland slows waves → crests bend parallel to the coast → energy **concentrated on headlands** (erosion), **spread out in bays** (deposition, beaches).

## Traps

- Corr**a**sion = material thrown; corr**o**sion = rock dissolved.
- Backwash goes straight down the beach, not back along the swash angle.
- Destructive = backwash stronger, not simply "big".
