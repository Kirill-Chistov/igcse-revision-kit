# Cycles within ecosystems — Questions

Specification points 4.10, 4.11B; questions marked **[P2]** are Paper 2 only. Marking guidance is
this repository's own, not an official Pearson mark scheme. No past papers are stored for Biology
yet.

## 1. Recall

1. Name the process that removes carbon dioxide from the air. (1)
2. Name two processes that return carbon dioxide to the air. (2)
3. **[P2]** Name the type of bacteria that converts ammonium ions into nitrate ions. (1)

## 2. Understanding

4. Describe how the carbon in a leaf that falls to the ground can be returned to the air. (3)
5. Explain how carbon can be locked away for millions of years and then returned to the air. (3)
6. **[P2]** Describe the roles of nitrogen-fixing bacteria and denitrifying bacteria. (4)

## 3. Application

7. **[P2]** Clover plants have root nodules. Explain why a farmer might grow clover in a field
   before planting wheat. (3)
8. **[P2]** A field becomes waterlogged. Explain why the wheat grown there may grow poorly. (3)
9. **[P2]** Describe how the nitrogen in a protein in a dead animal can become part of a protein
   in a plant. (4)

## Marking guidance

1. Photosynthesis (1).
2. Any two: respiration, combustion, decomposition / respiration of decomposers (2).
3. Nitrifying bacteria (1).
4. Decomposers (bacteria/fungi) feed on / break down the leaf (1); they respire (1); releasing
   carbon dioxide into the air (1).
5. Dead organisms do not decay, e.g. no oxygen (1); form fossil fuels such as coal, oil, gas (1);
   combustion/burning of fossil fuels releases CO₂ (1).
6. Nitrogen-fixing: convert nitrogen gas from the air (1) into nitrogen compounds / ammonium that
   plants can use (1); in root nodules of legumes or in soil (1). Denitrifying: convert nitrate
   into nitrogen gas (1); in waterlogged / anaerobic soil (1). Max 4.
7. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the nodules convert N₂ into nitrogen compounds (1); when the clover
   dies/is ploughed in, decomposers and nitrifying bacteria release nitrate into the soil (1);
   wheat absorbs nitrate to make proteins, so it grows better / less fertiliser needed (1).
8. Waterlogged soil lacks oxygen (1); denitrifying bacteria convert nitrate to nitrogen gas /
   nitrifying bacteria cannot work (1); less nitrate for wheat to make proteins, so poor growth (1).
9. Decomposers break down the protein (1) into ammonium ions (1); nitrifying bacteria convert
   ammonium to nitrite then nitrate (1); plant roots absorb nitrate by active transport and use it
   to make amino acids / proteins (1).
