## 2. Explain (3 marks each)

4. Explain **one** disadvantage for a manufacturer of relying on quality control.
5. Explain **one** reason why introducing TQM may be difficult for a business.

## 3. Exam-style

6. **(6)** A budget airline wants to improve its reputation. Analyse how improving the quality of its service could give it a competitive advantage.
7. **(9)** A phone manufacturer wants to reduce the number of faulty phones. Option 1: employ more quality control inspectors. Option 2: introduce total quality management (TQM). Justify which **one** of these two options it should choose.

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. Checking/inspecting products (at the end of production or at set stages) to find and remove those that do not meet the required standard.
2. Something that makes a business's product more attractive to customers than its rivals' products, e.g. better quality or a lower price.
3. Any one, in context: food served quickly/on time; polite, knowledgeable staff; clean dining area; the same standard every visit.
4. Identify (1) e.g. faults found only after they are made; develop twice (2): faulty items must be scrapped or reworked → wasted materials and time → higher cost per unit.
5. Identify (1) e.g. every employee must be trained; develop twice (2): training all staff is costly and takes time → benefits come slowly → profit may fall in the short term.
6. Two developed routes in context (3 + 3), e.g. punctual flights and helpful staff → good reviews and loyalty → repeat bookings even when rivals charge the same; fewer complaints and compensation claims → lower costs → can keep fares low.
7. For / against / judgement in context (`EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` § Justify). Option 1 is quick and stops faulty phones reaching customers but faults are still made (waste) and inspectors cost money; Option 2 prevents faults and builds reputation but needs training for every worker and takes time.
