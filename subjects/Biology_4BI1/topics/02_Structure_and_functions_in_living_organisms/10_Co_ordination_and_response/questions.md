# Co-ordination and response — Questions

**Specification points 2.80–2.95B** · questions marked (P2) are Paper 2 only

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Questions

1. Define *homeostasis* and give two examples of conditions it controls.
2. Name the three things a co-ordinated response requires.
3. Describe the responses of (a) a shoot, (b) a root, to gravity.
4. **[4 marks]** A shoot is lit from one side. *Explain* how auxin causes it to bend towards the
   light.
5. Give three differences between nervous and hormonal communication.
6. What does the central nervous system consist of?
7. **[4 marks]** *Describe* how an impulse passes from one neurone to the next across a synapse.
8. **[5 marks]** *Describe* the reflex arc involved when a person pulls their hand away from a hot
   object.
9. Why is a reflex action faster than a response involving conscious thought?
10. State the function of (a) the cornea, (b) the retina, (c) the optic nerve.
11. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how the eye focuses on a book held close to the face.
12. Describe how the iris responds when a person walks from a dark room into bright sunlight,
    and why this is useful.
13. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how the skin helps to cool the body on a hot day.
14. Explain how vasoconstriction reduces heat loss.
15. Give the source and one effect of (a) adrenaline, (b) insulin.
16. (P2) Name the gland that secretes FSH and LH, and give the role of each.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. Maintaining a constant internal environment (1); body water content, body temperature (1).
2. Stimulus, receptor, effector.
3. (a) Grows upwards, away from gravity — negative geotropism; (b) grows downwards, towards
   gravity — positive geotropism.
4. Auxin is made in the shoot tip (1); it moves to the shaded side (1); auxin causes cells to
   elongate (1); shaded side grows more, so the shoot bends towards the light (1).
5. Any three: nervous uses electrical impulses, hormonal uses chemicals; nervous via neurones,
   hormonal via blood; nervous faster; nervous effects short-lived, hormonal longer-lasting;
   nervous localised, hormonal widespread.
6. The brain and spinal cord.
7. Impulse arrives at the end of the neurone (1); neurotransmitter released into the gap (1);
   diffuses across the gap (1); binds to receptors on the next neurone, starting a new impulse (1).
8. Heat detected by receptors in the skin (1); impulse along sensory neurone to spinal cord (1);
   relay neurone in spinal cord (1); synapses / impulse to motor neurone (1); biceps muscle
   (effector) contracts, pulling hand away (1).
9. The impulse travels a short route through the spinal cord / few synapses and does not involve
   the conscious brain.
10. (a) refracts / bends light into the eye; (b) contains light receptors (rods and cones) that
    produce impulses; (c) carries impulses from the retina to the brain.
11. Ciliary muscles contract (1); suspensory ligaments slacken (1); lens becomes fatter / more
    convex and refracts light more onto the retina (1).
12. Circular muscles of the iris contract and radial muscles relax (1); pupil becomes smaller (1);
    less light enters, protecting the retina from damage (1).
13. Sweat glands release sweat (1); evaporation of sweat takes heat energy from the skin (1);
    vasodilation — arterioles supplying the skin widen (1); more blood flows near the surface, so
    more heat is lost by radiation (1).
14. Arterioles supplying the skin narrow (1), so less blood flows near the surface and less heat is
    lost to the surroundings (1).
15. (a) Adrenal glands; increases heart rate / breathing rate / blood glucose. (b) Pancreas; lowers
    blood glucose by converting glucose to glycogen in the liver.
16. Pituitary gland (1); FSH stimulates an egg to mature / oestrogen production (1); LH triggers
    ovulation (1).
