## Responses (4.4.1)

- **Primary** (hours–weeks): search and rescue, medical care, shelter, food, water, evacuation,
  reopen access.
- **Secondary** (months–years): rebuild to higher standards, restore services, restart economy,
  reduce future risk.
- LICs depend more on international aid, slowed by damaged ports and roads.

## Strategies and techniques — evaluation (4.4.2)

| | Key idea | + | − |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monitoring | seismometers, tiltmeters/GPS, gas, satellites | volcano warning signs | costly; no EQ timing |
| Prediction | volcanoes from data; EQ only probability | evacuation saves lives | false alarms; EQ unpredictable |
| Protection | aseismic buildings, codes, lava/lahar barriers | safe buildings save most lives | expensive; needs enforcement |
| Planning | hazard maps, zoning, evacuation, drills, education | cheap, suits LICs | needs government; ignored |
| Technology | early warning, GPS, satellites | fast automatic alerts | seconds only; costly |

**Judgement:** volcanoes → monitor, predict, evacuate; earthquakes → protect and plan. Money and
government decide what is possible.

## Haiti earthquake, 12 Jan 2010 (4.4.3)

- Near Port-au-Prince, 16:53; M 7.0; focus 13 km; conservative margin, North American / Caribbean.
- Primary: 230 000 dead, 300 000 injured, 250 000 homes + 30 000 commercial buildings destroyed,
  1 000 000 homeless. Secondary: 2 million without food and water, power cuts, crime, shelters.
- Responses: port damaged → slow aid; USA 10 000 troops; UK £20 million; water. Long-term:
  overseas aid, homes to higher standard, port rebuilt.
- Strategies *(authorised research)*: 10-station seismic network since 2011 (1 working in 2018);
  hazard map; first code CNBH 2012 (not law by 2020), CNBH 2025; 7 000 masons trained; up to 90%
  informal buildings. Limit = money and enforcement.

## Eyjafjallajökull, March–May 2010 (4.4.4)

- 1 700 m; constructive margin, North American / Eurasian; magma rose into the chamber.
- Primary: ash cloud, day turned to night. Secondary: glacier-melt flooding; no-fly zones —
  billions of euros, airlines £130 million/day, shares −3%; events cancelled; food imports stopped.
- Kenya: $1.3 million/day lost, 5 000 laid off (typically exports 500 tonnes of flowers).
- Positives: 2.8 million t CO₂ avoided; 50 000 extra passengers for other transport; fertile ash.
- Responses: flights cancelled, roads closed, Red Cross evacuated 700+ three times; EU integrated
  air traffic management.
