Do the real questions first; these add calculation practice and gaps.

## 1. Recall and calculation

1. **(1)** Define the term *batch production*.
2. **(2)** A car-parts factory makes 36 000 parts a month with 150 workers. Calculate labour productivity.
3. **(2)** After new machinery is installed, the same 150 workers make 42 000 parts a month. Calculate the percentage increase in labour productivity.
4. **(1)** State **one** disadvantage of just-in-time (JIT) for a business.

## 2. Explain (3 marks each)

5. Explain **one** disadvantage for a business of using job production.
6. Explain **one** reason why a business must balance cost against flexibility when introducing robotics.

## 3. Exam-style

7. **(6)** A bakery supplying supermarkets is considering Kaizen. Analyse the benefits to the bakery of using Kaizen.
8. **(12)** A clothing manufacturer is replacing many of its machinists with robotic sewing machines. Evaluate the impact of this decision on the business.

## Marking guidance

*Written for this revision pack — not an official Pearson mark scheme.*

1. Making a group of identical products together, then changing the equipment to make a batch of a different product.
2. 36 000 ÷ 150 (1) = **240 parts per worker per month** (1).
3. New productivity 42 000 ÷ 150 = 280 (1); (280 − 240) ÷ 240 × 100 = **16.67%** (1).
4. Any one: relies on suppliers delivering on time; no buffer stock for a sudden rise in demand; loses bulk-buying discounts.
5. Identify (1) e.g. high cost per unit; develop twice (2): skilled labour and no bulk buying → high costs → must charge a high price, so fewer customers can afford it.
6. Identify (1) robots are expensive and inflexible; develop (2): high investment is only worth it if output is large and the product rarely changes → otherwise reprogramming costs and lost flexibility outweigh lower unit costs.
7. Two developed benefits in context (3 + 3), e.g. small daily improvements to baking lines → less waste of ingredients → lower cost per loaf → better margins on supermarket contracts; staff suggestions used → motivation → higher productivity.
8. Define a term (e.g. robotics) in context; for: productivity and consistent quality → lower unit cost, competitive prices; against: high cost, less flexibility for fashion changes, redundancies → morale and image; each with a limitation; judgement depending on volume, product variety and finance (`EXAM_TECHNIQUE.md` § Evaluate).
