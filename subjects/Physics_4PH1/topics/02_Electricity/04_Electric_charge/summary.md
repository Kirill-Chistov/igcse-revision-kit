# Electric charge — Summary

Specification points 2.22P–2.28P · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1 · **Paper 2 only**.

- **Conductors** (metals, graphite): free electrons — charge flows away. **Insulators** (plastics such as polythene, PVC, acetate; rubber, glass, dry wood, dry air): electrons can't move — charge stays put.
- **Charging by friction:** rubbing moves **electrons** from one insulator to the other. Gain electrons → **negative**; lose electrons → **positive**; equal and opposite charges. Protons never move.
- Polythene + cloth → polythene −, cloth +. Acetate + cloth → acetate +, cloth −.
- **Like charges repel; unlike attract.** A charged object also attracts an uncharged one, so only **repulsion** proves like charge.
- **Practical:** hang a rubbed rod in a stirrup on nylon thread; bring other rubbed rods near; repel/attract shows the charge. Keep everything dry.
- **Uncharged objects attracted:** charged rod pushes electrons away from (−) or pulls them towards (+) the near side → near side oppositely charged → attraction (balloon on wall, paper bits).
- **Earthing:** electrons flow to earth (from a − object) or from earth (to a + object) until neutral. **Sparks/shocks:** electrons jump an air gap to an earthed conductor.
- **Refuelling danger:** fast fuel flow → friction transfers electrons → charge builds, large voltage → spark → ignites fuel vapour. Prevent by earthing/bonding aircraft or tanker and fuel truck.
- **Photocopier:** drum charged + → light from white parts discharges the drum → − toner sticks to + (dark) areas → + paper pulls toner off → heat fixes it.
- **Inkjet:** drops charged → pass between deflecting plates → attracted to opposite plate → computer varies each drop's charge to control deflection.
