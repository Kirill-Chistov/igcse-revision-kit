# Transport — Questions

**Specification points 2.51–2.69** · questions marked (P2) are Paper 2 only

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Transport systems and plants

1. Explain why *Amoeba* does not need a transport system but a human does.
2. Compare the substances transported by xylem and phloem.
3. (P2) Explain how water enters a root hair cell from the soil.
4. (P2) Define *transpiration*.
5. (P2) Explain why transpiration is faster on a windy day.
6. (P2) Explain why the rate of transpiration falls when humidity increases.
7. (P2) Why is the shoot cut under water when setting up a potometer?
8. (P2) A bubble moves 45 mm in 5 minutes. Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm per minute.

## Blood and immunity

9. Name the four components of blood.
10. State three substances transported by plasma, and where each is carried to.
11. **[3 marks]** *Explain* how a red blood cell is adapted to carry oxygen.
12. Describe how phagocytes and lymphocytes protect the body against pathogens.
13. (P2) **[4 marks]** *Explain* how vaccination prevents a person becoming ill when they are later
    infected with the pathogen.
14. (P2) Describe the role of platelets in blood clotting and give two benefits of clotting.

## Heart and circulation

15. Name the blood vessel that carries blood (a) from the lungs to the heart, (b) from the gut to
    the liver, (c) to the kidneys.
16. Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than that of the right ventricle.
17. What is the function of the valves in the heart?
18. **[3 marks]** *Explain* why heart rate increases during exercise.
19. Explain how adrenaline prepares the body for "fight or flight".
20. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how the structures of an artery and a capillary are related to their
    functions.
21. Give three factors that increase the risk of coronary heart disease and explain one.
22. What is meant by a *double circulation*?

## Answers and marking guidance

1. *Amoeba* has a large SA:V / short diffusion distances (1), so diffusion meets its needs (1);
   humans have a small SA:V and cells far from the surface, so diffusion would be too slow (1).
2. Xylem: water and mineral ions, from roots upwards (1). Phloem: sucrose and amino acids, from
   leaves to other parts, up and down (1).
3. Cell sap is more concentrated than soil water (1); water enters by osmosis (1) through the
   partially permeable membrane; the long hair gives a large surface area (1).
4. The evaporation of water from the surface of a plant.
5. Wind removes water vapour from around the leaf (1), keeping the concentration gradient steep so
   water vapour diffuses out faster (1).
6. More water vapour in the air outside the leaf (1) reduces the concentration gradient, so less
   diffusion out of the stomata (1).
7. To stop air entering the xylem (1), which would block water movement (1).
8. 45 ÷ 5 = 9 mm per minute.
9. Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, plasma.
10. Any three with destination: CO₂ to lungs; glucose/amino acids to all cells; urea to kidneys;
    hormones to target organs; heat around the body.
11. Contains haemoglobin, which combines with oxygen (1); no nucleus — more room for haemoglobin
    (1); biconcave disc — large surface area for diffusion of oxygen (1).
12. Phagocytes ingest pathogens and digest them (1); lymphocytes release antibodies (1) specific to
    the pathogen's antigens, which bind to it (1).
13. Vaccine contains dead/weakened pathogen or its antigens (1); lymphocytes produce antibodies and
    memory cells (1); on infection memory cells cause antibodies to be produced sooner, faster and
    in greater quantity (1); pathogen destroyed before symptoms develop (1).
14. Platelets release chemicals that convert fibrinogen to fibrin (1); fibrin mesh traps red blood
    cells, forming a clot (1). Benefits: prevents blood loss (1); prevents entry of
    micro-organisms (1).
15. (a) pulmonary vein; (b) hepatic portal vein; (c) renal artery.
16. The left ventricle pumps blood all round the body (1), which needs higher pressure (1); the right
    ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs (1).
17. Prevent backflow of blood.
18. Muscles respire faster (1), needing more oxygen and glucose / producing more CO₂ (1); faster
    heart rate delivers more blood to the muscles per minute (1).
19. Adrenal glands release adrenaline (1); heart rate increases (1), so more oxygen and glucose reach
    the muscles for respiration (1).
20. Artery: thick muscular and elastic wall (1) withstands high pressure from the heart (1).
    Capillary: wall one cell thick (1), giving a short diffusion distance for exchange with cells
    (1).
21. Any three from: saturated fat/cholesterol, smoking, high blood pressure, stress, lack of exercise,
    obesity, genes, age, being male. Explanation, e.g. high saturated-fat diet → fatty deposits in
    coronary arteries → narrowed arteries → less oxygen to heart muscle.
22. Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit of the body (heart → lungs → heart
    → body → heart).
