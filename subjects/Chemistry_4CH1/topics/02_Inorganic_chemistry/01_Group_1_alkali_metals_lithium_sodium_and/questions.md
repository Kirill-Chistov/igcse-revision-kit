# Group 1 (alkali metals) — Questions

Specification points 2.1–2.4C · Topic 2 Inorganic chemistry. **Q7 is Paper 2 only.** Marks in
brackets.

> All questions and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository; they are not
> official Pearson questions or mark schemes.

## 1. Recall and understanding

1. Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of potassium with water. (2)
2. Give **three** observations when a small piece of sodium is added to water. (3)
3. Explain how the reactions of lithium, sodium and potassium with water show that they belong to
   the same family. (2)
4. A freshly cut piece of lithium is shiny. Describe and explain what happens to the surface in
   air. (2)

## 2. Application

5. Caesium is below potassium in Group 1. Predict **two** observations when caesium is added to
   water, and the formula of the hydroxide formed. (3)
6. The melting points of lithium, sodium and potassium are 181 °C, 98 °C and 63 °C. Predict the
   melting point of rubidium. (1)

## 3. Paper 2

7. Explain why potassium is more reactive than sodium. (3)

## Answers and marking guidance

1. 2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g) — balanced (1); state symbols (1).
2. Any three: floats (1); fizzes / bubbles (1); melts into a ball (1); moves around the surface (1);
   gets smaller / disappears (1).
3. All react to form a metal hydroxide / alkaline solution (1) and hydrogen (1) (same type of
   reaction and products).
4. Goes dull / tarnishes (1); reacts with oxygen to form lithium oxide (1).
5. Reacts explosively / very violently (1); any other valid observation, e.g. fizzes very rapidly,
   melts (1); CsOH (1).
6. Any value 30–50 °C (1).
7. Potassium's outer electron is further from the nucleus / more shells / more shielding (1);
   weaker attraction to the nucleus (1); outer electron lost more easily (1).
