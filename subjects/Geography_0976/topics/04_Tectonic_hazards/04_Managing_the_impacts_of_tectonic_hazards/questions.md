## Questions

1. **[4]** *Describe* the primary responses to an earthquake.
2. **[3]** *Explain* why responses to earthquakes are often slower in LICs than in HICs.
3. **[4]** *Describe* how scientists monitor volcanoes to predict eruptions.
4. **[4]** *Describe* features of buildings designed to withstand earthquakes.
5. **[7]** *Evaluate* the strategies and techniques used to manage the impacts of earthquakes.

7. **[5]** For a named example of an earthquake, *evaluate* the strategies and techniques used

8. **[7]** For a named volcano, *describe* the causes and impacts of an eruption and the responses

## Marking guidance

1. Search and rescue (1); treating the injured / field hospitals (1); emergency shelter (1); food
   and clean water (1); clearing roads / reopening ports for aid (1). Max 4.
2. Less money / equipment / trained emergency services (1); rely on international aid, which takes
   time to arrive (1); damaged roads, ports and airports slow delivery (1); weaker planning (1).
   Max 3.
3. Seismometers — swarms of small earthquakes (1); tiltmeters / GPS — ground swelling (1); gas
   sensors — more sulphur dioxide (1); satellites — heat / thermal images (1).
4. Base isolators / rubber pads (1); cross-bracing (1); counterweight on roof (1); flexible steel or
   reinforced frame (1); deep foundations (1); automatic gas shut-off (1). Max 4.
5. Level-marked. Top level: covers monitoring, prediction, protection, planning and technology;
   strengths **and** weaknesses; points out earthquakes cannot be predicted so protection and
   planning matter most; cost / LIC–HIC contrast; clear final judgement.
6. Haiti, 12 January 2010: conservative margin, North American and Caribbean plates, friction and
   sudden slip (1–2); magnitude 7.0, focus 13 km, near Port-au-Prince (1); primary: 230 000 dead,
   300 000 injured, 250 000 homes destroyed, 1 000 000 homeless (1–2); secondary: 2 million without
   food and water, power cuts, crime (1–2). Place-specific detail needed for top marks.
7. Haiti: seismic network set up (10 stations) but only one working in 2018 (1); hazard map made
   but not updated (1); first building code CNBH 2012 but not law by 2020 / up to 90% informal
   buildings (1); masons trained (1); judgement — money and enforcement are the main limit (1).
8. Eyjafjallajökull, March–May 2010: constructive margin, North American and Eurasian plates
   moving apart, magma rising (1–2); ash cloud, glacier-melt flooding (1); no-fly zones — airlines
   £130 million a day, billions of euros (1); Kenya lost $1.3 million a day, 5 000 laid off (1);
   responses: flights cancelled, roads closed, Red Cross evacuations, EU integrated air traffic
   management (1–2).
