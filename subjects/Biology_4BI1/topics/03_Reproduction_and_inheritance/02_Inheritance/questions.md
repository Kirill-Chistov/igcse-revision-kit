# Inheritance — Questions

**Specification points 3.14–3.39** · Papers 1 and 2 (questions marked P2 are Paper 2 only)

Marking guidance below is written for this repository; it is not an official mark scheme.

## Questions

1. Define (a) genome, (b) gene, (c) allele.
2. **P2** A DNA strand has the base sequence ATTGCA. Write the sequence of the complementary DNA
   strand and of the mRNA made from the original strand.
3. **P2** Give two differences between DNA and RNA.
4. **P2 · [5 marks]** *Describe* how a protein is made from the information in a gene.
5. **P2** An mRNA codon is CGU. State the anticodon on the tRNA that pairs with it.
6. Explain the difference between genotype and phenotype, and between homozygous and heterozygous.
7. **P2** In cattle, a red bull (C^R C^R) is crossed with a white cow (C^W C^W). State the
   phenotype of the calves and explain why.
8. **[4 marks]** Brown fur (B) is dominant to white fur (b) in mice. Two heterozygous mice are
   crossed. Use a genetic diagram to predict the ratio of phenotypes in the offspring.
9. For the cross in Q8, give the probability that a single offspring has white fur.
10. A tall pea plant could be TT or Tt. Describe a cross to find out which, and state the results
    you would expect for each.
11. In a family pedigree, two unaffected parents have a child with a genetic condition. Explain
    how this shows the condition is caused by a recessive allele.
12. **[3 marks]** Use a genetic diagram to show why there is an equal chance of a baby being a boy
    or a girl.
13. A couple have three daughters. State the probability that their next child is a boy, and
    explain.
14. **[4 marks]** *Compare* mitosis and meiosis.
15. State two situations in which cells divide by mitosis.
16. State the number of chromosomes in (a) a human skin cell, (b) a human sperm cell, (c) a
    zygote.
17. Explain how sexual reproduction produces variation among offspring.
18. Identical twins have the same genes but can differ in mass. Explain why.
19. Suggest why human height shows continuous variation.
20. Define mutation.
21. **P2 · [3 marks]** *Explain* how a change in one DNA base can change the phenotype.
22. **P2** Explain why most mutations have no effect on the phenotype.
23. **P2** Name two factors that increase the rate of mutation.
24. **[5 marks]** *Explain* how Darwin's theory accounts for the evolution of long necks in
    giraffes.
25. **[4 marks]** *Explain* how a population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic.
26. Explain why antibiotic resistance makes infections difficult to control, and suggest one way
    to slow its spread.

## Answers and marking guidance

1. (a) The entire DNA of an organism. (b) A section of DNA that codes for a specific protein.
   (c) An alternative form of a gene.
2. Complementary DNA: TAACGT (1). mRNA: UAACGU (1).
3. Any two: DNA double-stranded, RNA single-stranded; DNA has thymine, RNA has uracil; DNA stays
   in the nucleus, mRNA leaves it.
4. DNA unzips at the gene (1); complementary mRNA made from one strand / transcription (1); mRNA
   leaves the nucleus and attaches to a ribosome (1); tRNA anticodons pair with mRNA codons (1);
   each tRNA carries a specific amino acid (1); amino acids joined in codon order (1). Max 5.
5. GCA.
6. Genotype = the alleles present; phenotype = the characteristic shown (1). Homozygous = two
   identical alleles; heterozygous = two different alleles (1).
7. Roan (red and white hairs) (1); alleles are codominant, so both are expressed in the
   heterozygote C^R C^W (1).
8. Parents Bb × Bb (1); gametes B, b and B, b (1); offspring BB, Bb, Bb, bb (1); 3 brown : 1 white (1).
9. ¼ / 0.25 / 25% / 1 in 4.
10. Cross with a short plant, tt (1). TT → all offspring tall (1); Tt → about half tall, half
    short (1).
11. Both parents are unaffected but have an affected child (1), so each parent must carry a
    hidden recessive allele / be heterozygous (1).
12. Parents XX × XY; gametes X, X and X, Y (1); offspring XX, XX, XY, XY (1); 1 female : 1 male /
    50% (1).
13. 0.5 / 50% (1); each fertilisation is independent — half the sperm carry Y (1).
14. Mitosis one division, meiosis two (1); mitosis 2 cells, meiosis 4 (1); mitosis diploid,
    meiosis haploid (1); mitosis identical, meiosis genetically different (1); mitosis for
    growth/repair, meiosis for gametes (1). Max 4.
15. Any two: growth; repair; cloning; asexual reproduction.
16. (a) 46; (b) 23; (c) 46.
17. Meiosis produces genetically different gametes (1); fertilisation is random — any sperm with
    any egg (1); so each offspring has a new combination of alleles (1).
18. Mass is affected by the environment (1), e.g. diet or exercise (1).
19. Controlled by many genes / polygenic (1) and affected by the environment (1).
20. A rare, random change in genetic material that can be inherited.
21. Base sequence changed alters a codon / triplet (1); a different amino acid in the protein (1);
    protein shape changes, e.g. enzyme active site, so it works differently (1).
22. Any one, explained: change in DNA that does not code for protein; new codon codes for the same
    amino acid; the protein still works.
23. Ionising radiation (gamma rays / X-rays / UV) (1); chemical mutagens, e.g. chemicals in
    tobacco (1).
24. Variation in neck length in the ancestral population (1), caused by genes / mutation (1);
    competition for food (1); longer-necked giraffes reach more leaves, so more likely to survive
    (1); they reproduce and pass on the alleles (1); over many generations long necks become
    common (1). Max 5.
25. A random mutation makes some bacteria resistant (1); the antibiotic kills the non-resistant
    bacteria (1); resistant bacteria survive and reproduce (1); pass on the resistance allele, so
    the population becomes resistant (1).
26. The antibiotic no longer kills the bacteria / resistant strains spread / new antibiotics are
    slow to develop (1). Any one: only prescribe antibiotics when needed; not for viral infections;
    finish the course (1).
