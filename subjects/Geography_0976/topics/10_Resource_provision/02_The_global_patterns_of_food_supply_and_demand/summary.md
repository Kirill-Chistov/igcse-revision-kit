# The global patterns of food supply and demand — Summary

Specification points 10.2.1–10.2.5 · Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography 0976

**Calorie intake (10.2.1)** — kcal available per person per day; adults need ~2,000–2,500.

- High (>3,500): North America, Europe, Russia, China, Australia (USA 3,947). Low (<2,300):
  sub-Saharan Africa, Haiti 1,959, Yemen 1,811. HICs 3,608 vs LICs 2,306 (2023).
- Reasons: wealth/ability to import · farming technology · physical conditions · conflict ·
  storage and transport · population growth.

**Changing production and consumption (10.2.2)** — consumption up: population (8 bn), rising
incomes → meat and dairy, urbanisation, globalisation. Production up: technology, more land,
agribusiness; held back by climate change, degradation, conflict, urban sprawl, biofuels.

**Strategies (10.2.3)** — Green Revolution (HYVs + fertiliser + irrigation; debt, water tables) ·
GM crops · irrigation (salinisation) · more land (deforestation) · mechanisation · aquaculture ·
soil-less farming · cut the ~⅓ of food wasted.

**Why food globalised (10.2.4)** — refrigerated transport and air freight · ICT supply chains ·
WTO/trade deals · TNCs and supermarkets · demand for variety and out-of-season food · cheap land,
labour and climate abroad (Kenyan beans and flowers).

**Impacts (10.2.5)** — producers: + jobs, foreign currency · − low wages, profits leave, cash crops
replace food crops, deforestation, water use. Consumers: + cheap food, choice · − local farmers
undercut, import dependence, food miles (CO₂), obesity.
