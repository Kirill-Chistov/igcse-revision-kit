# Density and pressure — Questions

Specification points 5.3–5.7 · Topic 5. Take *g* = 10 N/kg. Marks in brackets.

> **All questions, answers and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository and
> are not official Pearson questions or mark schemes.** No past papers are stored yet in
> `me/papers/Physics_4PH1/`.

## 1. Recall

1. State the equation linking density, mass and volume. (1)
2. State the equation linking pressure, force and area. (1)
3. State the equation for the pressure difference in a liquid, and what each symbol means. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

4. A student says the pressure at a point in a swimming pool only pushes downwards. Explain why
   she is wrong. (2)
5. Explain why a drawing pin has a sharp point and a wide, flat head. (3)

## 3. Application

6. A stone has a mass of 0.36 kg and a volume of 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Calculate its density. (2)
7. A tractor weighs 48 000 N and its tyres touch the ground over a total area of 1.6 m². Calculate
   the pressure on the ground. (2)
8. Calculate the pressure difference between the surface and the bottom of a 2.5 m deep tank of
   water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). (2)
9. A block of weight 36 N has a base measuring 12 cm × 15 cm. Calculate the pressure it exerts, in
   Pa. (3)

## 4. Practical

10. **[5 marks]** *Describe* how you would find the density of a small irregular stone. Include the
    measurements and how you would use them.
11. Give one way to make the volume reading in a measuring cylinder more accurate. (1)

## 5. Higher demand

12. A submarine window is 180 m below the sea surface (ρ = 1030 kg/m³). Atmospheric pressure is
    1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. (a) Calculate the total pressure on the window. (b) The window has an area of
    0.050 m². Calculate the force on it. (5)
13. Two tanks, one narrow and one wide, are filled with water to the same depth. Compare the water
    pressure at the bottom of each and explain. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. density = mass ÷ volume / ρ = *m*/*V* (1).
2. pressure = force ÷ area / *p* = *F*/*A* (1).
3. *p* = *h* × ρ × *g* (1); *h* = vertical height/depth difference, ρ = density of the liquid,
   *g* = gravitational field strength (1).
4. Pressure at a point in a liquid acts equally in all directions (1); particles move randomly and
   hit surfaces from every direction / water pushes sideways and upwards too (1).
5. Point has a small area (1) so the pressure is large and it pierces the board (1); head has a large
   area so the pressure on the thumb is small (1).
6. ρ = 0.36 ÷ 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ (1) = 3000 kg/m³ (1).
7. *p* = 48 000 ÷ 1.6 (1) = 30 000 Pa (1).
8. *p* = 2.5 × 1000 × 10 (1) = 25 000 Pa (1).
9. *A* = 180 cm² = 0.018 m² (1); *p* = 36 ÷ 0.018 (1) = 2000 Pa (1).
10. Mass on a (zeroed) balance (1); fill displacement can to the spout / until it stops dripping (1);
    lower stone in on a thread and collect the displaced water in a measuring cylinder (1); volume of
    water = volume of stone (1); ρ = mass ÷ volume (1). (Alternative for volume: measuring cylinder
    reading with stone − reading without.)
11. Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level / use a narrower cylinder with a finer scale (1).
12. (a) *p* = 180 × 1030 × 10 (1) = 1 854 000 Pa (1); total = 1.854 × 10⁶ + 1.0 × 10⁵
    = 1.95 × 10⁶ Pa (1). (b) *F* = *p* × *A* = 1.95 × 10⁶ × 0.050 (1) = 9.8 × 10⁴ N (1).
13. The same (1); pressure depends only on depth (and density), *p* = *h*ρ*g*, not on the width/shape
    of the tank (1).
