## Questions

1. **[2]** *Explain* why global sea level is rising.
2. **[2]** Use Figure 2 in the notes (sea level). *Describe* how sea level is predicted to change by
   2100, and why the prediction is given as a range.
3. **[3]** *Describe* **three** present-day impacts of climate change on weather patterns.
4. **[4]** *Explain* how climate change is likely to affect food production. Refer to different parts
   of the world.
5. **[3]** *Give* **one** impact of climate change at each of these scales: global, national, local.
6. **[4]** *Describe* the positive impacts of climate change.
7. **[6]** *Explain* why the impacts of climate change are likely to be greater in low-income countries
   than in high-income countries.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Guidance written for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. Warmer ocean water expands (thermal expansion) (1); glaciers and ice sheets on land melt into the sea
   (1). No credit for melting sea ice.
2. Rise of about 0.3–1.0 m by 2100, with the rise speeding up (1); range because it depends on future
   emissions/scenarios (1).
3. Any three: more frequent and intense heatwaves (a 1-in-50-year heat extreme ~5× as often); heavier
   downpours/flooding; more drought in some regions, e.g. the Mediterranean; larger share of intense
   tropical storms.
4. Lower yields of maize/wheat/rice in hot tropical regions due to heat and drought (1–2); higher food
   prices and food insecurity, especially in LICs (1); longer growing seasons/new farmland at high
   latitudes, e.g. Canada, Russia (1); fish stocks move towards the poles (1).
5. One mark each, correctly matched: global — sea level/temperature rise, food prices; national —
   e.g. land lost in Bangladesh or the Maldives; local — a coastal town losing homes, a farm's harvest.
6. Any four: fewer cold-related deaths; longer growing seasons/new crops in cool regions; shorter Arctic
   shipping routes; access to Arctic resources; longer tourist seasons in cool countries; some species
   spread; faster plant growth with more CO₂.
7. Levels-style: more people depend on farming, which is hit by heat and drought; less money for sea
   walls, irrigation, early warnings (→ 5.3); many live on low-lying deltas/coasts (e.g. Bangladesh);
   food insecurity as prices rise.
   Top answers link each reason to a named impact and note that LICs emitted least.
