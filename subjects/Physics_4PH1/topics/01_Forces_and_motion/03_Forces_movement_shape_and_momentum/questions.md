# Forces, movement, shape and momentum — Questions

Specification points 1.11–1.33P · Topic 1 Forces and motion. Marks in brackets. Questions marked
**[P2]** are Paper 2 only.

> **All questions, answers and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository and
> are not official Pearson questions or mark schemes.**

## 1. Recall

1. State three effects a force can have on an object. (3)
2. Name two forces that act without contact. (2)
3. Explain how a vector quantity differs from a scalar quantity, giving one example of each. (3)
4. State what is meant by stopping distance. (1)
5. State Hooke's law. (2)
6. Describe what is meant by elastic behaviour. (1)
7. **[P2]** State Newton's third law. (2)
8. **[P2]** State the principle of moments. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

9. A box is pushed with 45 N to the right; friction is 15 N. Calculate the resultant force and
   state its direction. (2)
10. Explain why a person's mass is the same on the Moon but their weight is less. (2)
11. Describe how (a) wet roads and (b) a tired driver affect the stopping distance of a car. (4)
12. **[6 marks]** *Explain* why a skydiver reaches a terminal velocity, and why opening the parachute
    leads to a lower terminal velocity.

## 3. Application

13. A 1500 kg car accelerates at 2.4 m/s². Calculate the resultant force. (2)
14. Calculate the weight of a 45 kg student on Earth (*g* = 10 N/kg). (2)
15. A spring is 8.0 cm long unloaded and 11.5 cm long with a 3.0 N load. Calculate the extension
    and predict the length with a 6.0 N load, assuming Hooke's law holds. (3)
16. **[4 marks]** *Describe* how to investigate how the extension of a spring varies with the force
    applied.

## 4. Higher demand (Paper 2)

17. **[P2]** A 0.050 kg bullet leaves a 4.0 kg rifle at 400 m/s. Calculate the recoil velocity of
    the rifle. (3)
18. **[P2]** A 900 kg car at 20 m/s hits a stationary 600 kg car; they move off together. Calculate
    their common velocity. (3)
19. **[P2]** A 0.40 kg ball hits a wall at 15 m/s and rebounds at 10 m/s. Contact lasts 0.050 s.
    Calculate the average force on the ball. (3)
20. **[P2]** **[4 marks]** *Explain* how an airbag reduces injury in a crash.
21. **[P2]** A uniform plank 3.0 m long with weight 200 N rests on supports at its ends. A 600 N
    person stands 1.0 m from the left end. Calculate the upward force from each support. (4)
22. **[P2]** Explain why the weight of a book on a table and the normal contact force on the book
    are not a Newton's third law pair. (2)

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. Change of speed (1); change of direction (1); change of shape (1).
2. Any two (1 each): gravitational; electrostatic; magnetic.
3. Vector has size and direction; scalar has size only (1); vector example e.g. force / velocity
   (1); scalar example e.g. mass / speed (1).
4. Thinking distance + braking distance (1).
5. Extension is directly proportional to the applied force (1), up to the limit of
   proportionality (1).
6. The material recovers its original shape when the deforming forces are removed (1).
7. When body A exerts a force on body B, B exerts a force on A (1) that is equal in size and
   opposite in direction (1).
8. For a body in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about a pivot (1) equals the sum of
   anticlockwise moments about the same pivot (1).
9. 45 − 15 = 30 N (1) to the right (1).
10. Mass is the amount of matter, which does not change (1); weight = *m* × *g* and *g* is smaller
    on the Moon (1).
11. (a) Less friction between tyres and road (1), so the braking distance increases (1). (b)
    Longer reaction time (1), so the thinking distance increases (1).
12. Indicative content — up to 6 marks for a clear, linked answer: at the start weight acts, air
    resistance is zero; resultant force downwards so accelerates; as speed increases air
    resistance increases; resultant force decreases so acceleration decreases; air resistance
    becomes equal to weight; resultant force zero so constant velocity = terminal velocity;
    parachute increases surface area so air resistance increases; air resistance greater than
    weight, resultant upwards, so decelerates; air resistance decreases as speed decreases until
    it equals weight again; new lower terminal velocity.
13. *F* = 1500 × 2.4 (1) = 3600 N (1).
14. *W* = 45 × 10 (1) = 450 N (1).
15. Extension = 11.5 − 8.0 = 3.5 cm (1); 6.0 N doubles the extension to 7.0 cm (1); length =
    15.0 cm (1).
16. Any four (1 each): measure the original length with a metre rule; add masses in equal steps
    (e.g. 100 g = 1 N); measure the new length each time; extension = new length − original
    length; read at eye level / use a pointer; plot a graph of extension against force; safety:
    eye protection.
17. Momentum before = 0 (1); 0.050 × 400 = 20 kg m/s so 4.0 *v* = −20 (1); *v* = −5.0 m/s,
    i.e. 5.0 m/s backwards (1).
18. Momentum before = 900 × 20 = 18 000 kg m/s (1); (900 + 600) *v* = 18 000 (1); *v* = 12 m/s (1).
19. Taking the rebound direction as positive: change in momentum = 0.40 × 10 − 0.40 × (−15) =
    10 kg m/s (1); *F* = 10 ÷ 0.050 (1) = 200 N (1).
20. The person's momentum must change to zero — the same change in momentum (1); the airbag
    increases the time taken for the person to stop (1); so the rate of change of momentum is
    smaller (1); so the force on the person is smaller (1).
21. Moments about the left support: *R*<sub>right</sub> × 3.0 = 600 × 1.0 + 200 × 1.5 (1) = 900
    (1); *R*<sub>right</sub> = 300 N (1); *R*<sub>left</sub> = 800 − 300 = 500 N (1).
22. Both forces act on the same body / the book (1); they are different types of force (1).
