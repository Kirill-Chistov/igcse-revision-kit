# Computer Science 0984 — Subject Rules

**Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Computer Science (0984)**, syllabus for examination in **2026, 2027 and
2028**. A 2-page official syllabus update is stored at
`subjects/Computer_Science_0984/official_syllabus_update.pdf` and must be read alongside the main document.

## Papers — the content is split, and this is the key planning fact

| Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 – Computer Systems | 1 h 45 min | 75 | 50% | **Topics 1–6 only** |
| Paper 2 – Algorithms, Programming and Logic | 1 h 45 min | 75 | 50% | **Topics 7–10 only** |

All questions are compulsory. **No calculators are permitted in either paper.** Both are
externally assessed; there is **no coursework and no separate practical exam**.

Topics 1–6: data representation · data transmission · hardware · software · the internet and its
uses · automated and emerging technologies.
Topics 7–10: algorithm design and problem-solving · programming · databases · Boolean logic.

## Paper 2 specifics

- The final question is a **15-mark unseen scenario question**.
- Where a solution involves coding, answers **must be written in pseudocode**; solutions written
  in programming code are **not** awarded marks. The single exception is the 15-mark scenario
  question, which accepts pseudocode **or** Python, Visual Basic or Java — no other language.
  (Syllabus guidance at the start of *Algorithms, programming and logic*, clarified by
  `official_syllabus_update.pdf` v2, May 2024.) Cambridge publishes its pseudocode conventions in
  section 4 of the syllabus; exam material must follow them.
- Assessable programming concepts are limited to those in topic 8 (variables, constants, data
  types, sequence/selection/iteration, input/output, string handling, arithmetic and logical
  operators, procedures/functions/parameters, local and global variables, 1D and 2D arrays, and
  file handling). Do not teach language features outside that list as if they were required.
- Databases (topic 9) require **SQL** on a single table — reading, understanding and completing
  SQL scripts.
- Boolean logic (topic 10) requires standard **logic-gate symbols**, truth tables and logic
  circuits built from problem statements, logic expressions and truth tables.

## Practical programming

> **Confirm before writing code examples:** the classroom programming language is a **centre
> choice** (commonly Python, Java, VB.NET or C#). Practical programming is compulsory *teaching*
> but is not separately examined. Ask the teacher which language the class uses and record it in
> `me/profile.md`; until then, prefer Cambridge pseudocode in study material.

## Assessment objectives

| AO | Qualification | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AO1 Knowledge and understanding | 40% | 60% | 20% |
| AO2 Application to a given context, including analysis and design | 40% | 20% | 60% |
| AO3 Solutions: evaluating systems, reasoned judgements, conclusions | 20% | 20% | 20% |

## Identifiers used here

Cambridge numbers content as topic → sub-topic (`1.1`) and then as a numbered list of
*"Candidates should be able to"* statements. This repository refers to a statement as `1.1.2` —
the sub-topic plus the item number **as printed in the syllabus**. Topics 7, 9 and 10 have no
sub-topics, so their statements are `7.1`, `9.2`, `10.3` and so on.

## Watch out for

- Material for **0478** (the A*–G Computer Science syllabus) or for the **2023–2025** version of
  0984 — topic content and the Paper 2 format have changed.
- UK GCSE Computer Science (OCR J277, AQA 8525) — different specification, and those courses have
  a programming NEA that 0984 does not.
