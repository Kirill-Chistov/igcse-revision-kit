# Geography 0976 — Subject Rules

**Cambridge IGCSE (9–1) Geography (0976)**, syllabus for examination in **2027, 2028 and 2029**.
This is the 9–1 graded syllabus (0976), not the A*–G syllabus (0460).

## Components

| Component | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Paper 1 – Physical Geography | Topics 1–5 |
| Paper 2 – Human Geography | Topics 6–10 |
| Component 3 – Coursework **or** Paper 4 – Geographical Investigations | Fieldwork / investigation skills |

> **Centre choice — check `me/profile.md` before producing fieldwork material.** A centre enters
> either **Component 3 (coursework)** or **Paper 4 (Geographical Investigations)**. These are
> different pieces of work with different requirements. `/setup` asks; if `me/profile.md` does not
> say, ask the student.

## Detailed specific examples are chosen by the centre

The syllabus deliberately does **not** name examples. Some questions require the candidate to
refer to a detailed specific example. Cambridge recommends examples from CE 2000 to the present.

Consequences:

- **Do not invent the student's examples.** Find out which examples the class studied and record
  them in `Geography_0976/`.
- Where no example has been agreed, mark the gap clearly rather than filling it with a plausible
  one; the student must be able to write detail (place, date, figures, outcomes) they actually
  know.

## Recurring analytical frames

Every topic is taught through the same lenses, and questions assume them:

- **Impacts** are positive and/or negative; social, economic and environmental; at local,
  regional, national and global scales.
- **Management** distinguishes *strategies* (action plans, agreements) from *techniques* (the
  methods used).
- **Sustainability** applies across all topics, especially management.

## Assessment objectives

AO1 Knowledge and understanding 32% · **AO2 Skills and analysis 48%** · AO3 Evaluation and
decision-making 20%.

Skills carry the largest share. Resource-based questions (maps, photographs, satellite images,
graphs, statistics, text extracts) appear in all written papers, and candidates may not know the
world region shown — the skill, not the place, is being tested.

## Units and conventions

Metres and kilometres for height and distance; degrees Celsius for temperature; millimetres for
rainfall. Questions may reference latitude and longitude. Candidates should use the printed
sub-marks as a guide to the length of an answer.

## Watch out for

- Material for the **2025–2026** version of the syllabus or for 0460 — topic content differs.
- Cambridge command words differ from Edexcel's; use the syllabus's own command-word list.
