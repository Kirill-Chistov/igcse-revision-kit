## 1. Recall

1. What is meant by a renewable energy resource? Give three examples.
2. Name two non-renewable energy resources.
3. Which method of generating electricity does not use a turbine or generator?

## 2. Understanding

1. Describe the energy transfers in a coal-fired power station, from the fuel to the grid.
2. Describe the energy transfers in a hydroelectric power station.
3. Describe how a solar heating system can be used to generate electricity.
4. Describe the energy transfers in a nuclear power station.

## 3. Application

1. A remote island has strong winds, a volcanic area and no fuel supplies. Suggest two methods of
   generating electricity for it and give one disadvantage of each.
2. Explain why a country with many wind farms still needs some power stations that can be switched
   on at any time.

## 4. Exam-style prompts

1. **[4 marks]** *Describe* two advantages and two disadvantages of generating electricity with
   nuclear power.
2. **[4 marks]** *Describe* the advantages and disadvantages of using tidal power rather than gas.
3. **[3 marks]** *Describe* how electricity is generated in a geothermal power station.

## Answers and marking guidance

*Not an official mark scheme — written to the specification wording.*

**Recall.** 1. One replaced naturally as fast as it is used, so it will not run out; e.g. wind,
hydroelectric, tidal, waves, geothermal, solar. 2. Coal, oil, natural gas, uranium. 3. Solar cells.

**Understanding.** 1. Chemical store of the coal → burned, heats water (by heating) → steam in a
thermal store → turns the turbine (kinetic store) → generator → electrically to the grid; energy
wasted heating the surroundings. 2. Water held high behind a dam (gravitational store) flows down
(kinetic store) through turbines → generator → electrically. 3. Curved mirrors focus sunlight onto
a boiler; the water heats and boils to steam; the steam turns a turbine, which turns a generator.
4. Nuclear store of uranium → fission in the reactor releases energy → heats water to steam →
turbine (kinetic) → generator → electrically.

**Application.** 1. Wind — unreliable when the wind is too weak or too strong; geothermal — deep
drilling is expensive (only possible where hot rocks are near the surface). 2. Wind turbines give no
output when the wind is too weak (or too strong), so reliable stations are needed to meet demand at
those times.

**Exam-style.** 1. Advantages (any two): no carbon dioxide while running; reliable; very large
output; small mass of fuel. Disadvantages (any two): radioactive waste stays dangerous for a long
time; risk of accidents releasing radioactive material; expensive to build/decommission;
non-renewable. 2. Advantages of tidal (up to 2): renewable; no carbon dioxide while running;
predictable. Disadvantages of tidal (up to 2): few suitable sites; barrage changes habitats/blocks
shipping; high building cost; output depends on the tide, so not available on demand (gas is).
3. Water is pumped down to hot rocks underground (1); it returns as steam (1); the steam turns a
turbine that turns a generator (1).
