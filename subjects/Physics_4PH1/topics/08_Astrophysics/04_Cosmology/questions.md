# Cosmology — Questions

Specification points 8.13P–8.18P · Topic 8 Astrophysics · **all Paper 2 only**. Marks in brackets.

> **All questions, answers and marking guidance in this file are written for this repository and
> are not official Pearson questions or mark schemes.**

## 1. Recall

1. State what happens to the observed wavelength and frequency of light from a galaxy moving away
   from the Earth. (2)
2. Name the two pieces of evidence for the Big Bang theory. (2)
3. State what CMB stands for and the region of the electromagnetic spectrum it is in. (2)

## 2. Understanding and explanation

4. Describe the red-shift in light received from galaxies at different distances from the Earth. (2)
5. Explain why the red-shift of galaxies is evidence that the universe is expanding. (3)
6. Describe the past evolution of the universe according to the Big Bang theory. (3)
7. Explain how the CMB radiation supports the Big Bang theory. (3)

## 3. Application

8. A line in the hydrogen spectrum has a wavelength of 486 nm in the laboratory. In light from a
   galaxy it is observed at 490.9 nm. Calculate the velocity of the galaxy. (*c* = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s) (3)
9. A galaxy moves away from the Earth at 1.5 × 10⁷ m/s. A line has a laboratory wavelength of
   656 nm. Calculate the wavelength observed from the galaxy. (3)

## 4. Higher demand

10. The same 656.0 nm line from a nearby galaxy is observed at 655.4 nm. Calculate the galaxy's
    speed and state what the result tells you about its motion. (3)
11. **[6 marks]** *Describe* the evidence for the Big Bang theory and explain how each piece of
    evidence supports it.

## Answers and marking guidance

> Marking guidance written for this repository, not an official Pearson mark scheme.

1. Wavelength increases / longer (1); frequency decreases / lower (1).
2. Red-shift (of galaxies) (1); cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation (1).
3. Cosmic microwave background (1); microwaves (1).
4. Light from (almost all) galaxies is red-shifted (1); the further away the galaxy, the greater the
   red-shift (1).
5. Red-shift shows galaxies are moving away from us (1); in every direction, and more distant
   galaxies are moving away faster (1); which is what happens if space / the universe is expanding (1).
6. The universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in a very small, hot, dense region (1); it has
   expanded (1) and cooled ever since, with atoms, stars and galaxies forming as it cooled (1).
7. The CMB is radiation left over from the hot early universe / the Big Bang (1); the expansion of
   the universe has stretched / red-shifted it to microwave wavelengths (1); it comes from every
   direction almost uniformly, as the Big Bang predicts (1).
8. Δλ = 490.9 − 486 = 4.9 nm (1); v = c × Δλ/λ₀ = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 4.9 ÷ 486 (1) = 3.0 × 10⁶ m/s (1).
9. Δλ = λ₀ × v/c = 656 × 1.5 × 10⁷ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ (1) = 32.8 nm (1); λ = 656 + 32.8 = 689 nm (1).
10. Δλ = −0.6 nm (1); v = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 0.6 ÷ 656 = 2.7 × 10⁵ m/s (1); the wavelength is shorter
    (blue-shift), so the galaxy is moving towards the Earth (1).
11. Indicative content (level-style marking, up to 6):
    - light from distant galaxies is red-shifted — longer wavelength than in the laboratory;
    - so galaxies are moving away from us, in every direction;
    - greater red-shift for more distant galaxies — they move away faster;
    - so the universe / space is expanding; running this backwards, it started from one small,
      hot, dense region;
    - CMB is microwave radiation detected from every direction, almost uniform;
    - it is leftover radiation from the hot early universe, stretched to microwaves by the
      expansion; the Big Bang theory predicts it.
    Top answers cover both pieces of evidence and link each clearly to the theory.
