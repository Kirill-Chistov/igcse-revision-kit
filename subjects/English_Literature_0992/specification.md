## Qualification at a glance

All candidates take **Paper 1**, and then **either** Paper 2, **or** Paper 3 + Paper 4,
**or** Paper 3 + Component 5. Grades 9–1.

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Paper 1 – Poetry and Prose** (all candidates) | 1 h 30 min | 50 | 50% | Two questions on two texts: one poetry, one prose |
| Paper 2 – Drama *(route A)* | 1 h 30 min | 50 | 50% | Two questions on two texts |
| Paper 3 – Drama (Open Text) *(routes B and C)* | 45 min | 25 | 25% | One question on one text |
| Paper 4 – Unseen *(route B)* | 1 h 15 min | 25 | 25% | One question requiring critical commentary |
| Component 5 – Coursework *(route C)* | — | 25 | 25% | Portfolio of two assignments, each on a different text |

> **Centre choice — `/setup` asks the student and records it in `me/profile.md`.** Which of the three routes the centre enters, and
> **which set texts the class is actually studying**. Both are centre choices and neither can be
> inferred from the syllabus.

## Assessment objectives

All four AOs carry 25% of the qualification, and 25% of every component.

| AO | Description |
| --- | --- |
| AO1 | Show detailed knowledge of the content of literary texts in the three main forms (drama, poetry and prose), supported by reference to the text |
| AO2 | Understand the meanings of literary texts and their contexts, and explore texts beyond surface meanings to show deeper awareness of ideas and attitudes |
| AO3 | Recognise and appreciate ways in which writers use language, structure and form to create and shape meanings and effects |
| AO4 | Communicate a sensitive and informed personal response to literary texts |

## Set texts for examination in 2027

Candidates answer on **one** poetry text and **one** prose text in Paper 1, and on the drama
texts required by their route.

## Course distinctions that affect what can be assessed

- **Set texts change every year.** This repository holds the **2027** list. Never use a 2026 or
  2028 text list.
- Paper 3 is **open text**: candidates may take in a copy of the set text, but it must carry
  **no notes, highlighting or underlining**.
- Editions: where the syllabus does not specify an edition, any edition may be used **provided it
  is not an abridgement or a simplified version**.
- Study material must be written for the texts the class actually studies. Do not produce notes
  on options the student is not taking.

## Note on identifiers

Cambridge does not number set texts. This repository uses local references `P1A.1`–`P1A.3`
(Paper 1 poetry options), `P1B.1`–`P1B.8` (Paper 1 prose options), `DR.1`–`DR.5` (drama options)
and `AO1`–`AO4`. These are **repository-local**, not Cambridge codes.

## How to read this file

- This file is a **curriculum representation**, not revision notes. It exists so that AI
  agents can plan and trace study material without re-parsing the official PDF.
- Official identifiers (section numbers, specification point numbers, topic names) are
  preserved so every statement can be traced back to `subjects/English_Literature_0992/Specification.pdf`.
- Requirement wording is condensed/paraphrased from the official objective statements.
  **The official PDF is always authoritative** where this file is ambiguous.
- `specification_map.csv` maps each specification point to its planned folder in `English_Literature_0992/`.
- `coverage_checklist.md` tracks which points have study material written.
