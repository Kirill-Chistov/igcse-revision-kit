# The organism in the environment — Questions

Specification points 4.1–4.5; questions marked **[P2]** cover 4.3B/4.4B (Paper 2 only).
Marking guidance is this repository's own, not an official Pearson mark scheme. No past papers are
stored for Biology yet.

## 1. Recall

1. Define *population* and *community*. (2)
2. What is the difference between a habitat and an ecosystem? (2)
3. **[P2]** Define *biodiversity*. (1)
4. Give two abiotic and two biotic factors. (2)

## 2. Understanding

5. Explain why quadrats are placed at random coordinates when estimating population size. (2)
6. Explain how an increase in a predator population can lead to a decrease in its own numbers
   later. (3)
7. **[P2]** Explain why quadrats are placed at regular intervals, not randomly, along a transect. (2)

## 3. Application

8. A student counted buttercups in 10 quadrats of 0.25 m² in a 40 m × 25 m field. Counts: 3, 5, 0,
   4, 6, 2, 3, 5, 4, 8. Estimate the buttercup population. (3)
9. Describe how the student could compare the buttercup population in this field with a second
   field. Include two control variables. (4)
10. **[P2]** Along a transect from a hedge into a field, a shade-tolerant plant decreases as light
    intensity increases. A student concludes that light controls its distribution. Evaluate this
    conclusion. (3)

## Marking guidance

1. Population: all organisms of one species in an area (at the same time) (1). Community: all the
   populations of different species in an area (1).
2. Habitat is the place where an organism lives (1); ecosystem is the community plus the abiotic
   environment, interacting (1).
3. The variety of different species (of organisms) in an area (1).
4. Abiotic: any two of light, temperature, water, oxygen, CO₂, mineral ions, pH (1). Biotic: any two
   of food, predators, competition, disease/pathogens (1).
5. Avoids bias / the student choosing where to sample (1); so the sample is representative of the
   whole area (1).
6. More predators eat more prey (1); prey population falls (1); predators have less food, so more
   die / fewer reproduce, and the predator population falls (1).
7. Conditions change along the transect (1); regular intervals sample every part of the gradient so
   changes in distribution can be linked to position/the abiotic factor (1).
8. Mean = 40 ÷ 10 = 4 per quadrat (1); field area = 1000 m²; number of quadrat areas = 1000 ÷ 0.25
   = 4000 (1); population = 4 × 4000 = 16 000 (1).
9. Same random-sampling method in the second field (1); same quadrat size (1); same number of
   quadrats (1); same time of year / same counting rule (1); calculate and compare the means or
   population estimates (1). Max 4.
10. Supports: clear negative correlation with light (1). Against: correlation does not prove cause
    (1); other factors also change, e.g. soil moisture, temperature, competition from grass (1);
    repeat transects / measure other factors (1). Max 3.
