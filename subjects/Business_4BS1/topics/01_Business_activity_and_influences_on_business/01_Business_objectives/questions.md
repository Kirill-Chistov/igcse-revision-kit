## Practice

1. **[1]** Define the term *objective*.
2. **[2]** State one financial objective and one non-financial objective.
3. **[3]** Explain one reason why a new business is likely to have survival as its main objective.
4. **[3]** Explain one reason why a business's objectives may change when a new competitor enters its market.
5. **[6]** A family bakery with 12 staff has changed its main objective from survival to increasing market share. Analyse two reasons why the bakery may have changed its objective.
6. **[9]** A small clothing manufacturer is choosing its main objective for next year. Option 1: increase profit. Option 2: use only sustainable materials. Justify which one of these two options it should choose.

## Marking guidance (not an official mark scheme)

1. A specific, measurable target set to help a business achieve its aim.
2. Financial: survival / profit / sales / market share / financial security. Non-financial: social objectives / personal satisfaction / challenge / independence and control.
3. New business has few customers and high start-up costs (1) → revenue may not cover costs at first (1) → so its first priority is to keep enough money to keep trading (1).
4. Rival takes some customers (1) → sales and revenue fall (1) → objective may switch from growth to survival or defending market share (1).
5. Two reasons, three linked strands each, in context — e.g. performance: survival target met, loyal customers built up → can aim higher; market conditions: a rival bakery closed → chance to win its customers → market share objective.
6. Three paragraphs: for the chosen option (with a comparison), against it, then a judgement using a reason specific to this business. E.g. sustainable materials attract ethical customers and build reputation, but raise costs and cut profit; judgement depends on its target market and financial position.
