3. **[4]** *Explain* how **two** adaptation techniques reduce the impacts of rising sea levels.
4. **[4]** *Compare* the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement.

6. **[6]** *Evaluate* the success of international agreements in managing climate change.
7. **[7]** *Evaluate* mitigation and adaptation strategies and techniques used to manage the impacts of

## Answers and marking guidance

*Guidance written for this repository — not an official Cambridge mark scheme.*

1. Mitigation reduces the cause — cuts greenhouse gas emissions/increases sinks (1); adaptation adjusts
   to the impacts to reduce harm (1).
2. CO₂ captured at power stations/factories (1); compressed and piped/transported (1); stored deep
   underground, e.g. old gas fields, so it does not enter the atmosphere (1).
3. Two techniques × (named technique 1 + how it works 1), e.g. sea walls/flood barriers block storm
   surges and high tides; mangroves absorb wave energy; raising buildings keeps homes above flood level;
   managed retreat moves people away from the risk.
4. Similarity: both aim to cut emissions / UN agreements (1). Differences (up to 3): Kyoto HICs only vs
   Paris every country; Kyoto binding targets vs Paris self-set voluntary targets; Paris aims for well
   below 2 °C/1.5 °C and reviews targets every 5 years; Paris includes climate finance; Kyoto excluded
   China and India.
5. Warming is global — every country's emissions affect all (1); one country acting alone makes little
   difference (1); stops countries leaving the cost to others / shares cost fairly, finance for LICs (1).
6. Levels-style. Successes: Kyoto first binding targets, carbon trading, participants met target; Paris
   includes almost all countries, 5-yearly reviews, climate finance reached US$100 bn (2022). Failures:
   USA never ratified Kyoto and left Paris twice; China/India had no Kyoto targets; world emissions kept
   rising; Paris targets voluntary; pledges lead to ~2.3–2.8 °C, above the 1.5–2 °C aim. Judgement:
   Paris is more inclusive but not yet enough.
7. Levels-style. Mitigation: tackles the cause, lasting, global benefit; but slow, costly, needs all
   countries. Adaptation: immediate, local, protects people now; but treats symptoms, costs rise, some
   impacts cannot be adapted to, LICs can't afford it. Named techniques with strengths/weaknesses.
   Sustainability: mitigation (renewables, forests) most sustainable; soft adaptation more sustainable
   than hard engineering. Judgement: both needed; mitigation is the only long-term solution.
